## GTM Implications

### Which ICP/segment does this account confirm or challenge?

**Edge case, and a segment the ICP doesn't describe.** ICP A (the rep) and ICP B (the DSO ProPay unlocker) are both written for the installed base. The new-prospect entry point in the outcome pitch assumes an established DSO with a back office to replace or connect to. **Sciteria is neither: a pre-launch company with no back office, no field data and no incumbent rep app.**

The pricing grid does name this buyer. Freedom's **Lite tier ($2,000–$2,500/mo) targets "Startup DSOs"** (`my-gtm-context.md` §5). Wayroo's positioning has no startup row. The one startup-specific argument came from Autumn, live: **a startup doesn't have to fight adoption**, because every rep gets the app as part of the launch. That inverts the installed base's core problem: ~20% of Paparazzi's field, and 28% at Jordan Essentials' launch. *(Nov 2 debrief; Autumn and the room agreed.)*

### What does this tell us about positioning?

1. **The notification center tied to autoresponders is an unpositioned feature that won the demo.** The repo positions notifications as "corporate reaches the field" (push alerts, `06-positioning-bydesign-wayroo.md`). What Terri liked was the **automation**: the field gets the right message without anyone at the home office sending it. For a founder-run startup with no ops team, that replaces staff. **Candidate line for lean or startup home offices: "Your field hears from you automatically, from day one."** One account, secondhand, so probationary.
2. **White-label appetite is segment-dependent, not low.** The only prior evidence was Color Street's CEO: *"function over fashion"* (`06-persona-positioning.md`, H1). Terri wants the opposite: a founder building a brand from zero doesn't want her field's first app to carry someone else's name. **Two accounts, two readings.** An established C&C DSO wants function. A startup founder wants brand. Neither will pay much for it right now: Color Street because it doesn't care, Sciteria because it can't afford to.
3. **"Start with Wayroo, go branded later" is a usable startup talk track, if the mechanics hold.** It turns price into a growth path. But it competes with the day-one adoption benefit she valued if rebranding later means reps must re-download. Confirm before it goes in any pitch doc.
4. **Media Library is a buyer feature with a condition attached.** At Jordan Essentials the buyer rated Media Library high and reps rated it low. Here the buyer wants it only with AI. Either way, Media Library on its own isn't winning buyers.

### What does this tell us about the product?

- **Media Library + AI layer** is the clearest product request (stated as "very important"). It overlaps with the pending **Aice** partnership (Aice owns content creation) and with **Fluid**'s AI positioning. Log it to the product team. Don't promise it.
- **Branding mechanics need a clear answer:** what "gray label" means technically (in-app theming vs. a separate listing), and whether upgrading forces reps to re-download.
- **International restricted categories:** the Adyen AU/NZ nutraceutical restriction is a qualification gate for any health or wellness DSO launching outside the US.

### What does this tell us about the sales motion?

- **Two-seller new logo, in sequence:** Dan closes Freedom, then Autumn demos Wayroo at the close stage. Wayroo is working as a **deal-closer and differentiator** on a Freedom sale, not as its own revenue line. Wayroo earns only from payments, and here there is a tiny field and no selling-tools fit, so its near-term revenue is close to $0.
- **Price ladder tactics:** Autumn deliberately **held back the $100K own-app figure** and framed price as "depends on how custom you want to go." That's sound anchoring, but the ladder she used isn't written down anywhere. Three live numbers and one repo anchor ($25K) will drift across sellers.
- **Startup qualification questions to add:** *"How many people are coming with you, and from which company?"* (field size and reference stack) and *"What did your field use to take payments there?"* (tests the "selling tools aren't a fit" assumption with evidence).
- **Salesforce discipline:** the stage definitions agreed in this meeting (Proposal = 50%, SOW in progress; Agreement = 75%, order form) put Sciteria correctly in Proposal.

## Anti-Patterns / Disqualifiers

- **Not disqualified. Freedom is verbally committed.** The risks are to deal *size*, not to the deal.
- **Field size is hidden.** In the room's experience, a founder who won't share a number usually has very few reps. Freedom rep fees and any Wayroo payments revenue scale with a field that doesn't exist yet.
- **Wayroo's commercial value here is near zero in year one.** Selling tools are judged not a fit, and AU/NZ underwriting is in question. Branding is the only paid Wayroo surface, and she won't pay for it now. **Wayroo's job on this account is to help close Freedom and to launch with 100% adoption.** It isn't a revenue line yet.
- **Restricted-category exposure** (health claims, AU/NZ) could limit the payments product in two of the three launch markets.
