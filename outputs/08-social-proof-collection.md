## The Actual Problem

`my-gtm-context.md` §9 lists this as a key constraint: *"zero published case studies, zero testimonials."* The OPE calls it *"the #1 constraint on GTM credibility."*

But running the inventory shows the diagnosis is slightly wrong, and the correction matters:

> **This is not an evidence problem. It is a permission-and-packaging problem.**

You have a $31M customer result, a 28%-in-48-hours adoption event, three founder-level quotes, 51 active clients, a DSA award, DSN Premier Supplier status, and 4.8 stars on Capterra. **None of it is published.** The work required is asking and formatting — not generating new proof.

That reframe changes the cost estimate substantially. This is days of work, not a quarter.

## ⏰ The Timing Point That Should Drive Everything

The Jordan Essentials launch was **July 17–18. Today is August 8 — three weeks ago.**

Testimonial enthusiasm decays fast. Nancy pulled her own paid start forward *because of how the launch went*; that is peak sentiment and it will not be higher in October. Hope watched her field react to a product she championed. Roughly 700 reps just adopted something they were visibly excited about.

**Every collection ask below should go out inside two weeks.** The single most perishable asset in this plan is goodwill that nobody has asked to convert.

## The Ask Template

Short, specific, references something they actually said, and offers an easy out. Adapted from the methodology's three-option approach.

> **Subject:** Quick ask — that 700-download weekend
>
> Nancy,
>
> Still thinking about the launch. Seven hundred downloads in the first weekend — that's 28% of your field in 48 hours, and it's the best launch adoption we've seen.
>
> Would you be open to us sharing that number publicly, with Jordan Essentials named? Other DSO leaders ask us constantly whether reps will actually adopt a new tool, and your launch is the clearest answer we have.
>
> Three options, whichever is easiest:
>
> 1. We name Jordan Essentials and use the launch numbers
> 2. Same, plus a short quote — here's a draft you can edit or replace: *"[draft]"*
> 3. You write your own version, however you'd say it
>
> A "no" is completely fine and changes nothing on our side.
>
> Sam

**Why it's built this way:** it leads with something specific to them rather than a generic request, gives three escalating options so the lowest-effort yes is still valuable, and removes any relationship risk from declining — which matters more than usual with a customer whose paid contract starts next month.
