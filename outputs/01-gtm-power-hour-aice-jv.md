# GTM Power Hour — Aice Joint Venture

**GTM author:** Sam Atieh
**Date:** June 21, 2026
**Entity:** Aice JV (ByDesign/Wayroo ~70–75% + Aice ~25–30%; Brian Juhl CEO) — **provisional, term sheets June 2026; hedge until close**
**Builds on:** `outputs/01-one-page-endgame-aice-jv.md` + the May 23 "Wayroo × AiCE" partnership deck (M1–M9)

> **Method note.** This is the fast 10-element GTM diagnostic. Each element = current best answer + a confidence flag. Channels, competition, differentiation, and key metric are pre-filled from the deck and re-cast under the single-entity (merger) lens — the deck's partnership economics (rev-share/deal-reg/splits) are treated as superseded internal allocation, not external terms.

---

## 1. Target Customer — Confidence: **High**

Two co-equal customer types (the dual motion), plus a net-new channel:

- **Enterprise (DSO leadership):** VP Sales / Founder / CEO / Field Ops at mid-market DSOs (300–10,000 reps) — jewelry, skincare, wellness, beauty/nail. The existing ByDesign/Wayroo buyer.
- **PLG (individual reps + creators/sellers):** the rep running their own micro-business; the creator/seller who wants to sell on day one. Aice's existing 100K users (50K DSO reps + 50K creators) *are* this segment, already acquired.
- **Net-new DSO startups:** Aice closes 1–2/month, startup-heavy — today routed to a Pillars hand-off; under the JV, kept in-house on headless Freedom-lite.

*Not "everyone in MLM."* The wedge is: DSOs stitching together 4 vendors, and reps/creators juggling 4–6 tools.

---

## 2. Problem — Confidence: **High**

- **DSO:** buys four things from four vendors — back office (commissions/genealogy), rep coaching/community, payments processor, commerce/cart — none integrated. Procurement, billing, support, and data are all fragmented. FTC/tax exposure from untracked rep transactions sits on top.
- **Rep:** runs a business across 4–6 disconnected tools; loses sales to payment friction; spends selling time on admin.
- **Creator/seller:** can't transact without bolting on Shopify + a payments processor + waiting for a DSO sponsor.

**Urgency:** High and structural, not nice-to-have — it's the cost/compliance/retention spine of the business.

---

## 3. Solution — Confidence: **High**

**The first unified operating system for direct selling — commissions, commerce, payments, point-of-sale, and rep enablement under one roof.** Aice absorbs the Wayroo transaction backbone and the Freedom/Revolution back office into one all-in-one product, sold/billed/supported through a single relationship.

---

## 4. Value Proposition — Confidence: **High**

| Audience | The promise |
|---|---|
| DSO | **One contract instead of four.** Collapse 4 vendors into one relationship; gain full rep visibility + FTC/tax audit trail. |
| Rep | **One surface, not five tabs.** Coaching, content, replicated site, commissions, payouts, payments — one place; onboard once, sell more, stay longer. |
| Creator/seller | **Sell on day one — on rails.** Built-in commerce + payouts on the Aice surface; no separate Shopify, no waiting for a sponsor. |

---

## 5. Pricing — Confidence: **Medium** (model clear; numbers gated on payments data)

**The strategic core of the JV.** Payments are monetized on the Aice side (take rate / rev-share on transaction volume). That recurring payments revenue **cross-subsidizes software pricing** — so the JV can lower license prices and **undercut every competitor while being the only all-in-one**. The weapon is being *the most complete and the cheapest at once* — a move only a player who owns payments can make.

- Inputs to reconcile in Phase 5: DSO platform/contract pricing, per-user license, Aice PLG tiers ($9.99–$49.99), and the payments take rate.
- **Pillars referral retired:** the $2,000–2,500/mo that today leaks to a 3-person headless vendor is captured internally via headless Freedom-lite (deck M5).
- Today's anchor: Aice DSO "startup light" at $2,500/mo; ByDesign tiers $2K–$10K+/mo + per-user.

**Open question (gates the whole undercut thesis):** how much can we actually lower software price, given the payments take rate and volume? Needs the Metrics-installed DSO count + average transaction volume (deck flags "ask Brian").

## 7. Competition — Confidence: **High**

**Aice exits the competitor column entirely.** The merger redraws the map:

- **Exigo** — back office + lighter rep app; no all-in-one, no per-rep merchant accounts.
- **Rallyware** — enterprise enablement; no commerce primitives; Tier-1 only.
- **Fluid** — we-commerce front-end; always a second vendor; no payouts/compliance/comp engine.
- **Shopify** — cart + 3% single-merchant-account model; the cost/liability foil.
- **Pillars** — headless back office (~3 employees); the JV displaces it.
- **The multi-tool patchwork / "do nothing"** — the real status-quo competitor.
- **A future PE rollup** — the deck's sharpest framing: anyone trying to *assemble* this stack by acquisition is 12–24 months behind on integration, brand collision, and certification. The JV is "already integrated, already in market."

---

## 8. Differentiation — Confidence: **High**

1. **Only true all-in-one** across all four layers (funnel → commerce → payments → back office). Everyone else is a point solution requiring 2–4 vendors.
2. **Payments-funded price advantage** — the only player who can be the most complete *and* undercut on price, because payments revenue replaces the software margin (see §5).
3. **Per-rep merchant accounts** (ProPay) — structural FTC/tax compliance fix no competitor offers.
4. **12-month integration head start** over any rollup — already shipping, already in market.
5. **Proven at every layer** — $31M Paparazzi, $7B+ payouts, 51 DSO clients, 100K Aice users. Demand risk retired.

## 10. Key Metric — Confidence: **High**

**North star (from deck):** net-new rep sales volume on the Wayroo cart, originating from Aice-distributed DSOs and creators. (Baseline $0; targets calibrate once payments/installed-base data is in hand.)

**The metric that proves the thesis:** the **M7 up-chain conversion rate** — % of PLG-acquired reps/DSOs that convert into or pull in a full-stack DSO deal. This is the flywheel/bridge metric an acquirer underwrites; it has no prior proof and must be instrumented from day one.

## Top 3 Gaps to Close First

1. **Payments economics are unknown — and they gate the entire pricing/undercut strategy.** *Partially resolved:* the BYD installed base runs ~$448M/yr in processing volume, and most is already on monetized rails (Nuvei/ProPay/Authorize.Net) — so the **incremental** payments driver is **net-new rep ecommerce GMV on the Wayroo cart** (projected in the model's Summary/Assumptions tabs), not back-office migration. Still need: the projected rep-GMV attach assumptions and the negotiated take rate, which together set *how far* software price can be cut. Until then, "undercut everyone" is a thesis, not a plan. See `outputs/installed-base-tam-analysis-aice-jv.md`. **(Owner: Aice finance / model Summary tab.)**
2. **The all-in-one must actually ship.** The single-product promise depends on Cart V3, SSO, cart embed, and Shopify→native migration (deck targets Integration GA Q4 2026). The positioning writes checks engineering has to cash — confirm the integration timeline is real before launching the "one stack" message externally.
3. **The M7 bridge has zero proof.** PLG→enterprise up-chain is asserted, not demonstrated. The cheap rep license that supposedly "clears the DSO price objection" is unvalidated, and the conversion path isn't instrumented. This is the riskiest assumption in the whole model — and the most important to the exit story.
