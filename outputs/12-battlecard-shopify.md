# Battlecard — Wayroo + Freedom vs. Shopify

**Audience:** Sales reps (Dan, Autumn, Cassie)
**Format:** 1-page field reference. Print, bookmark, or open before any deal where Shopify is in the picture.
**Last updated:** 2026-09-11 *(rate figures corrected — see the banner below)*
**Source material:** `outputs/06-uvp-usp.md`, `outputs/06-positioning-statement.md`, `outputs/06-positioning-cash-carry-variant.md`, my-gtm-context.md §6
**Related battlecards:** Rallyware (live). Aice (pending partnership confirmation). Exigo (pending rep app feature-scope research).

---

## How they describe themselves

> "Build your ecommerce experience on the world's leading commerce platform."

Shopify is the dominant ecommerce platform for retail brands. Their pitch into DSOs: use Shopify for the corporate brand store, use the app ecosystem (or a partner like Aice) to bolt on rep storefronts, and let Shopify Payments process every transaction. Modern UX, large app marketplace, mainstream brand recognition.

---

## Honest strengths (don't sugarcoat)

- **Best-in-class ecommerce UX.** Storefront builder, checkout flow, mobile shopping experience — top of the category for retail.
- **Brand recognition.** Most DSO executives and reps already know what Shopify is. Zero category education needed.
- **App ecosystem.** Thousands of integrations. Anything Shopify doesn't do natively, an app probably does.
- **Engineering depth.** Strong platform foundation, fast page loads, reliable uptime, mature developer tools.
- **POS hardware (general).** Shopify POS exists for retail. (Caveat: not designed for DSO vendor-event / party-plan flow.)

---

## Strategic weaknesses (where we win)

- **TCO at scale — and the rate is the *smallest* line.** Shopify stacks a **$29–$2,300/mo platform subscription**, a **0.35–0.40% variable fee on *total* GMV** once a Plus merchant passes ~$800K/mo (capped $40K/mo), and a **0.2–2.0% surcharge** if they ever use a non-Shopify gateway — *then* card processing at 2.15–2.9% + 30¢. **A Plus merchant at $10M/yr GMV pays ~$30–40K/yr in platform fees before processing a single card.** That is the 7-figure argument, and every line of it is verified. Wayroo charges **no separate platform subscription**, and **Spendback drops the DSO's fee to 1% on wholesale orders paid from a rep's ProPay balance.**
- **No fixed per-transaction fee card-present — we win small tickets outright.** Wayroo is **2.85% + $0.00** in person; Shopify POS charges **10¢** and Square **15¢** on top of their percentage. On a $5 vendor-table sale: **Wayroo $0.14, Shopify Basic $0.23, Square $0.28.** We stay cheaper up to a **$40** ticket vs Shopify Basic and **$60** vs Square — which is most of a Cash & Carry basket.
- **Single merchant account model.** All rep transactions flow through the DSO's one Shopify merchant account. The DSO holds 100% of the chargeback liability and gets every rep's transaction on its own books.
- **FTC contractor classification risk.** When reps transact through the DSO's merchant account, the FTC can argue the rep is functionally an employee (not a contractor). Wayroo's per-rep ProPay merchant accounts are the structural fix — reps own their own merchant, their own chargebacks, their own 1099.
- **Built for retail, not direct selling.** No party-plan mechanics, no host rewards, no vendor-event POS flow, no genealogy, no commission engine, no cash-and-carry inventory model, no "sell-on-the-fly" for catalog-rotation product lines.
- **No native back-office integration.** Shopify needs to be stitched to your commission engine via custom integration or a third-party connector. Wayroo + Freedom is native — same vendor, same data model.
- **Rep experience splits across multiple tools.** Even with Aice or another rep-app overlay, the rep still ends up in 2+ apps to run their business. Wayroo is one app: storefront, POS, invoicing, inventory, payouts, taxes.
- **No FTC/tax-grade transaction trail at the rep level.** Shopify creates transaction records under the DSO, not under the rep. Wayroo creates the audit-grade per-rep record the FTC asks about during enforcement actions.

---

## When to expect to win

- **Mid-market DSOs (200–10K reps) with growing transaction volume.** The platform-fee stack — subscription + GMV variable fee + gateway surcharge — is the easiest math problem in the deal. *(Argue the stack, not the card rate.)*
- **Cash & Carry DSOs.** Vendor events, party plan, in-person sales, barcode POS, inventory management — Shopify is not built for any of this. Wayroo's C&C positioning lands hard.
- **DSOs facing FTC scrutiny or sensitive to contractor classification risk.** Especially relevant 2026+ as enforcement increases.
- **DSOs already on Freedom or evaluating Freedom.** Native Wayroo + Freedom integration vs. Shopify needing a custom commission-engine stitch — no contest on TCO of the integration alone.
- **DSOs where Shopify is in use for the corporate brand site only, not rep commerce.** Wayroo + Shopify coexist cleanly: Shopify keeps the corporate site, Wayroo handles rep transactions.

---

## When to expect to lose (qualify out)

- **DSOs where modern ecommerce UX is the deciding criterion.** If the buyer's #1 priority is "the brand experience must look like a top retail site," Shopify wins until Wayroo's storefront polish closes the gap.
- **DSOs whose transaction volume is too low to feel the platform-fee stack.** Sub-200-rep companies, or early-stage DSOs on Shopify Basic ($29/mo) — the math doesn't bite yet.
- 🆕 **DSOs with high average order values and no in-person selling.** Above a ~$25 online ticket Shopify Payments is genuinely cheaper per transaction than we are. **Do not open on price here** — lead with architecture, POS and FTC instead.
- **DSOs deeply committed to Shopify across both corporate and rep layers.** High switching cost, and without a champion willing to argue TCO internally, the inertia wins.
- **DSOs whose rep base is 100% online order-forwarders.** No vendor events, no party plan, no cash & carry, no inventory. The Wayroo advantage narrows.

## Top 3 objections + responses

Format: **Acknowledge → Explore → Respond.**

### Objection 1: "Shopify is more modern and has a bigger ecosystem."

- **Acknowledge:** "You're right — Shopify is a phenomenal ecommerce platform. Best in the world at what it's designed for."
- **Explore:** "What ecommerce scenario are you optimizing for — the corporate brand store, or rep-led commerce at vendor events, parties, and in-person?"
- **Respond:** "Shopify wasn't designed for rep-led commerce. And the rate you're quoted isn't the bill — there's the subscription, up to $2,300 a month, plus a third of a percent of your *total* GMV once you're past about $800K a month, plus another chunk if you ever want your own gateway. At $10M a year that's thirty to forty thousand before a single card is processed. The single merchant model puts your DSO on the chargeback hook for every rep dispute. And there's no real POS for vendor events. The 'modernness' doesn't survive contact with how DSO reps actually sell. Most of our clients keep Shopify for the corporate brand site and put rep commerce on Wayroo — that's the architecture that wins both arguments."

### Objection 2: "Our reps already know Shopify."

- **Acknowledge:** "True — the brand is familiar."
- **Explore:** "Do they actually use Shopify to sell, or just to view a corporate store?"
- **Respond:** "Familiarity isn't fit. Reps don't need a storefront viewer; they need a transaction tool — one that handles POS at a vendor table, invoicing after a party, online orders, inventory, and payouts. Wayroo is what reps actually run their business on. Paparazzi's 4,000 monthly active reps process $31M+ on Wayroo, and 60%+ YoY growth is what adoption looks like at scale. Shopify can't show you that for a DSO."

### Objection 3: "We're already on Shopify; the switching cost is too high."

- **Acknowledge:** "Migration is real and I won't pretend otherwise."
- **Explore:** "Which part of Shopify are you actually using — the corporate brand site, or rep storefronts and rep transactions?"
- **Respond:** "You don't have to switch all of it. Most of our clients keep their corporate brand site exactly where it is on Shopify. What changes is the rep commerce layer — POS, invoicing, rep storefronts, payments, inventory. Our Path B integration architecture connects Wayroo directly to your commission engine, so we don't replace your back office and we don't replace your brand site. We replace the broken middle, which is the part that's costing you the platform stack and accumulating chargeback liability."

## Proof points for this matchup

- **Paparazzi:** $31M+ processed, 4,000 monthly active reps, 60%+ YoY growth on Wayroo's per-rep merchant model — the structural alternative to Shopify's single-merchant approach.
- **Per-rep ProPay merchant accounts:** two-tier underwriting, ~99% approval rate. Shopify cannot offer this structurally — it's not part of the platform model.
- **Spendback:** a rep pays their next **wholesale** order from their ProPay balance, ProPay-to-ProPay, off the Visa/MC rails — the **DSO's fee drops from 2.55%+ to 1%.** Cross-account validated (Nancy at Jordan Essentials, "big benefit"; Edmond at Color Street asked for it). Material CFO-level argument. ⚠️ **Scope it correctly: wholesale orders from a rep balance — not retail, not "every transaction."**
- **Path B integration architecture:** Wayroo connects directly to the DSO's commission engine, bypassing Shopify for rep transactions. Lets the corporate brand site stay on Shopify while rep commerce moves to Wayroo.
- **Three-pillar FTC compliance pass-through:** retail transaction record + contractor classification fix (per-rep merchant) + automatic 1099-NEC from ProPay. Shopify is silent on all three.
- **Color Street live evaluation:** $300M+ DSO currently evaluating Wayroo specifically for the rep commerce layer that Shopify can't service (per Color Street's CTO Edmond, May 1 + May 6 calls).

## What NOT to say

- ❌ "Shopify is bad." (It isn't. It's just not built for DSO rep commerce.)
- ❌ "Don't use Shopify." (Most prospects already do; we coexist, not replace.)
- ❌ Lead with FTC compliance. (Supporting argument, not the headline. Lead with POS + per-rep ProPay first, then native integration, then FTC.)
- ❌ Compare storefront UX directly. (Lose by going there. Reframe to transaction architecture.)
- ❌ 🆕 **"Shopify charges 3%."** (Only on Basic. Your mid-market prospect is on Advanced at 2.5% or Plus at 2.15%. Quoting 3% overstates their cost by up to 40% and tells them you didn't check.)
- ❌ 🆕 **"We drop your processing to 1%."** (That's Spendback, on wholesale orders paid from a rep balance. Our retail card rate is 2.85% CP / 3.5% + 15¢ CNP.)
- ❌ 🆕 **Any claim that our card rate beats Shopify's.** (It doesn't above ~$25–40 per transaction. If they run the numbers and you were wrong, you lose the room and every other number on this card.)
