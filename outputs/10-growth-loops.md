# Loop 1 — The Field Leader Loop

```
Field leader adopts and transacts
        |
        v
She sees her downline dashboard          <- works with ZERO DSO involvement
        |
        v
She sees which downline reps are NOT transacting
        |
        v
She tells them to get on it              <- her commissions depend on their volume
        |
        v
Downline reps adopt
        |
        v
Some become field leaders themselves ----+  (loop closes)
```

## Why it works here and almost nowhere else

**The advocate is paid on the outcome.** A field leader's income is a function of her downline's sales volume — so pushing adoption is **self-interested, not altruistic.** That is a structurally stronger referral mechanism than any incentive scheme could manufacture.

## The evidence, now two accounts by two methods

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **JE convention** | Downline dashboards a **top-tier** rep reaction |
| 📊 **Paparazzi survey** | **Adoption rises with rank: Director 100% (18/18) · Star Consultant 80% · Consultant 50%** |

**The rank gradient is the loop, visible in cross-section.** Higher rank = bigger downline = higher adoption.

## Multiplication factor

**The leader's span of control.** One activated leader with 20 downline reps is worth up to 20 individually-persuaded reps at a fraction of the effort.

⚠️ **Unmeasured.** We do not know what share of a leader's downline follows her. **Instrument it:** tag adopters by upline, and measure downline adoption rate for leaders who transact vs. leaders who do not.

## The bottleneck, and the fix

🔴 **She can see who is not transacting — but the product does not tell her.** The dashboard shows sales performance, not Wayroo adoption.

> ### The single highest-leverage product change in this document
>
> **Surface "% of your downline transacting on Wayroo" inside the downline dashboard.**
>
> It converts a reporting feature into a loop amplifier and **makes every field leader an unpaid adoption manager.** Likely a small change to a surface that already exists. Already recommended in the playbook; **still not built.**

## Amplifiers

| Amplifier | Effect |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **"% of downline transacting"** | Closes the loop's broken step |
| **Activate leaders first at every account** | Playbook step 0.4 — a leader who has personally made a sale is the most persuasive asset at a launch |
| **Creative C targeted by downline size** | Reaches leaders directly; works at ProPay-pending accounts |
| Leader-specific first run | Open on the downline widget, not inventory |

# Loop 2 — The Demand Loop

```
Rep adopts (inventory + dashboards work, no DSO needed)
        |
        v
Rep hits a locked selling tool
        |
        v
Rep taps "tell my company I want this"    <- the demand counter
        |
        v
Count accumulates at that DSO
        |
        v
"47 of your reps have asked"  ->  DSO completes ProPay
        |
        v
Selling tools unlock for the WHOLE account  <- step change, not increment
        |
        v
More reps transact, more reps adopt -------+
```

## Why this is the strategically important loop

**It is the only mechanism that opens a DSO conversation**, now that permission is not required. And its payoff is **discontinuous**: one ProPay approval flips an entire account from 0% transacting to eligible.

**It also inverts the sales motion.** ProPay is never requested cold — it arrives as a response to the home office's own field. **The rep is the channel.**

## 🔴 The loop does not exist yet

**The demand counter has not shipped.** Without it:
- The lock produces frustration and **no signal**
- Beat 2 has **no artifact** and no opener
- There is **no account priority queue**

> **Shipping the lock without the counter is worse than not shipping the lock** — rep friction, zero learning, and no fallback conversation. **Critical path.**

## Loop metrics

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Demand density** | Taps ÷ adopters, per DSO — **the ProPay priority queue** |
| **Demand → ProPay conversion** | Do high-density accounts onboard faster? |
| **Post-unlock transact rate** | The step change |
| **Downline adoption rate** | Loop 1's multiplication factor |

# How the Two Loops Compose

**Loop 1 grows adoption within an account. Loop 2 converts adoption into revenue for the whole account.**

| | Loop 1 · Field leader | Loop 2 · Demand |
|---|---|---|
| Needs the DSO? | ❌ **Works at any account** | ✅ It is the DSO ask |
| Output | More adopters | **Revenue eligibility** |
| Payoff | Incremental | **Step change** |
| Status | ⚠️ Partially built | 🔴 **Not built** |

> ### Loop 1 is the hedge, and that matters more than it looks
>
> If the Revolution placement converts poorly, **Loop 1 still works** — it needs no placement, no ProPay, and no permission. **It is the only growth mechanism in the plan that is not gated on something unbuilt.**
>
> Which is also the argument for building the one thing it is missing.
