### Slide 7 — How It Works (1/4): Per-Rep ProPay Merchant Account

**Visual:** Two diagrams side-by-side.

LEFT (titled "Shopify single-merchant model — what you have today on Shopify"):
> Customer → Card processor → DSO single merchant account → All chargebacks, all fees, all liability flow to DSO

RIGHT (titled "Wayroo per-rep ProPay merchant model — what we deliver"):
> Customer → Card processor → Individual rep merchant → Chargebacks, fees, transactions all flow to rep, not DSO

**Headline:**
> **Each of your reps is her own independent business — with her own merchant account.**

**Body copy:**
> Every Wayroo rep gets a ProPay merchant account during onboarding. ProPay underwrites your DSO portfolio once, and each rep then gets approved in a 4-step onboarding flow — ~99% approval rate.
>
> Once she has her merchant account, three things shift to her structurally:
> - **Chargeback liability** — disputes land on her, not on your DSO single merchant
> - **Processing fees** — she pays her per-transaction fees, you don't absorb them across every rep
> - **1099 issuance** — ProPay sends her a 1099 at year-end for credit card sales, automatically. You don't issue it.

**Bottom callout:**
> Result: when one rep has a string of customer disputes, your aggregate merchant standing stays clean. When you scale to 10,000 reps, your processing cost doesn't compound the way it does on Shopify.

**Speaker notes:**
- This is the foundational architectural slide. The CEO needs to understand that per-rep merchant isn't a feature — it's the architectural decision that unlocks everything else (Spendback, Dropship, FTC compliance, 1099 automation).
- Use Brian B's exact line if it lands: *"Okay, interesting"* — the moment the chargeback liability shift was absorbed. If asked, "what does this actually cost?": "ProPay's rate is competitive with Square and lower than Shopify; we'll get you a portfolio-specific quote in the proposal."
- For CTO audience, this is also where Path B integration architecture matters. Defer the architecture deep-dive to a follow-up technical call.

### Slide 10 — How It Works (4/4): Economic Alignment (Spendback + Dropship)

**Visual:** Two flow diagrams side-by-side.

LEFT (titled "Spendback — for wholesale purchases"):
> Rep sells retail → Earnings sit in ProPay → Rep buys next wholesale order from corporate → Paid ProPay-to-ProPay (off Visa/MC rails) → DSO pays 1% Wayroo fee instead of 3% Shopify

RIGHT (titled "Dropship — for catalog sales (Phase 2, 2026)"):
> Rep promotes catalog item → Customer buys → Order ships from corporate directly → Rep paid immediately into her ProPay (vs. 30-day commission wait) → DSO pays 0% processing + 0% chargeback exposure on dropship orders

**Headline:**
> **When your reps sell, the economics work in your favor — not against it.**

**Body copy:**
> **Spendback** is what happens to rep retail earnings. They sit in her ProPay account. When she places her next wholesale order from corporate, she can pay from that balance — directly, ProPay-to-ProPay, off Visa/MasterCard rails. Your processing fee drops from ~3% to 1%.
>
> **Dropship** (launching in 2026) gives reps a third income stream — catalog items they don't stock, shipped directly from corporate, paid immediately into the rep's own ProPay merchant. You eliminate processing fees AND chargeback exposure on every dropship order.

**Bottom callout:**
> Three accounts have validated Spendback at the CEO/Founder level. Paparazzi quantified the savings; Jordan Essentials and Color Street both reacted with "big benefit" / "that's great." Dropship is first-mover — no competitor offers rep-level dropship with individual merchant accounts.

**Speaker notes:**
- This is the CFO slide. The economic argument that lands at the leadership level.
- Pre-bake the math for the specific prospect: "At your scale and average ticket, that's $X/year in Spendback savings at even 25% adoption."
- For Color-Street-style C&C accounts: lead with Spendback (Brian B did the savings math live in May 6 call). For Hybrid accounts: lead with both — Spendback for the wholesale-order economics, Dropship as the Phase 2 hero for the non-C&C majority.

### Slide 15 — Investment Model + Economic Alignment

**Visual:** Two-column diagram.

LEFT — Traditional SaaS vendors (Aice, Rallyware, Shopify):
> Vendor wins when DSO subscribes (regardless of rep activity).
> DSO wins when reps sell.
> Misalignment: vendor doesn't need rep activity to be profitable.

RIGHT — Wayroo:
> Vendor wins when reps transact (transaction-fee economics).
> DSO wins when reps transact.
> Rep wins when reps transact.
> **All three optimize for the same metric.**

**Headline:**
> **The more your reps sell, the cheaper Wayroo gets.**

**Body copy:**
> Wayroo's revenue comes from transaction-fee rev-share on every transaction running through ProPay. The SaaS license fee ($1/active rep/month) phases out as your processing volume crosses a threshold.
>
> **Practically:** for DSOs with high rep transaction volume, Wayroo becomes near-zero on the SaaS side. We make our money from your reps' success — not from your subscription.
>
> **eSuite pass-through:** Most DSOs already charge their reps a monthly tech-access fee (eSuite). Wayroo's per-user fee bundles into your existing eSuite billing — *you don't absorb the cost, you collect it.*

**Bottom callout:**
> No vendor whose incentive is misaligned with yours can compete on this. Aice charges per-seat regardless of activity. Shopify charges 3% on every transaction plus monthly license. Wayroo charges when your reps win.

**Speaker notes:**
- This is the CFO / CEO slide on pricing. Don't get into specific numbers unless the prospect pushes — defer detailed pricing to the proposal.
- The three-way alignment framing is the headline. Brian B (Color Street) absorbed this directly when Autumn disclosed the revenue model in the May 6 call. CEOs respond well to "vendor incentive matches yours" — it's a trust-building moment.
- For Color-Street-specific Wayroo-only pricing conversations: emphasize that we'd model the deal against the *real-seller cohort* (not nominal active rep count). This is the seller-vs-hobbyist sub-dimension applied to pricing.

Before launching into the deck, surface these answers in conversation. The deck order and emphasis adapts based on what you learn.

**For all DSO conversations:**
- *"What percentage of your active reps are actually selling to customers vs. just buying at the rep discount?"* (Seller-vs-hobbyist split → addressable Wayroo cohort sizing)
- *"What percentage of your reps sell from personal inventory vs. fulfilling catalog orders?"* (C&C / Hybrid / Non-C&C segmentation)
- *"What does your rep-facing tech stack look like — what does the rep log into, what does that pull from?"* (Integration scope — Shopify? Custom? Other?)
- *"When is your next stylist convention, summit, or annual event?"* (Forcing function)
- *"What's your current processing rate on rep wholesale orders, and how much do you process per month?"* (Spendback economic argument setup)

**For CEO/Founder conversations:**
- *"Where are your rep sales and engagement trends today?"* (Decline-driven recovery framing — Hybrid pattern)
- *"Walk me through what happens in the first 30 days after a new stylist signs up — do you feel that flow is working?"* (Onboarding pain → Phase 2 expansion lever)
- *"How much time do your reps spend per week on admin tasks — and how much of that generates revenue?"* (Admin-time-vs-selling-time cornerstone)

**For CTO conversations:**
- *"Do you have any 'two sources of truth' sync issues today between systems?"* (Path B integration positioning)
- *"What does SSO and user management look like today for your rep environment?"* (Integration scope)
