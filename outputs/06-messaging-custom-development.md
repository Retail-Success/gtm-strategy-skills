## The one idea

> **Configuration is included. Your process is an investment.**

Everything below serves one behaviour change. Custom development is **named, scoped and priced** in every conversation, and **never offered to close a deal.**

## 2. Messaging pillars (three, no more)

### Pillar 1 — Your process, inside your platform

**Headline:** *We build it into the module you already run. We don't build you another app.*
- **Party 2.0** is a ground-up rebuild of the native party module, with per-client branding. It *"runs on the same underlying data as the experience it replaces,"* so existing parties, orders and history were there from day one.
- **Dynamic Kit Builder** lives inside the party and cart order flow. Reps don't go to a separate tool.
- The alternative is an agency building on an API, or another point tool with another login. Both add a seam that someone has to maintain.

**Objection it answers:** *"Why not have our agency build it on your API?"*

### Pillar 2 — Built by the team that builds the platform

**Headline:** *One team, one contract, one number to call.*
- Custom work is built in ByDesign code by the platform team (the JBloom/Customly precedent).
- Our track record: **Dynamic Kit Builder** (Tropic), **Customly** (JBloom), **Custom Pack Slip** (Lemongrass), **ShipStation integration** (JBloom) and **Party 2.0** (Tropic) all started as one client's request. They're in the product today.
- Back-office and rep-app changes come from the same team (ByDesign + Wayroo).

**Objection it answers:** *"Who do we call when it breaks?"*
⚠️ **Not a uniqueness claim.** Exigo builds in-house too. Use this pillar against alternatives 2, 4 and 5 (agency, point tool, workaround), not against Exigo.

### Pillar 3 — Scoped, priced, scheduled

**Headline:** *A spec, a BA, a price and a date. Not a favour.*
- Every request is triaged, and most turn out to be configuration, included in implementation.
- What's truly custom gets a BA-led scoping session, a written spec, a fixed price and a scheduled start after launch.
- Changes after sign-off are change orders. That protects the client's budget as much as ours.

**Objection it answers:** *"Can't you just throw that in?"*
**This is the pillar that fixes the ticket.** Free work comes with no spec, no date and no accountability. Paid work comes with all three. Say that out loud.

## 3. By audience

| Audience | Their priority | Lead with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CEO / founder** | Their process is their brand, and they don't want to hire developers | Pillar 1 plus the feature track record | Hours, story points, internal process |
| **COO / VP Ops** | Fewer workarounds, fewer vendors | Pillar 2 (one vendor), then Pillar 3 | "Custom" in the context of launch. Ops hears risk. |
| **CFO** | A predictable number | Pillar 3: a fixed price, change orders, dates | Open-ended "time and materials" language without a cap |
| **CTO / IT** | Maintainability and ownership | Pillar 1 (same data, no seam). Say ownership and upgrade terms are defined in the SOW. | Promising upgrade carry-forward or IP ownership until engineering and legal confirm |
| **Existing client (CSM / AM conversation)** | "We already pay you, so why does this cost more?" | "Your subscription covers the platform and its roadmap. This is a change built for your business." | "Everyone gets this for free." Since 9/17, gaps past the group-2 rollout are billable. |

## 6. Words we use / words we avoid

| We say | We don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "That's configuration. It's part of implementation." | "Sure, we can build that." | Most requests *are* configuration. Say so first. |
| "That's an extension. Let's scope it." | "That's a small tweak." / "That's easy." | Nobody on a sales call knows the effort. Party 2.0 was estimated at 1,200 hours. |
| "Phase 2, after you're live" | "We'll have it ready for launch" | Rule 2: custom work is never on the launch path. |
| "Investment" / "engagement" / "SOW" | "Fee," "charge," "extra" | Frames it as value, not a penalty |
| "Let me get a BA on the next call" | Giving any estimate, in hours or dollars, on the call | Estimates come from scoping, not from sales |
| "That's on our roadmap. Here's what's shipped today." | Demoing unshipped work as if it's available | The January Party 2.0 demo set a prospect's expectation for work that wasn't ready |
| "We may not be the right fit for that." | "Yes" to everything | Rule 6: sometimes no is the right answer |
| "Exigo sells professional services too. Ask them to scope it." | "Exigo doesn't do custom dev." | It's false. See the positioning file. |
| "Platform extension" | "Hack," "workaround," "one-off" | We're changing the product. Present it that way. |
| "Included" (config and implementation) | "Free" (anything) | "Free" trains the buyer to expect free. "Included" means they already paid for it. |
