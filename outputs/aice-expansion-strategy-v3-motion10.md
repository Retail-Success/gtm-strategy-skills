## Motion 1 — Referral channel (no-code, fastest)

**Mechanic:** AiCE refers net-new clients on the AiCE platform to ByDesign (back office) and Wayroo (rep-facing inventory + POS). Standard partner referral with deal-reg and a rev-share % on closed business. **Creator-economy sub-track** (formerly Motion 5): AiCE refers creators / social-commerce brands that mature into needing back-office (commissions, genealogy, payments at scale) into ByDesign. Distinct deal-reg track because the deal profile (creator-economy startup vs. classic MLM DSO) is materially different.

### Expansion math

- BYD-3 (new client booked revenue) target = $500K for 2026 (corporate goal, pre-existing).
- AiCE referrals are expected to contribute a material portion of BYD-3 in 2026; specific $ target set once term sheet rev-share % is confirmed.
- Assume average new BD/Wayroo ACV ≈ $50–80K. A handful of referred deals materially moves the BYD-3 number.

### Sequencing

- June 2026 — term sheet with rev-share %, deal-reg rules, exclusivity language, creator-economy sub-track.
- July 2026 — AiCE sales team enablement: one-pager, BD/Wayroo positioning, when-to-refer triggers.
- Q3 2026 — first referred deals in pipeline.
- Q4 2026 — new revenue from referrals (within 4 months of term-sheet close).

### Open levers

- Rev-share % — pricing mood board hypothesis: floor 10% Y1, mid 15% Y1 + 5% Y2, ceiling 20% Y1 + 7–10% recurring Y2+ with exclusivity.
- Recurring kicker on years 2+ — without it, AiCE will lose interest. Build in tail rev-share.
- Creator-economy sub-track deal-reg — separate pipeline track for ACV / cycle-length differences.

## Motion 2 — Payment processor recommendation switch (highest LTV)

**Mechanic:** AiCE currently recommends **Metrix Global** to its DSO clients. Going forward AiCE recommends **ProPay** (and Nuvei for Freedom replicated sites) instead. Every AiCE client that adopts the recommendation generates payments rev-share for Wayroo. **This is the highest-LTV motion** because payments revenue is recurring transaction-volume revenue, not one-time.

### Two sub-plays — confirm scope with Brian

- **Sub-play 2a — New AiCE clients only.** Slow ramp; only captures AiCE's net-new pipeline.
- **Sub-play 2b — Migration of existing Metrix clients.** Materially larger TAM but requires AiCE's active cooperation (their CS, their relationship capital). Likely requires a kicker for AiCE on migrated accounts.

**Recommendation:** Push for 2b. The size of AiCE's installed base on Metrix is the determining variable — get that number from Brian on the next call.

### Expansion math (illustrative — inputs unconfirmed)

- Annual payments rev-share scales with migrated AiCE volume + new AiCE clients going forward. Range to be confirmed once Metrix-installed DSO count + average transaction volume are known.
- **AiCE payments rev-share hard ceiling: 25%.** Pricing mood board hypothesis: open at 18% AiCE / 82% Wayroo; concede toward 25% only for exclusivity or aggressive 10a/10b account commitments.

### Key open items

- Exact size of Metrix-installed AiCE DSO base (ask Brian).
- Wayroo's payments rev-share net margin per transaction (ask Morgan).
- Migration friction: does Metrix have lock-in, processing penalties, or contract termination clauses on these accounts?
- AiCE's incentive structure on migrated accounts — must be enough for their CS team to do the work. Pricing mood board recommends **2x rev-share for 12 months on migrated accounts** as the migration kicker.

## Motion 9 — Wayroo + AiCE pitched to existing ByDesign clients

**Mechanic:** Wayroo proactively pitches "Wayroo + AiCE" to existing ByDesign clients. Two outcomes we want: (a) **retention** — partnership-led upsell deepens the account and reduces churn risk, (b) **Wayroo cross-sell** — standalone Wayroo has been hard to close inside our existing BYD base; co-selling with AiCE gives a stronger value prop and a fresh reason to re-open the conversation.

**Confirmed structure:** Wayroo is the lead contracting entity; AiCE is the bolt-on. BYD account team owns the relationship and billing.

### Why this works now

- BYD clients already trust the ByDesign relationship — adding Wayroo + AiCE is an additive sell, not a vendor swap.
- AiCE brings rep-facing tooling we don't have, which is the gap most often cited by BYD clients evaluating standalone Wayroo.
- Joint pitch reduces procurement friction (one conversation, one bundle).

### Expansion math

- BYD-4 (Churn) and BYD-3 (new revenue) both move. Sized once the pilot pitch lands with the first 3 clients.

### Sequencing

- June 2026 — pitch deck drafted (Sam + Morgan).
- June–July 2026 — first 3 BYD clients identified for pilot pitch.
- Q3 2026 — pilot pitches delivered; iterate based on response.
- Q4 2026 — pilot conversions reported; scaled rollout if signal is positive.

### Key open items

- Which BYD clients are highest-fit? (Account-by-account scoring needed.)
- **Who pays for the AiCE component in the bundle** — keep rep-paid PLG (with eSuite pass-through), or shift to corporate-paid seat licensing, or hybrid. Central pricing decision; ask Brian May 29.
- Deal-reg handling when the BYD relationship pre-dates AiCE involvement. Working assumption: BYD-led deals = AiCE earns AiCE-component revenue only; AiCE-sourced deals = standard Motion 1 referral rev-share applies on top.

## Motion 8 — Pillars introduction + lite back-office bundle

**Mechanic:** AiCE introduces Wayroo to **Pillars**, a back-office-lite competitor with roughly 10 clients. Two angles:

1. **Direct integration** — Wayroo integrates with Pillars. If Pillars clients adopt Wayroo, we earn payments revenue from that base.
2. **Bundled lite offering** — "Wayroo + AiCE + Pillars" packaged as a lite back-office offering at low price point, aimed at new DSO startups that don't yet need full ByDesign back-office. This is a wedge into the very-early-stage DSO market we don't service today.

### Expansion math

- ~10 Pillars clients today. If 30–50% adopt Wayroo over 12 months, payments revenue accrues per transaction.
- Bundled lite SKU TAM = new DSO startups we currently miss because ByDesign's full back-office is overkill / too expensive at their stage.
- Pricing mood board hypothesis: Lite-SKU bundle $3K–$4.5K/mo all-in; 3-way split Wayroo 50% / AiCE 30% / Pillars 20%; Wayroo as lead vendor + billing entity.

### Sequencing

- June 2026 — warm intro to Pillars from Brian.

- Q3 2026 — commercial scoping of the lite bundle (price, rev-share split across Wayroo/AiCE/Pillars).

### Key open items

- 3-way rev-share split.
- Does the lite bundle cannibalize ByDesign upsell into Pillars clients later? Mitigated by a clear graduation path: clients outgrowing Pillars's cap upgrade to full ByDesign at full pricing.

## Motion 10 — Cart-replacement payments rev-share (recapture + winback) **NEW**

**Mechanic:** Some BYD clients have refused or migrated away from our shopping cart because of the outdated UI. **Bravenly** (current BYD client, refused Wayroo, low C&C, runs AiCE) is hypothesized to be on AiCE Campfire + Medusa + Metrix — confirmation outstanding. **Super Patch** (ex-BYD client) migrated to Shopify. Cart V3 (Wayroo's new shopping cart, being built on Medusa with Adyen/ProPay/Nuvei processor stack) creates a structural opportunity to capture or recapture payments revenue from these account profiles.

### Two sub-plays

**Motion 10a — Recapture (BYD client on AiCE + non-Wayroo cart):**
- Target profile: current BYD client using AiCE + Medusa + Metrix (Bravenly hypothesis)
- Mechanic: swap the underlying cart to Wayroo-on-Medusa (technically same Medusa base — primarily a processor swap + native Wayroo cart features)
- Wayroo earns payments rev-share on transaction volume; shares a portion with AiCE in exchange for AiCE offering the DSO a *discounted monthly rep license fee*
- Net effect: DSO sees lower AiCE cost (buy-in friction drops); AiCE earns payments revenue they don't have today (they earn zero from Metrix and Medusa currently); Wayroo captures rep-layer payments that would otherwise route to a competing processor

**Motion 10b — Winback (ex-BYD client on Shopify):**
- Target profile: BYD ex-client migrated to Shopify (Super Patch)
- **Primary winback lever is the new back-office cart we're building.** The "outdated UI" objection that drove these accounts away is the problem Cart V3 is being built to solve. Cart V3 alone may pull these accounts back without needing the AiCE-fronted recapture motion at all.
- **Motion 10b is the fallback** for accounts where Cart V3 doesn't close a full back-office winback: pitch the DSO that their reps can use AiCE with Wayroo cart underneath for rep-level commerce only. Same rev-share + license-discount mechanic as 10a.
- Net effect: Cart V3 leads with the back-office winback; 10b is the rep-side recapture path if full back-office winback fails. Two sequenced levers.

### Why AiCE says yes (strongest "yes" lever in the partnership)
- Today AiCE earns zero from Metrix and zero from Medusa
- A payments rev-share cut from Wayroo is **pure additive revenue** for AiCE
- Funding a DSO license discount comes out of new revenue — net positive for AiCE
- No cannibalization of their existing PLG revenue

### Why the DSO says yes
- Reduces monthly cost (AiCE license discount)
- No back-office migration required for 10a; for 10b, Cart V3 closes the UI objection that drove them away
- Reps stay on the AiCE surface they already use

### Why Wayroo says yes
- Captures payments revenue from accounts where we'd otherwise earn zero
- Recapture (10a) without needing the DSO to fully re-adopt the Wayroo rep app
- Winback (10b) leverages Cart V3 (already on 2026 roadmap) — no incremental engineering scope
- ByDesign / Wayroo owns the cart integration work unless AiCE volunteers eng effort

### Pricing (per mood board)

| Variable | Open at | Hold ceiling at |
|---|---|---|
| AiCE share of Wayroo payments rev-share on Motion 10 volume | 18% | **25% (hard cap, applies across Motions 2 + 10)** |
| AiCE-funded rep license discount to DSO | 15% off list | 20% off list |
| Wayroo retention of payments rev-share | 82% | 75% |

## Motion 7 — PLG via AiCE individual users

**Mechanic:** AiCE has ~100k individual users (creators, individual reps, social sellers). Wayroo onboards them as **individual merchants** through Motion 3's self-serve flow. Once those individual users are on Wayroo, they have an incentive to push Wayroo up-chain to the DSO they're affiliated with — driving DSO-level adoption from the bottom up. This mirrors AiCE's own bottom-up GTM motion.

### Why this is a separate motion from 3'

- Motion 3' is the **technical / product play** — Wayroo as the commerce stack for individual sellers.
- Motion 7 is the **GTM extension** — using the individual-seller base as a Trojan horse to land at the DSO level.

### Expansion math

- 100k individual-user TAM. Conversion to Wayroo merchant + average per-seller transaction volume sizes payments revenue.
- Up-chain conversions: of the individual users who adopt Wayroo, what % successfully push Wayroo up to their DSO? This is the multiplier on Motion 1.

Reframed v2 play: Wayroo cart powers commerce not just for Campfire DSO deployments but for AiCE's **individual sellers / creators**. Wayroo earns payments rev-share on every transaction.

## Motions 4 and 6 — sized at sketch level

### Motion 4 — Agentic features / collaboration

**Mechanic:** AiCE's Agent (hybrid retrieval over content + external tool reach per their architecture diagram) integrates with Wayroo data as a tool. Reverse direction: Wayroo's AI Shopping Agent reads AiCE content / contacts / promotions context to personalize cart recommendations.

**Cost:** Medium-high engineering, mostly reusing API gateway + AI Shopping Agent scope on the 2026 roadmap.

**Revenue:** API tier revenue (Partner tier, per unified strategy Pillar 3) + secondary lift on AI Shopping Agent conversion. Note: Partner-tier fees are waived for AiCE as long as revenue flows via other motions (revenue-share offset mechanic — see pricing mood board Structure E).

**Timing:** Q4 2026 design, 2027 build.

### Motion 6 — Advanced agentic commerce

**Mechanic:** Agent-to-agent commerce. AiCE's Agent recognizes commerce intent and hands off to Wayroo's AI Shopping Agent (cart, inventory, payment context) to close the loop autonomously. AiCE owns the intent surface; Wayroo owns the transaction surface; agents handshake.

**Cost:** High engineering, 2027 horizon.

**Revenue:** Premium transaction rev-share + API tier; positions both companies as the AI-native commerce default in direct selling.
