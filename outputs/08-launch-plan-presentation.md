# Executive Summary

**On July 17–18, Jordan Essentials launched Wayroo to their field. 700+ reps downloaded it in 48 hours — 28% of a 2,500-rep base. The deal closed in 11 weeks, not the usual 4–6 months. Three weeks later, the founder asked to start paying early, cutting short her own free period.**

That launch, combined with confirmation that Wayroo integrates with Exigo and other back offices, surfaces a conclusion this plan is built around:

> ## We are leading with the harder product.
>
> We sell Freedom — a back-office replacement — and attach Wayroo. Every piece of evidence says the reverse works better. Wayroo excites both the buyer and the field, closes in a quarter of the time, and can sit on top of any back office, **including Exigo's installed base.**

**This plan does not ask leadership to reposition the company.** It asks for approval to test that thesis cheaply over the next 21 weeks, using only shipped product, and to make the category decision in Q4 with real evidence instead of two anecdotes.

# 1. What Changed

## The Jordan Essentials launch

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Downloads, launch weekend | **700+**, still growing |
| Rep base | ~2,500 |
| **Adoption in 48 hours** | **~28%** |
| Deal cycle | **~11 weeks** (vs. 4–6 months standard) |
| Rep-facing price | $15/mo, up from $10 — **eSuite pass-through now proven in production** |
| Customer action after launch | **Founder pulled paid start forward to September** |

## What the field told us

Rep enthusiasm concentrated entirely on **selling and visibility tools**:

1. **Tap-to-Pay** — the strongest single reaction. **Not yet shipped.**
2. Invoicing with payment links
3. Downline dashboard reports
4. Dashboard widgets
5. Back-office data on their phone, without a separate Freedom login

**Media Library landed flat with reps** — it is Hope's brand-control requirement, not a field need. Worth noting because it is currently credited against our Wayroo active-user target; a feature reps don't value will not drive rep adoption.

## Three findings with consequences beyond this account

**1. Reps spend a minimum of $60/month on tools** — Canva, ChatGPT, Square, Boards, Project Broadcast. Wayroo arrives for **$5 incremental.** This is the clearest rep-economics argument we have ever had, and it is quotable.

**2. We were wrong about how many reps sell in person.** Jordan Essentials was classified at ~20% cash-and-carry. That is confirmed understated — **at an account where we hold the back-office data.** If we misread it here, the same estimate is unreliable across the six remaining candidates. **Recommendation: stop asking DSOs what their C&C share is and derive it from Freedom data. They appear not to know.**

**3. Our own back office was part of the problem.** Before Wayroo, reps had to log into ByDesign after each sale to manage inventory, or keep spreadsheets. Part of Wayroo's value to the field is fixing a Freedom UX gap. Internal framing only — but it should inform product priorities.

# 2. The Strategic Case: The Wedge Is Backwards

## The evidence

| | |
|---|---|
| Both live prospect records are **Wayroo-first**. Color Street is Wayroo-only with no Freedom pitch at all. | `prospects/color-street.md` |
| Jordan Essentials closed in **~11 weeks** as a Wayroo attach | `prospects/jordan-essentials.md` |
| **28% field adoption in 48 hours**; enthusiasm described as palpable | Launch |
| The founder **shortened her own free period** | Launch |
| **Wayroo integrates with Exigo and other back offices** | Confirmed Aug 8 |

## What inverting the motion would change

| | Freedom-led (today) | Wayroo-led |
|---|---|---|
| The ask | "Replace your back office" | "Add the app your reps already want" |
| Cycle | 4–6 months | **~11 weeks observed** |
| Addressable market | DSOs willing to rip out a back office | **Any DSO — including Exigo's installed base** |
| Champion | Corporate only | **Corporate and the field** |
| Expansion | — | Wayroo → Freedom at renewal |
| Competitive effect | **Exigo is a wall** | **Exigo's installed base becomes a market** |

That last row is the one worth sitting with. Exigo is the largest player in the category. A product that rides on top of Exigo converts our biggest competitor's customer base from a barrier into a target list.

## What this plan does — and deliberately does not do

**Does:** sequences Aug–Dec so a bounded, low-cost test (Action 5) produces real evidence, using only shipped capability.

**Does not:** rewrite category positioning. That is a joint Unified-Strategy/GTM decision, not a Phase 8 call. **We are asking for the Q4 decision to be scheduled, not made now.**

**Also does not:** build any GTM on unshipped product. Wayroo Back Office Lite and rep-added personal inventory would both strengthen this case considerably. Neither appears anywhere in this plan. Tap-to-Pay has just demonstrated the cost of getting ahead of the roadmap — at 2,500 reps.

# 3. The Plan — Five Actions

**Ownership splits along one line, and it is the organizing principle of the plan:**

> **The sales cycle is 4–6 months. There are 4.7 months left in the year.** Nothing opened in August closes in 2026. So Cassie and Autumn own the 2026 number through the installed base, and Daniel's job is to seed 2027 — because that is what the calendar permits.

| # | Action | Owner | 2026 revenue? | Success metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Hybrid DSO decline-scan upsell sprint** | **Cassie + Autumn** | ✅ **The number** | **2 closed by Dec 31** |
| **2** | Consultant referral program activation | Daniel | ⚠️ Partial | 5 partners, 5 intros, 1 close |
| **3** | Publish the proof (Jordan Essentials + Paparazzi) | Sam | Indirect — **gates the others** | Both live by Sept 30 |
| **4** | Kill Google Ads, reallocate to Capterra | Sam | Cost avoidance | $0 Ads spend; Capterra opps ≥10 |
| **5** | Bounded Exigo-base test | Daniel | ❌ **No — buys evidence** | Meeting acceptance vs. baseline |

## Action 1 — Hybrid DSO Upsell Sprint · *the 2026 number*

Seven candidates were identified May 7 and have sat for three months. Jordan Essentials proves the pattern converts: existing client + declining rep engagement + a convention deadline + two aligned economic buyers = an 11-week close.

**Two screening corrections from the launch:** derive C&C share from Freedom data rather than asking, and screen for the forcing function in Week 1 rather than Week 8. Candidates without a Q4 event revert to a slow cycle and fall outside the year.

## Action 2 — Partner Program · *the largest correctable inefficiency*

Our partner channel converts at **71.4% lead-to-opportunity.** Google Ads converts at 2.5% — a 29x difference.

**The partner channel supplied 5 of 67 opportunities in 2025. That is 7.5%.**

The best-converting channel we have receives almost none of the input. This is a supply problem, not a performance problem, and the program design is already complete and unlaunched. Compensation is entirely performance-based — **no fixed spend, no downside.**

## Action 3 — Publish the Proof · *gates everything else*

Zero published case studies. Zero testimonials. Our own 90-day plan set "Paparazzi case study published" as an August milestone — **that is now, and it has not shipped.**

The correction worth noting: **this is not an evidence problem, it is a permission-and-packaging problem.** We hold a $31M customer result, a 28%-in-48-hours launch, three founder-level quotes, 51 clients, a DSA award, and 4.8 stars on Capterra. None of it is published. The work is asking and formatting — days, not a quarter.

**Jordan Essentials is now the more valuable of the two assets.** Paparazzi proves scale at a long-live client. Jordan Essentials proves **repeatability in the exact segment Action 1 sells into, on an 11-week timeline** — and it is three weeks old.

> 🔴 **One risk that could still break this.** Jordan Essentials was sold on reversing a 6-month decline, and **no pre-launch baseline was captured.** 700 downloads proves adoption, not recovery. The adoption story publishes without it. The recovery story — the version that sells the other six candidates — does not. **Reconstruct from Freedom historical reports in Week 1.**

## Action 4 — Kill Google Ads

2025: **$17,000 → 5 opportunities → $0 closed.** Capterra: $19,345 → $213,275 at ~29%.

Stated plainly rather than buried: a 0% close rate on 5 opportunities is too small a sample to prove a channel is dead. The argument is **opportunity cost against a 29x-better alternative in the same budget line** — not proof the channel cannot work.

## Action 5 — Bounded Exigo Test · *buys evidence, not bookings*

**Hypothesis:** a DSO on Exigo will take a meeting about a rep app that sits *on top of* their back office at a materially higher rate than about replacing it.

**Scope: 5–10 accounts. Hard cap.** Measured on **meeting acceptance rate vs. the Freedom-led baseline** — explicitly *not* on closed revenue, which the calendar forbids. Uses only shipped capability.

If acceptance beats baseline, leadership has evidence for the Q4 decision. If it doesn't, the thesis is weakened cheaply and early. Both outcomes are worth a few weeks of one rep's time.

# 4. The Structural Problem With WAY-1

This needs to be said directly, because two of our 2026 objectives are in tension and it will surface in January if it doesn't surface now.

**Closing a Wayroo deal in 2026 does not add Wayroo users in 2026.**

```
Jordan Essentials:  May 6 (agreement in motion) → July 17 (launch) → 700 users
                                   ~11 weeks
```

A deal closing December 31 launches in **March 2027**. So Action 1's two targeted closes — the centerpiece of our 2026 revenue plan — contribute **approximately zero** to the active-user target this year. Implementation sits between them.

**Projected December 31: ~5,600–5,900 active users against a 10,000 target.** The gap holds in every scenario, including the optimistic one.

### Three responses, in order of preference

1. **Re-baseline WAY-1 to ~6,000 for 2026**, with 10,000 as a Q2 2027 target reflecting implementation lag. **Recommended.**
2. **Apply the real-seller denominator.** `strategic-inputs.md` already flags that WAY-1 should mean the real-seller cohort, not nominal active reps. Against that denominator ~5,900 is a materially better result than it appears — **but this must be settled before the number is reported, not after.**
3. **Deepen Jordan Essentials adoption** — the cheapest lever in the entire plan.

### The cheapest lever available anywhere in this plan

Jordan Essentials sits at 28% of 2,500 reps after 48 hours, **with zero adoption spend since.** Moving 28% → 85% adds roughly **1,400 users — 26% of the entire WAY-1 gap** — from a customer who has already said yes, at the cost of an enablement push rather than a sales cycle.

**Nothing else in this plan produces users that cheaply.** It was not in the original action list; the funnel math says it should be. **Recommend adding it as a formal workstream.**

⚠️ **And it depends on Tap-to-Pay.** The single strongest rep draw at launch has not shipped. The adoption curve past 700 likely depends on it more than on any enablement campaign we run.

## The unit economics — and why they argue for a person, not a budget

| Channel | Cash | Closes | CAC | LTV:CAC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Installed base | $0 | 2 | $6,000 | **67:1** |
| Capterra | $12,060 | 2.9 | $6,227 | **64:1** |
| Partner network | $2,500 + comm. | 1 | $16,500 | ~15:1 |
| *Google Ads (2025)* | *$17,000* | *0* | *∞* | *0:1* |

**Every retained channel clears the 3:1 benchmark by more than an order of magnitude.** That is not a sign of excellent marketing. It is a sign of **structural under-investment** — we spend 0.4% of revenue on marketing against a 5–20% benchmark.

**But the constraint is not cash.** Capterra at 64:1 would justify 5–10x the spend on pure economics, and cannot absorb it, because ten more opportunities per quarter would sit unworked in a one-rep pipeline.

> **The 2027 ask is therefore a sales hire, not a media budget.** Increasing spend before capacity converts a cash constraint into a queue.
