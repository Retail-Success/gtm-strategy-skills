## The one rule

> **Demo the seam, not the surface.**

A renovated storefront looks like the storefront they already have. What they can't get anywhere else is the **joins**: shop → enrollment, one brand across both, configuration instead of a ticket. The 2026-09-22 demo proved this the hard way — five minutes on the storefront got *"it's pretty much the same,"* thirty seconds on enrollment got *"probably the best thing that I've seen so far."*

---

## The pitch in four beats

**1 · The seam (lead here).** *"Your next rep is already on your site — they're shopping."* Today they leave, find a form somewhere else, start over. Here it's the same journey, and the referring rep is still attached at the end of it. *"A click is costly."*

**2 · Configuration instead of a ticket.** Branding Studio: colour, type, layout, page configuration — live preview, draft → publish. **The cost line:** *"This is the ~$4,500 conversation. Customization is a services engagement today. Here the everyday version is a setting. You're paying for the hard part, not for changing a colour."* Close on posture, not controls: *"the answer to 'can it do this?' stops being 'file a request and wait.'"*

**3 · The DSO moat, shown not listed.** AutoShip that honours promos and price changes on renewal · shop-on-behalf (**structurally prohibited on Shopify** — Pure Haven still dual-runs our cart just for this) · embedded enrollment (**Shopify has no distributor object — the hand-off is forced by its data model**) · parties, rank pricing, volume, ~148 per-country settings, native not plugins.

**4 · The custom-build cost (only if they're building or evaluating).** Delivered as observation, never warning: *"The clients who build their own usually aren't wrong about the problem — they're right, and they stopped believing we'd fix it. What gets underestimated is that the storefront isn't the hard part. The hard part is AutoShip that catches a promo on renewal, and attribution that still resolves to the right rep nine months later. That's what you rebuild every time your back office changes — and you'd be building it alone."*

⚠️ **Never name another client.** Use the shape.

Then stop driving for ten minutes. Capability gaps are parity-sweep input, not objections to handle.

## ⛔ Do not say

| Don't | Instead |
|---|---|
| A date, or "Q1" as a promise | *"Targeting Q1, and taking the time to get AutoShip, attribution and commissions right"* |
| "It's ready" | *"The experience is real. The back end is the work in front of us"* |
| Any speed number | **No benchmark exists.** *"I won't give you a number I haven't measured on your data"* |
| "It'll be free" | *"Everyday customization becomes self-serve; bespoke stays a services engagement. Packaging isn't mine to commit to"* |
| Any extension API / SDK / plugin mechanism | Nothing is specified. Ask what they'd want to extend |
| "Enroll without leaving the cart" | ✅ **"Enroll from the shop"** — entry point is client-placed, MVP hands off for payment |
| "Since you're on Shopify…" | *"What are you on today?"* |
| Anything about single-domain / framed cart (U6) | 🔴 Legacy has it, **V3 records it as a gap.** Unresolved — keep it out of client-facing conversation |
| TCO / "you stop paying twice" percentages | No verified model. Do not improvise |
