# Influencer Strategy — Field Leaders

**Phase:** 11 — Running Marketing, Task 7
**Date:** 2026-08-30 · **Owner:** Sam Atieh · Autumn Fowers (relationships)

> ### The influencers are already inside the accounts, and they are paid on the outcome
>
> Task 7 assumes finding external micro-influencers whose audience overlaps the ICP. **This market has something better.**
>
> **A field leader has a downline of 20–200 reps who are exactly the ICP, who already trust her, and whose sales volume determines her own income.** She is not a borrowed audience — she is a distribution channel with financial incentive to promote.
>
> **No payment, no contract, no disclosure requirement.** The alignment is structural.

---

# Why This Beats Conventional Influencer Marketing

| | External micro-influencer | **Field leader** |
|---|---|---|
| Audience overlap | Approximate | **Exact — her downline is the ICP** |
| Trust | Borrowed | **Direct, ongoing, financial** |
| Cost | $500–5,000+ per placement | **$0** |
| Incentive | Paid to post | 🔴 **Paid on the outcome** |
| Measurable | Referral links | **Deterministic — we know the upline relationship** |
| Scale | One at a time | **Every account has dozens** |

# The Evidence

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **JE convention** | **Downline dashboards a top-tier rep reaction** — leaders want visibility into their teams |
| 📊 **Paparazzi survey** | **Adoption rises with rank: Director 100% (18/18) · Star Consultant 80% · Consultant 50%** |

**We do not need to find these people. The back office already knows who they are.**

| Signal | Source |
|---|---|
| **Downline size** | Back office — **the primary signal** |
| **Rank** | Back office. Paparazzi: **Director-level is 100% adopted** |
| **Personal sales volume** | Back office |
| **Already adopted Wayroo** | Product |

> **Deterministic targeting.** No outreach lists, no engagement audits, no follower-count guessing. **A query returns the top 30 field leaders at any account in seconds** — an advantage no conventional influencer programme has.

# The Partnership Models

**Lightest to heaviest. Start light.**

| # | Model | Ask | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 🔴 **Early access** | Be first at your company. **Get transacting before your team sees it.** | Minimal |
| **2** | **Live training seat** | Join Autumn's session; bring your team | Low |
| **3** | **Feedback partner** | Copy tests, usability sessions, "what would you change?" | Low — **and it doubles as research** |
| **4** | **Named advocate** | Let us quote you at other accounts | Medium — **needs written permission** |
| **5** | Convention co-presenter | Share the stage | High, and the highest impact |

> ### Model 1 is the whole programme, and it is already in the playbook
>
> **A field leader who has personally made a sale on Wayroo before her account launches is the most persuasive asset available at that launch.** Playbook step 0.4 says exactly this — *"the highest-leverage step in the entire playbook."*
>
> **Jordan Essentials launched without it and still hit 29.2%.** With it, the number should be higher — and that is a testable claim at the next account.

# The Amplifier That Does Not Exist Yet

🔴 **Surface "% of your downline transacting on Wayroo" inside the downline dashboard.**

**Today a field leader can see her team's sales. She cannot see who has adopted.** So the mechanism has motive and no means.

**Adding that one number converts a reporting feature into a distribution channel** — it tells her exactly who to talk to, and her commissions tell her why. **Likely a small change to a surface that already exists. Still not built.**

---

# Outreach

**Sequenced, and it never opens with an ask.**

**1 — Identify.** Top 20–30 by downline size at the target account.

**2 — Personal, from Autumn.** She is named positively by reps in survey feedback; that is a real asset and it should be used.

> *"You've got one of the biggest teams at [DSO]. We're turning on the app before everyone else sees it — your inventory's already in there and you can take card payments on your phone. Want first access?"*

**Value first. The ask — telling her team — comes only after she has used it and it worked.**

**3 — Support to a first transaction.** 🔴 **The conversion moment.** A leader who has taken a payment advocates from experience; one who has only downloaded advocates from politeness.

**4 — Give her the number.** Once shipped, her downline-transacting percentage is the ask, and it makes itself.

**5 — Then ask.** *"Would you tell your team? Here's what to say."*

# Measurement

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Leaders activated** | Transacting before the account launch |
| 🔴 **Downline adoption lift** | Adoption among reps under an activated leader **vs. under an unactivated one** |
| **Multiplication factor** | Downline adopters ÷ activated leaders |
| **Time to first transaction** | Leaders vs. general population |

> **The lift comparison is the whole measurement**, and it needs one thing: **tag adopters by upline.** The relationship is already in the back office. Without it the programme runs on faith.

# What Is Not Applicable

| Conventional tactic | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Paid sponsorships** | No payment needed — incentives are structural |
| **Affiliate commissions** | ⚠️ **She already earns on downline volume.** A second incentive would confuse the comp plan and invite DSO objections. |
| **External creators / podcasters** | Reps are not on LinkedIn or in tech media |
| **Disclosure / FTC influencer rules** | No paid relationship. ⚠️ **Would apply immediately if we ever paid** — a reason not to. |
