# The Job Statements

## Primary functional job

> **When** I'm standing at my table and a customer wants to buy,
> **I want to** take her money and hand her the product without fumbling,
> **so I can** close the sale instead of losing it.

**Evidence: HIGH.** Tap-to-Pay drew the strongest reaction of the JE launch, ahead of everything else shown.

## Secondary functional job

> **When** my wholesale order arrives,
> **I want to** have it already counted and ready to sell,
> **so I can** spend Saturday selling instead of Sunday typing.

**Evidence: HIGH.** Cross-validated at four accounts.

## The team-leader job *(R2)*

> **When** I'm between events or at convention,
> **I want to** see how my team is performing without opening a laptop,
> **so I can** coach the right person before they go quiet.

**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Two accounts, two methods — JE observation plus the Paparazzi rank gradient.

## Emotional job

> **When** I ask a customer to pay,
> **I want to** look like a real business rather than someone collecting a favour,
> **so I can** feel — and be treated as — professional.

## Social job

> **When** I recruit or lead,
> **I want to** look like someone who has her operation together,
> **so I can** attract people who want what I have.

> ⚠️ **The social job is under-served and under-exploited.** Wayroo delivers it — branded storefront, branded invoices, a real dashboard — but nothing in the messaging speaks to it, and in direct selling **recruiting is how a rep's income actually compounds.** Worth testing in Phase 6.

# Pains → Pain Relievers

| Pain | Reliever | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Loses the sale when the customer has no cash | **Tap-to-Pay** | 🟢 **Strongest** |
| 🔴 **"Getting started seems too complicated"** | **Pre-loaded data — nothing to set up** | 🟢 **Strong, and now the most important** |
| Types every SKU into a spreadsheet | **Wholesale order arrives pre-loaded** | 🟢 Strong |
| $60–100/month across 6–10 apps | **$0 incremental** | 🟡 Partial — Canva and SMS survive |
| Asking for Venmo feels unprofessional | **Branded invoices with payment links** | 🟢 Strong |
| Can't see her or her team's numbers without a laptop | **Dashboard widgets — no DSO dependency** | 🟢 Strong |
| No records at tax time | ProPay 1099 + transaction record | 🔵 Real, but **she does not feel it** |
| **Can't message her customers** | ❌ **Nothing** | 🔴 **Gap — repeatedly requested** |

> ### 🔴 The pain hierarchy inverted on 2026-08-30, and it changes what to build first
>
> The library treated payment friction as the top pain. **The Paparazzi survey shows a pain that sits *upstream* of all of them: six of seven non-adopters never got far enough to experience any of it.**
>
> **"Getting started seems too complicated" is not one pain among several. It is the gate on every other reliever in this table.** A rep who stalls at setup never reaches Tap-to-Pay, never sees pre-loaded inventory, and never opens a dashboard.
>
> **Consequence for the roadmap: onboarding is not a polish item. It is the first-order product problem.**

# Tension With the Original Value Proposition

**Two places where JTBD analysis disagrees with what the library currently says.**

> ### 1. The hero and the job are not the same thing
>
> **Positioning leads with Tap-to-Pay because it produces the loudest reaction.** That is correct for *acquisition* — it is what makes a rep tap the ad.
>
> **But the job that determines whether she stays is "let me start without a project."** The strongest hook and the strongest job are different, and the library has been treating the hook as if it were the value proposition.
>
> **Resolution — they are sequenced, not competing:** Tap-to-Pay gets her to download. **Nothing-to-set-up is what makes the download count.** Both belong; the second is the one currently under-weighted.

> ### 2. "One app replaces the 6–10 you pay for" overclaims
>
> Wayroo replaces the **selling and record-keeping** half. **Canva, ChatGPT, and SMS survive** — and SMS was requested repeatedly and unprompted.
>
> **The honest claim is a large dent, not a clean sweep**, and the honest version survives contact with a rep who still has a Canva subscription. Softening it costs almost nothing and protects credibility with the skeptical rep, who is the hardest to win and easiest to lose.

# Refined Value Proposition

> ### For an independent seller at a direct selling company who needs to take a payment, know her stock, and see her numbers without running a second business to do it —
>
> ### **Wayroo is the selling app that comes with her back office:** it opens already loaded with her own inventory and her own numbers, and it turns her phone into a card reader.
>
> ### **Unlike Square**, which is free and good but knows nothing about the company she sells for, **Wayroo already knows** — her wholesale order, her stock, her team, and her commissions are the same system.
>
> ### **So there is nothing to set up.** That is the promise, and it is the one that decides whether she is still using it in a month.
