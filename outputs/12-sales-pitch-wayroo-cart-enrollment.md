## 🆕 Our new direction: sell the outcome, not the app

**This pitch is the first under a new sales strategy.** From now on, we don't sell a client one app or one feature at a time. **We start from the goal the client is trying to reach, and bring together the products that get them there.**

**The first goal is helping reps earn more.** Reaching it takes two things working together:

| To help reps earn more, we need… | Delivered by |
|---|---|
| **Reps who can sell the moment a customer wants to buy.** They take payment on the fly (card, Apple Pay or Google Pay) and their inventory is tracked automatically, from wholesale order to sale, with no spreadsheets | **Wayroo** |
| **A smooth, modern shopping experience for customers that matches the DSO's brand and style** | **Shopping Cart V3 + Branding Studio** (and Wayroo's own storefront, which is merging with it) |
| **A path for customers who want to join, with the rep who referred them still credited** | **Enrollment** |

**Why we're changing.** A client who buys one feature compares it with one competitor: Wayroo against Square, or our cart against Shopify. A client who buys an outcome compares us with a stack of separate vendors, and we're the only one that connects every piece to the back office. Selling the outcome is also how reps actually earn more. A rep who can take a payment but whose customer can't reorder on a good storefront still leaves money on the table.

**What this changes for anyone selling:**
- **Open with the client's goal, not our product list.** Discovery is about where their reps lose money, not which features they want.
- **Present the products as parts of one result, never as a menu.** In beat 7, the products connecting is the pitch.
- **For new prospects, aim for both Wayroo and Freedom.** Either one alone is a fine outcome. Both is the goal. **New-prospect pricing isn't set:** free Wayroo is for existing clients only, and a free tier (Essentials + selling tools, with upgrade tiers) is under consideration. Never tell a prospect Wayroo is free, and never mention the beta.
- **Measure success by the outcome:** rep sales, credited online orders and recruits. Not by how many features a client adopted.

> ### How this fits with the older rules
>
> - **"Cart V3 is Freedom ecommerce, not Wayroo. Don't mix them."** Replaced for pitches to a DSO's home office. Products that serve the same goal are sold together.
> - **"The DSO motion and the rep motion never cross."** Still holds. This pitch is for the home office only.
> - **Wayroo-led entry (decided 2026-10-05).** Still holds for a new prospect that isn't in the market for a back office: Wayroo is the way in, and Freedom follows at renewal. For a prospect that is open to a back-office change, offer both.

## The idea that ties it together

A rep makes money in three moments with a customer:

| Moment | What happens | Product | Today's leak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meet** | She sells in person: a party, a vendor table, a coffee | **Wayroo** | Impulse buys get lost. If she can't take the customer's card or phone on the spot, the sale becomes "order it later," and many of those never happen. The ones paid by Square, Venmo or cash never reach corporate. |
| **Shop** | The customer reorders online, or sets up AutoShip | **Cart V3 + Branding Studio** | A dated storefront where every brand change is a services ticket, or a Shopify store that needs three extra bills to work for direct selling |
| **Join** | The customer decides to sell | **Enrollment** | She leaves the shop, finds a form somewhere else and starts over, and some never finish |

**The pitch is that all three moments run on one system, so the rep who meets the customer is the rep who gets credit for the reorder and the recruit.** That's the rep-revenue story. The customer-experience story is the same thing seen from the other side: she pays however she wants, shops a storefront that looks like the brand, and signs up without being sent anywhere else.

**Brand runs through all three moments.** At the table, reps post approved content from the Wayroo media library instead of their own photos. Online, the storefront is styled in the Branding Studio. At signup, enrollment uses the same brand settings. Use this when the buyer cares about brand: *"Your brand is the same at the table, in the shop and at signup."*

## The pitch: ~4½ minutes, spoken (shared core)

Deliver it after 5–10 minutes of discovery. Bracketed lines are where you use what they just told you. **For a new prospect, swap beats 4, 7, 8 and 9 using the table after the pitch.**

> **[1. The frame, 30 sec]**
> "Our goal with you is simple: help your reps earn more. Your reps make money in three moments. When they meet a customer in person. When that customer reorders online. And when that customer decides she wants to sell too. Today each of those happens in a different place, and some of it never reaches you. We've built one connected answer to all three. Let me show you how they fit."
>
> **[2. Their world, 30 sec]**
> "Here's what we usually see. At the table, reps take Square, Venmo and cash. *[You mentioned ___.]* Online, either the storefront is dated and every change is a ticket and a wait, or it's on Shopify, held together with apps and integrations. And when a shopper wants to join, she leaves the shop to find a form, and starts over."
>
> **[3. What it costs, 40 sec]**
> "So three things leak. The first is the impulse buy. A customer at the table wants it *now*. If your rep can't take her payment right there, and today that's mostly a card or a phone, the sale turns into 'I'll send you a link' or 'order it online and it'll ship to you.' Between the table and the checkout, people change their minds. A lot of those sales never happen.
>
> The second is every cash or Venmo sale that does happen, because that customer never shows up on your side. And the third is the shopper who wanted to join but didn't make it to the form. One marketing director put it in four words: *'a click is costly.'*"
>
> *[Pause: "Does that sound like your field?" If not, go back to discovery.]*
>
> **[4. Meet: Wayroo, 45 sec]**
> "The first piece is Wayroo. It's an extension of Revolution, in the palm of your consultant's hands. She takes the customer's card, Apple Pay or Google Pay right there with Tap-to-Pay, no reader, so the impulse buy closes while the customer still wants it. Her inventory loads from her wholesale order and counts down as she sells, so she knows what she can hand over on the spot. For customers who aren't in front of her, she sends an invoice with a payment link. And she sees her sales and rank without logging into anything. It replaces Square and nearly every app she uses to sell. And every sale comes back to you as a record, with the customer attached."
>
> **[5. Shop: Cart V3 + Branding Studio, 50 sec]**
> "The second piece is your storefront, and it comes with the Branding Studio. Your team sets your colours, fonts, layout and pages themselves. They see a live preview, save it as a draft, and publish when it's right. So when your CEO says 'I want it to look like *this*,' the answer stops being 'file a request and wait.' It's the kind of control people go to Shopify for, without the extra Shopify bills: no Shopify Plus license, no back-office integration to build and maintain, and no extra fee on every transaction. *[If they've paid us for customization before: "That's the conversation that costs you around $4,500 today. Here, the everyday version is a setting."]* *[Clients on the old cart: show the storefront and Studio with confidence. Clients who've built their own: keep it short and move on to the joins.]* What doesn't change is the direct-selling logic underneath: AutoShip that keeps a promo when it renews, shopping on behalf of a customer, and every order credited to the right rep. That's the part Shopify doesn't have."
>
> **[6. Join: Enrollment, 40 sec. Slow down here.]**
> "And the third. Your next rep is already on your website. She's shopping. With the new enrollment flow she signs up from the shop, the rep who referred her is attached from the first screen, and she picks her kit right there. It's the same brand you set for the storefront. She's the same person, ten seconds apart. When we showed this to one client's marketing director, he called it *'probably the best thing I've seen so far.'*"
>
> **[7. Why it only works together, 30 sec]**
> "Here's why this is one conversation and not three. One back office sits under all of it. The rep who met the customer at the table gets credit when that customer reorders online, and gets the sponsor credit when she joins. Square doesn't know what your rep ordered from you. Shopify has no concept of a distributor, so on Shopify the moment a shopper decides to sell is the moment she has to leave. Ours is one journey, and the rep stays attached the whole way."
>
> **[8. What's live and what it costs, 30 sec. Say it before they ask.]**
> "Straight answers. Wayroo is live now, and it's free to your reps on Revolution Pro. We earn a small share of the payments they take, so it costs you nothing, and it's the reason your reps buy Pro, which you make margin on. The new cart and Branding Studio are live too. What you just saw is the product, not a mock-up. Enrollment from the shop comes next. It's in development, and I won't give you a date I can't defend."
>
> **[9. Two steps, 20 sec]**
> "So, two steps. First, the one that pays off right away: let's launch Wayroo to your field. Your next convention or field call is the perfect moment. Second, let's plan moving your storefront to the new cart. I'll bring you pricing in writing, and we'll scope the move with your tech lead around your field calendar. When's your next big field event?"

**Why this order:** live before coming, so credibility builds first. Meet → Shop → Join follows one customer's life with a rep, which is also the demo running order. **The one line that closes it is in beat 7.** If you have to cut, cut beat 2, never beat 6 or 7.

## The demo running order: one customer, three moments

Tell it as one story, not three products.

1. **Meet.** At a vendor event, a customer buys from her rep. Tap-to-Pay on the rep's phone, inventory counts down. *(Wayroo, live.)*
2. **Shop.** Next month she reorders from the rep's link on the branded storefront and sets up AutoShip. The order is credited to the same rep. Show the Branding Studio change behind the look, quickly. It proves we meet the standard Shopify set. It doesn't win the deal. *(Cart V3 + Branding Studio, live.)*
3. **Join.** ⭐ **Spend the most time here.** She clicks "become a partner" from the shop, her rep is already attached as sponsor, and she picks her kit. Then say: *"and both of those wear the brand you set once."* *(Enrollment, in development.)*
4. **Back at the office.** All three land in the back office, credited to the same rep.

> **Book 60 minutes, not 30.** At Pomifera, the call ran out of time at exactly the point it got valuable.

## 🆕 What competitors can't do

The single list behind beat 7 and the objections. Lead with the structural points: they're permanent, not a race to ship features.

### Against Shopify: same look, without the three extra bills

The Branding Studio gives a DSO the storefront control people go to Shopify for. **Shopify only works for a DSO if three more things are paid for, and we don't need any of them:**

| What Shopify needs to work for a DSO | Why | With us |
|---|---|---|
| **Shopify Plus.** Public pricing (third-party sources) is ~$2,300–2,500/month, more at high volume | Most DSOs at scale need Plus | No separate storefront license |
| **A back-office integration**, built and then maintained, every time either side changes | Shopify has no commissions, genealogy or rep attribution, so all of it has to be wired in | The cart is native to the back office |
| **Transaction fees.** Shopify adds a surcharge (~0.15–0.25%) on every transaction if the DSO uses its own payment processor, on top of the processor's own fees | It pushes merchants onto Shopify Payments | ✅ **Lower transaction fees: claimable** (Sam, 2026-10-15). **[RATE: numbers to come]** |

**And the things Shopify structurally can't do, at any price:**
- **No concept of a distributor.** On Shopify, becoming a rep is always a hand-off to another system. Ours is enrollment from the shop.
- **Shopping on behalf of a customer is against Shopify's policy.** Ours keeps attribution and commission intact.
- **AutoShip** that keeps a promo or a price change on renewal.

> **The line:** *"The Branding Studio gives you the control people go to Shopify for. Shopify then needs Plus, an integration to your back office, and a fee on every transaction. And it still doesn't know what a distributor is."*

⚠️ **Shop IQ is our partner** for clients fully on Shopify. Never name it, and never criticise the integration it sells. Talk about "an integration someone has to build and maintain."

> **🆕 When the prospect loves Shopify (TLC, 2026-11-05): don't use this argument. Land on the back office instead.**
> Some Shopify DSOs chose it, like it, and credit it with conversion (Shop Pay, the standard cart). Arguing the three bills with them argues with a decision they're happy with. **If they built their own Shopify → back-office middleware, re-point it at Freedom and offer to maintain it.** That removes the bill they hate (the integration) while Shopify keeps selling. Cart V3 becomes the expansion, once we can show conversion at least equal to Shop Pay's.
> **Ask in discovery:** *"Who built the integration between Shopify and your back office, and who maintains it?"* If they built it themselves, this is the path. If a partner built it, Shop IQ rules apply. Pattern: [`shopify-middleware-repoint-intelligence.md`](shopify-middleware-repoint-intelligence.md).

### Against Square, Venmo and the rep's app stack
- Square takes a card well. **It can't load the rep's inventory from her wholesale order, and it can't send the sale or the customer back to the DSO.** Processing costs are roughly the same as Square's. **Never claim we're cheaper.**
- Replaces Square and **nearly** every app a rep uses to sell and create content. Reps spend $60–100 a month across 6–10 of them.

### Against building your own storefront
*"The clients who build their own usually aren't wrong about the problem. They're right, and they stopped believing we'd fix it. What gets underestimated is that a storefront isn't the hard part. The hard part is AutoShip that catches a promo on renewal, and attribution that still resolves to the right rep nine months later. That's the part you rebuild every time your back office changes, and you'd be building it alone."* (Say it as an observation, never as a warning.)

### Against Exigo (new prospects)
**Never claim Exigo has no rep app. It does.** The difference is scope: Wayroo is where the rep actually sells and runs her business (payments, inventory, invoicing), connected to the back office. Ask what their reps use Exigo's app for, and whether they still use Square and Venmo.

## Objection handling (acknowledge → explore → respond)

| Objection | Explore | Respond |
|---|---|---|
| **"That's three projects."** | "Which one worries you?" | "It's one goal, and the first step is the easiest. Wayroo is live, and launching it to your field is the first step. The new cart is live, and we plan the move around your calendar. Enrollment joins it when it's ready." |

## Claim guardrails

| ✅ Safe to say | 🚫 Don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Wherever your customer buys, the sale lands with the rep who earned it" | "One customer record across all three" | Not verified, and not needed for the pitch |
| "Wayroo is live today" · "Tap-to-Pay, invoicing, inventory from her wholesale order" | Dropship · Aice features | Unshipped or unresolved |
| "Takes the customer's card, Apple Pay or Google Pay on the spot" · "closes the impulse buy" | "Takes any form of payment" | Tap-to-Pay covers contactless cards and Apple Pay / Google Pay. It doesn't take Venmo, and recording cash sales isn't confirmed |
| "The new cart and Branding Studio are live" · "colours, fonts, layout and pages, live preview, draft → publish" | "Unlimited customization" · a migration timeline before scoping | Bespoke work is still a services engagement |
| "Every rep gets her own Wayroo storefront" · "we're bringing them together into one" | A date for the merge · Cart V3 or Branding Studio as part of a Wayroo-only deal | The Wayroo storefront is the rep's. The company storefront is Cart V3. The merge has no date |
| "The control people go to Shopify for, without Plus, a back-office integration to maintain, or Shopify's higher transaction fees" | "Huge integration fees" · a fee percentage before Sam's numbers arrive | Our own partner's Shopify integration costs $2,500–5,000 one-time, so "huge" won't survive a client who has priced it. **Lower transaction fees are claimable; quote [RATE] once confirmed** |
| "Shopify Plus runs about $2,300–2,500 a month" *(when asked)* | Quoting Shopify's fees as exact | From third-party sources, not Shopify's own page. Their contract may differ |
| "Shopify has no concept of a distributor; becoming a rep is always a hand-off" | "Shopify can't do enrollment" · "at any price, through any app" | Apps can bolt something on. The hand-off is the claim that holds up |
| "Enrollment is next, in development" · "Enroll **from** the shop" | A date · "without leaving the cart" · "enrollment takes payment" | No defensible date for PL-1. The payment hand-off isn't built |
| "Your storefront, your brand, changed in a setting" | Any speed or performance number | No baseline exists |
| "Replaces Square and nearly every app a rep uses to sell" | "Every app" · "cheaper than Square" | Price is roughly level. Never claim cheaper |
| "Run both, and Wayroo is already connected" *(new prospects)* | "Free," "free tier," or the beta, to any prospect | Free Wayroo is for existing clients only. Prospect pricing isn't set |
| Demo quotes, verbally, attributed by role | Naming Nuvi Global, Pomifera or Pure Haven · quotes on slides or in email | Never name another client. Get permission before anything goes in writing |
| Rep search by name, as a **question** | Rep search by name, as a **feature** | Not built anywhere |
| "All-in-one: back office and field, from one vendor" *(new prospects buying both)* | "All-in-one" to existing clients or Wayroo-only prospects | Only true when they buy both |
| — | FTC or compliance as the opener | Message-tested failure. A late supporting point for Finance at most |
| — | Single-domain / framed cart | V3 records it as a gap, unresolved |
| — | Naming or criticising Shop IQ | Partner |
