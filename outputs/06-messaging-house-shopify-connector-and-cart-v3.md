## Part 3 — Messaging Pillars

Three per motion. **Three is the limit — more than three and nothing sticks.**

### Connector

#### Pillar C1 — One company is accountable for the whole path ✅ *ships today*

**Headline:** *When your storefront and your back office are two vendors and the connector is a third, nobody owns the failure.*

**Supporting points:**
- The connector maps your catalog, orders, customers and commissions — the highest-consequence data in the business — and it is the one piece no vendor in the chain fully owns.
- ByDesign owns the compensation engine the data lands in. A connector built by the same company removes the handoff at the exact point failures happen.
- Nothing customer-facing changes. Same Shopify storefront, same theme, same checkout.

**Objection it addresses:** *"It works fine — why change something in my live revenue path?"*

#### Pillar C2 — Your revenue path should not depend on a vendor you cannot see ⛔ *BLOCKED — items 2 & 7*

**Headline:** *A dependency you have no visibility into and no contract with is a risk you cannot manage — regardless of how well it currently works.*

**Supporting points:**
- The connector carrying your orders was built by a party you have no agreement with and whose commercial standing you have no way to assess.
- ByDesign has no contractual ability to support, fix or guarantee software we did not write.
- Moving to a first-party connector puts the dependency inside a relationship you already hold.

**Objection it addresses:** *"Nothing is broken."*

> 🔴 **Do not use until both gates clear.** See the ShapeTech handling rules in Part 6 — including the rule that **the general form above is usable without naming ShapeTech at all**, and the specific delisting fact is not.

#### Pillar C3 — This is core product now, not a partner favor ✅ *ships today*

**Headline:** *We build and support the Shopify connection ourselves because that is where demand in this industry is going.*

**Supporting points:**
- Search demand for Shopify in direct selling grew **3.5× in three years** while the core category stayed flat.
- Our Shopify page is our **most-read ecommerce page** — ahead of both its siblings on views, users and time on page — despite sitting two levels deep in the nav.
- Shopify support is on the product roadmap as core product, not delegated.

**Objection it addresses:** *"Are you going to maintain this, or is it a one-off?"* — the question a client must answer 'yes' to before depending on us.

⚠️ **Present the 3.5× against flat, never the absolute volume.** The whole cluster is 320 searches/month; quoting it invites the obvious rebuttal. The shape of the trend is the argument.

---

### Shopping Cart v3

#### Pillar V1 — Autoship that follows your rules ✅ *ships today*

**Headline:** *Your subscription program should apply your promotions and your prices. On a retail platform it doesn't, and it fails quietly.*

**Supporting points:**
- Retail auto-renewal is catch-all — every item is renewable, with no per-item eligibility.
- Promotions are not reliably captured and price changes are not reliably applied. **This is a margin leak on every cycle, and it is silent.**
- Autoship-specific pricing, scheduling and promotions are configurable, and your field can manage customer autoships without calling support.
- Third-party auto-renewal plugins have been tried by clients and did not close the gap.

**Objection it addresses:** *"An app can do subscriptions."*

**Why it leads:** the client named it directly — *"the best edge our cart has over Shopify"* — and ranked it the biggest Shopify pain point after cost.

⚠️ **Claim boundary:** the source frames this as *"less of a rave review for our AutoShip … more a criticism of Shopify."* **Claim the comparative advantage. Never claim a client praised our AutoShip on its own merits.**

#### Pillar V2 — Built on the three-party model your business actually runs on ✅ *ships today*

**Headline:** *A retail platform models a brand selling to a shopper. You have a company, a rep, and the rep's customer — and that third party changes everything.*

**Supporting points:**
- **Reps can place and service orders for their customers.** On Shopify this is not a workaround problem — **the platform disallows it.** Clients who need it keep our cart for exactly that, even after moving everything else.
- Commissionable volume, rank qualification and party/host logic resolve at checkout, where the comp plan lives — not in reconciliation afterwards.
- The alternative is building it: **one DSO built their own commission engine on top of Shopify, and it was part of why they left their previous platform.**

**Objection it addresses:** *"Shopify is the better ecommerce platform."* — Correct, for retail. This is not retail.

**This is the pillar that cannot be competed away.** Every other advantage is a race. This one is a policy on someone else's platform.

#### Pillar V3 — You control your own storefront ✅ *3a ships today* · ⛔ *3b BLOCKED — item 3*

**Headline:** *Change your storefront and your enrollment experience yourself, the day you decide to.*

**Supporting points — 3a, ships:**
- **Branding Studio:** layout, fonts, color, video and imagery, across **both the cart and the rep enrollment experience.**
- Configured by you. **No ticket, no third party, no quote.**
- This is the direct answer to the most-cited reason clients left the cart.

**Supporting point — 3b, ⛔ BLOCKED:**
- ~~You stop paying a platform subscription *and* the category's highest processing rate.~~ **Do not use until the TCO model exists (open item 3). Do not improvise percentages.**

**Objection it addresses:** *"Your cart couldn't be customized without calling someone."* — the #1 recorded complaint.

> 🔴 **V3 is a de-blocker, not a differentiator.** It stops us being disqualified. It is not a reason to switch — **Shopify has more customization and always will.** Never lead with it. Use it to answer the objection, then return to V1 and V2.

## Part 5 — Objection Handling

**Format: Acknowledge → Explore → Respond.**

### Connector

#### "It works fine. Why would I touch my live revenue path?"

- **Acknowledge:** "That's the right instinct. Nobody should change a working order path without a reason."
- **Explore:** "When an order doesn't land or a commission looks wrong, what does figuring out why look like today?"
- **Respond:** "That's the reason. Not that it's broken — that when it does break, you're coordinating three companies to find out whose problem it is, and we can't fix code we didn't write. This puts the whole path with one vendor. Your storefront doesn't change; the pipe does."

#### 🔴 "Are you doing this so you can move us off Shopify later?"

**The sharpest objection in the motion. It will be asked, and it deserves a straight answer — a deflection here costs the relationship, not just the deal.**

- **Acknowledge:** "Fair question, and yes — we'd like you on our cart one day."
- **Explore:** "Is the concern that this is a lock-in step?"
- **Respond:** "When our cart is genuinely better for you, we'll make that case on its merits and you'll be free to say no. That's a separate conversation and it isn't this one. Right now your orders run through a connector we don't control and can't support. That's worth fixing whether or not you ever leave Shopify — and if you never do, this still stands on its own."

> **Why this answer:** it is true, it concedes the intent openly, and it separates the two decisions. **Denying that we'd like them on our cart is a lie the client will eventually catch, and it makes every later cart conversation look like the trap they suspected.**

#### "What's the migration effort?"

- **Acknowledge:** "It's real work and I won't pretend otherwise."
- **Explore:** "What's your current sync doing beyond catalog and orders — any customisation on top?"
- **Respond:** "The storefront, theme and checkout are untouched. What moves is the sync layer. Let us scope it against your actual configuration and give you a real number — and if it isn't worth it on your setup, we'll say so."

#### "What does it cost?"

⚠️ **Commercial terms for the connector are not defined in this messaging system.** Do not improvise. Route to the account owner. **Flag: this needs an answer before Segment A outreach opens.**

### Shopping Cart v3

#### 🔴 "Your cart was slow and unstable. That's why we left."

**The hardest objection, and the one most likely to be asked by the accounts that matter most.**

- **Acknowledge:** "You're right, and I'm not going to argue with it. We worked on it while you were on the cart and we didn't get it to where you needed it."
- **Explore:** "Where did it hurt most — the field's experience, or checkout under load?"
- **Respond — *only what is true today*:** "I'm not going to ask you to take performance on faith, because you've heard that before. When we have real numbers against the old baseline, I'll bring them to you and you can judge. Until then I'd rather talk about the things that are true right now — autoship, and reps ordering for their customers."

> 🔴 **The rule, absolute: never make a performance claim to this audience without a published benchmark.**
>
> **Volunteering the limitation is the strongest available move.** These clients have been promised performance before and watched it not arrive. A rep who says *"I won't claim that yet"* is the only kind of rep they will believe when the numbers do exist.
>
> ❌ "v3 is much faster." ❌ "We've completely rebuilt performance." ❌ "That won't be an issue anymore."
> ✅ "I'm not claiming that until I can show you numbers."

#### "Customizing meant filing a ticket with a third party."

- **Acknowledge:** "Yes. That was the most common complaint we had."
- **Explore:** "What would you want to change yourself, and how often?"
- **Respond:** "That's what Branding Studio is — layout, fonts, colors, video, imagery, across the cart and the rep enrollment experience, configured by you. No ticket, no third party." *(Give an honest date. Do not imply it is available if it is not.)*

#### "Shopify just looks better."

- **Acknowledge:** "It does. They're the best retail storefront in the world and we're not going to out-design them."
- **Explore:** "How much of your revenue is a customer browsing a storefront, versus a rep serving a customer they already have?"
- **Respond:** "That's the split that matters. Shopify is built for a brand selling to a shopper. You have a company, a rep, and the rep's customer. Your reps aren't permitted to place an order for a customer on Shopify — that's a platform policy, not a gap they'll close. And autoship treats every item as renewable and misses your promotions. Those aren't design problems."

> **Never defend the UX.** Conceding it is what makes the reframe credible.

#### "We just moved to Shopify. We're not moving again."

- **Acknowledge:** "Then you shouldn't. Two migrations in two years is a bad trade for anyone."
- **Explore:** "Is there anything you've had to build around or work around since the move?"
- **Respond:** "The version of this that usually makes sense isn't a move at all — keep Shopify where it's working and run the flows it can't handle where they work properly. That's what other clients have landed on, and it's a smaller decision than the one you're bracing for."

#### "An app handles subscriptions."

- **Acknowledge:** "There are good ones."
- **Explore:** "Does yours handle per-item autoship eligibility, and does it pick up your promotions and price changes?"
- **Respond:** "That's where they tend to stop. Retail auto-renewal makes everything renewable and doesn't reliably catch a promo or a price change — which is a silent margin leak, not a visible failure. Clients have tried the plugins; it didn't close it."

### The provenance problem — read carefully

**The source document does not name ShapeTech.** Drew's data review says only *"a partner removed from the preferred partner marketplace in June 2026 for non-payment."* The identification came from internal conversation on 2026-08-27 and **is not independently sourced anywhere in this repo.**

> 🔴 **Confirm the identification and the underlying fact independently before either is stated to a client.** Repeating an unverified claim about a named company's finances is the one failure mode here with consequences beyond a lost deal.

### The frame: risk transfer, not FUD

**The argument is about a dependency the client cannot see or manage. It is not about ShapeTech's viability, and we do not benefit from making it about that.**

A client who thinks we are running a smear campaign discounts everything else we say. A client who concludes on their own that an unmanaged dependency is worth removing acts without being pushed. **Give them the structure and let them draw the conclusion.**

### Approved vs. prohibited phrasings

| ✅ Defensible | ❌ Never say |
|---|---|
| "Your order path depends on software from a party you don't hold a contract with." | "ShapeTech is in financial trouble." |
| "We can't support or guarantee code we didn't write." | "They might go under and take your store with them." |
| "That's a dependency you have no visibility into and no way to manage." | "You should be worried about them." |
| "Our recommendation is that a first-party connector belongs inside a relationship you already hold." | "They're not paying their bills." |
| *(post-legal only)* A factual, legal-approved statement of the marketplace status. | Any speculation about cause, finances, or what happens next. |
| "Whatever you decide about the connector, it's worth knowing where that dependency sits." | Any characterisation of their product quality. |

### Four standing rules

1. **Never put it in writing before legal signs off.** No email, no deck, no leave-behind. Verbal, in context, only.
2. **Never volunteer it as the opener.** Lead with C1 (accountability). Vendor risk is the *second* thing said, if the client asks why it matters now.
3. **Never attack the product.** Their connector's quality is not the argument and attacking it invites a comparison we have not earned yet.
4. **If the client defends them, stop.** Some clients have a direct relationship. *"That's fair — the point isn't about them, it's about where the dependency sits."* Then return to accountability.

### What to use while the gates are closed

**Pillar C2's general form needs no gate and names nobody:**

> "The connector between your storefront and your back office is the one piece nobody in the chain fully owns. We can't support code we didn't write, and you don't hold a contract with whoever did."

**This is defensible today, requires no legal review, and is most of the argument.** The specific delisting fact sharpens it. It does not carry it.

## Part 9 — Words We Use / Words We Avoid

### Product and category

| ✅ We say | ❌ We don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "the ByDesign Shopify connector" | "our integration platform" / "our Shopify solution" | Platform language invites comparison to Shopify's app marketplace. The value is whose it is. |
| "first-party" | "native" | "Native" is vague and every vendor claims it. "First-party" states the actual fact. |
| "the cart that knows your compensation plan" | "our Shopify" / "Shopify for MLM" | 🔴 The single most damaging framing available. Puts us on Shopify's evaluation axes. |
| "direct selling" | "MLM" | Client-facing standard. **"MLM" stays in SEO and internal analysis** — it is the search term. |
| "shop on behalf of" / "rep-assisted ordering" | "impersonation" / "admin order entry" | The client's own words. Use them. |
| "three-party model" | "B2B2C" | Concrete beats jargon with this audience. |
| "Branding Studio" | "the new theme editor" | It covers cart *and* rep enrollment. "Theme editor" undersells it. |

### Competitive

| ✅ We say | ❌ We don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Shopify is the best retail storefront in the world" | "Shopify is bad" | It isn't, everyone knows it isn't, and conceding buys the reframe. |
| "built for retail, not for selling through reps" | "not built for enterprise" | Precise and true. The other is false. |
| "Shopify disallows this" | "Shopify can't do this" | **Prohibited ≠ missing.** The prohibition is the whole point — it is permanent. |
| "keep Shopify where it's working" | "rip and replace" | Split-stack converts. All-or-nothing doesn't. |
| "a dependency you can't see or manage" | anything about a third party's finances | See Part 6. |

### Voice

| ✅ We say | ❌ We don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "3.5× in three years while the category stayed flat" | "explosive growth" | §10: specific numbers over vague claims. |
| "I'm not claiming that until I can show you numbers" | "significantly improved" | 🔴 The only credible register with clients who left over performance. |
| "here's what's new" *(Segment E)* | "here's what we fixed" | Tells a content client they had a problem. See the Segment E trap. |
| "let us scope it and we'll tell you if it isn't worth it" | "seamless migration" | "Seamless" is the word that ends credibility with an operator. |
| — | "revolutionary" · "game-changing" · "best-in-class" · "cutting-edge" · "unlock" · "empower" | §10: no hype words. |
