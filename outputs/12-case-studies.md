# Case Study 1 — Paparazzi 🔴 The flagship

## Snapshot
**Paparazzi** · jewellery · **~20,000 reps** · US · ByDesign client running Wayroo white-labeled as **Premiere**.

## Challenge
Reps carried their own inventory and sold at events, parties, and markets — and ran the whole operation on tools they bought themselves. Square for payments, spreadsheets for stock, Venmo when Square wasn't handy. **None of it connected to the company they sold for**, so reps entered their wholesale orders by hand and corporate could see none of the resulting sales.

## Why Wayroo
Wayroo runs on the same system as the back office, so **a rep's wholesale order and inventory arrive already loaded.** Nothing to import, nothing to set up. Reps also get their own ProPay merchant account — the **Pink Card** — so payments land in their own account rather than routing through corporate.

## Solution
Premiere gives Paparazzi reps inventory that fills itself from wholesale orders, an online store, invoicing, and payments. **Autumn Fowers runs live training at Break Through Convention each year** — a session reps voluntarily return to, some for a third year.

## Results

| | |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Rep sales through Wayroo, 2025** | **$31M** |
| **Year-over-year growth** | **60%+** |
| **Reps on the platform** | ~4,000 |
| **GMV per rep per year** | **~$7,750** |
| **Satisfaction** *(50 surveyed, Aug 2026)* | **4.5 / 5 — 90% rate it 4 or 5** |

## Pull quote
> *"Thank you all for providing such valuable information to help me run my business more efficiently."* — Paparazzi consultant, Break Through Convention 2026

# Case Study 2 — Jordan Essentials

## Snapshot
**Jordan Essentials** · personal care · **~2,500 active reps** · US · existing ByDesign Freedom client that added Wayroo.

## Challenge
Six months of declining rep sales and engagement. More reps sold in person than leadership realised, and those reps were on **Square, Venmo, and PayPal** — with corporate seeing none of it. Reps also had to log into the back office after every sale to manage inventory, or keep spreadsheets.

## Why Wayroo
An existing client adding a rep-facing layer to a back office they already ran. **No new platform, no migration.** Leadership chose their annual convention as the launch moment.

## Solution
Wayroo launched to the field at convention, July 17–18 2026. Reps got inventory pre-loaded from wholesale orders, their back-office numbers on their phones, and a media library.

## Results

| | |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Downloads in the launch window** | **764 — within 2–3 days** |
| **Adoption rate** | **~29.2%** of active reps |
| **Deal cycle** | **~11 weeks** vs. a 4–6 month standard |
| **Commercial signal** | 🔴 **The founder asked to start paying early**, cutting short her own agreed free period |

## Pull quote
> *[Pending — capture from Nancy or Hope while enthusiasm is high. Repeatedly flagged, never done.]*

## 🔴 What this case study cannot claim

**Do not write "Wayroo reversed the decline."** The deal was sold on reversing a six-month decline and **no pre-launch baseline was captured**, so the recovery claim cannot be evidenced. **Only adoption can.**

**Do not say "and still growing."** All 764 arrived in 2–3 days and **no post-launch measurement exists.**

**Also unsaid:** selling tools were dark at launch, and 510 of the 764 lack the Revolution Pro subscription Wayroo requires. **Neither belongs in a customer-facing asset — both belong in how the next launch is run.**

> **The adoption story is publishable today. The recovery story is not.** Publishing the first while implying the second is the way this asset damages trust rather than building it.

# Customer Interview Questions

**For the quotes both case studies lack. 20 minutes, conversational.**

1. *"What did running a rep business look like before Wayroo?"*
2. *"What made you decide to bring it to your field?"*
3. *"What did you consider instead?"*
4. *"Walk me through your reps' reaction."*
5. *"What surprised you?"*
6. *"What can you see now that you couldn't before?"*
7. *"What numbers changed?"*
8. 🔴 *"If a peer at another company asked whether it was worth it, what would you say?"* — **the pull quote question**
9. *"What would you tell us to do better?"*
10. *"Would you be comfortable if we shared this?"*

**Ask for a 60-second phone video alongside the written quote.** A founder saying *"my reps did $31M on this"* to camera outperforms any written asset, and an iPhone selfie video is enough.
