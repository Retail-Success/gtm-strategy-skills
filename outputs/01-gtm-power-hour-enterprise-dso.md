## The Core Thesis (One Paragraph)

At mid-market, FTC compliance is a late-stage close move, and `strategic-inputs.md` §3 correctly demotes it below the POS/ProPay rep-experience pitch. Jordan Essentials confirmed this: do not lead with compliance at a 2,000-rep DSO. **At enterprise scale the argument inverts, because the liability scales with headcount and revenue while the remedy does not.** A contractor-reclassification finding against a 2,000-rep DSO is expensive. The same finding against a 300,000-consultant, $3B DSO is an extinction-level payroll-tax and benefits event, and it lands on the CFO and General Counsel, not the VP of Sales. Wayroo Dropship plus individual ProPay merchant accounts is the only structural fix in the category, and Rallyware, Exigo's rep app, and Shopify each structurally cannot offer it (none of them make the rep the merchant of record). That gives us a compelling event, an economic buyer with budget authority, and a differentiator no incumbent can close in a quarter. We do not enter Tier 1 by displacing anyone. We enter by serving the cohort and the risk their incumbent cannot touch.

## The Two Motions (Run in Parallel)

Per the decision to run two motions rather than one sequenced play:

### Motion A — Wayroo Cohort Wedge (overlay, incumbent stays)

**Target:** Enterprise DSOs with an entrenched back office (Exigo, SAP, custom-built) that they will not replace. This is most of E1.

**Deal shape:** Wayroo-only, no Freedom pitch. This is the **Color Street deal shape already proven in your pipeline** (`prospects/color-street.md`: left Exigo, custom commission engine, Shopify stack, Wayroo-only, Path B integration endorsed by their CTO). We have run this play. It works.

**The wedge:** Land the cash-and-carry / event-selling cohort inside the enterprise DSO, not the whole rep base. Every Tier 1 DSO has one. Mary Kay consultants carry inventory. Scentsy consultants carry testers and stock. Thirty-One and Pampered Chef run party-plan inventory models. Rallyware does not serve that cohort (zero commerce primitives, confirmed in `02-competitor-analysis-rallyware.md`). Exigo's rep app is CRM-tier, not ERP-tier (research still open, see §7). Shopify cannot make the rep the merchant.

**Expansion path:** Cohort pilot (2K–5K reps) → full C&C cohort → all reps → Freedom conversation at the next back-office renewal.

**Why this wins:** It is not a rip-and-replace. Nobody has to lose. The CIO keeps their platform, the CFO gets the liability fix, the reps get the tool, and we get a foothold inside a $1B account with ERP-tier switching costs.

### Motion B — Freedom + Wayroo Full Platform (migration window)

**Target:** Enterprise DSOs already in motion. Post-Exigo departures, Party Platform Solutions price-hike refugees at scale, Thatcher bankruptcy fallout, and any DSO whose back-office contract is inside 12 months of renewal.

**Deal shape:** Full platform replacement, highest ACV, longest cycle.

**Why we can credibly run this at enterprise scale (assets already in hand):**

| Proof point | Why it matters to E1 procurement |
|---|---|
| 25 years in the industry | Survives the "will you exist in 5 years" question |
| $7B+ in bonus payouts processed | Answers scale doubt with a number, not an adjective |
| 36,000+ bonus runs executed | Operational reliability at volume |
| 2,700+ compensation plans built | Any comp plan they have, we have built something like it |
| **185 countries, 74 languages** | The single most underused asset in the deck. Every Tier 1 DSO is multinational. |
| **"Companies scaled from startup to $1B+ on the platform"** | Direct rebuttal to "you are a mid-market vendor" |
| DSA Partnership Award, DSN Premier Supplier 2026 | Industry-body validation that procurement recognizes |

**Critical asymmetry to manage:** Freedom is enterprise-ready and global (185 countries, 74 languages). **Wayroo is US-only** (`my-gtm-context.md` §2, §9: "No international expansion in 2026"). At E1, every account is multinational. See §11, this is the largest single gap in the strategy.

## Element 1: Target Customer

**Confidence: MEDIUM** (E2), **LOW** (E1, zero primary research)

### The Decision-Making Unit changes completely at enterprise

This is the most important operational difference from the mid-market motion. Mid-market DSO DMU is one or two aligned Economic Buyers (Nancy + Hope at Jordan Essentials, Brian + Edmond at Color Street). At E1 the DMU is six to nine people across four functions, and **the VP of Sales is no longer the Economic Buyer.**

| Role | Title | What they care about | Our entry argument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Economic Buyer** | CFO | Processing cost, liability exposure, contractor classification risk | Spendback (2.55% → 1% on wholesale), Dropship (3% off retail), reclassification cost avoidance |
| **Risk Buyer (new)** | General Counsel / Chief Compliance Officer | FTC retail-sales documentation, contractor classification, state-level payment rules (24+ states) | Three-pillar compliance argument. **This is our door.** |
| **Technical Gatekeeper** | CIO / CTO / VP Engineering | Integration architecture, security posture, data residency, does this destabilize the back office | Path B integration (Color Street CTO endorsed), API gateway, coexistence not replacement |
| **User Champion** | VP Field Ops / VP Sales | Rep activation, retention, admin time, field adoption | Admin-time-to-selling-time (4-account validated), rep dashboards, Paparazzi outcome |
| **Blocker** | Head of Procurement / Vendor Management | SOC 2, SLA, insurance, MSA terms, vendor concentration | Readiness pack (§11). Nothing else works here. |
| **Sponsor** | President / COO | Does this move the growth number | $31M / 60% YoY Paparazzi proof |

**Implication for Daniel:** the mid-market playbook (`outputs/12-sales-call-script.md`) opens with the VP of Sales. At enterprise that is a champion-level conversation, not a buying conversation. Enterprise outbound must open with **CFO or General Counsel**, and the compliance argument is the opener, not the closer. This is a direct reversal of `strategic-inputs.md` §3 lead-differentiator hierarchy, and it applies **only** at enterprise.

### Qualification questions (enterprise-specific, additive to `02-hybrid-dso-qualification-questions.md`)

1. "What percentage of your consultants carry personal inventory?" (existing question, sizes the Motion A wedge)
2. "Of your active consultants, how many are actually selling to customers versus buying at discount for personal use?" (real-seller denominator, existing)
3. **"When your consultants sell to an end customer, whose merchant account processes that transaction?"** (the reclassification qualifier. If the answer is "ours," they are exposed.)
4. **"Has outside counsel reviewed your contractor classification posture in the last 24 months?"** (surfaces whether the risk is already on their radar)
5. **"What is your back-office contract renewal date?"** (routes to Motion A or Motion B)
6. "How many countries do your consultants operate in, and what share of rep sales volume is US?" (sizes the Wayroo international gap)
7. "Who owns the decision on rep-facing tooling, and does it go through procurement?" (maps the DMU, sets the cycle length)

## Element 2: Problem

**Confidence: HIGH** on problem existence, **LOW** on enterprise severity ranking (no primary research)

The mid-market problem set in `my-gtm-context.md` §4 still applies, but the **ranking inverts** at enterprise. Same problems, different order, different owner.

| Problem | Mid-market severity | Enterprise severity | Why it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FTC contractor reclassification exposure** | 13 (late-stage argument) | **15, board-level** | Liability scales with headcount and revenue. At 100K+ consultants, reclassification means payroll taxes, benefits, and worker protections retroactively. This is not a line item, it is a solvency question. |
| **Processing cost on rep transactions** | 13 | **15, CFO-level** | 3% on retail plus 2.55% on wholesale across billions in volume. At $1B revenue, Spendback alone (2.55% → 1%) is an eight-figure annual line. The deal funds itself. |
| **No auditable retail transaction record** | 15 | 15 | Unchanged, universal, and worse at scale |
| **Vendor concentration / platform risk** | Not present | **14, new at enterprise** | Enterprise procurement actively fears single-vendor dependency. Cuts both ways: helps Motion A (we are additive), hurts Motion B (we are a big bet). |
| **Rep activation and turnover** | 13 | 11 | Still real, but at $1B scale it is a field-ops KPI, not a CFO problem. Demote as an opener. |
| **Admin time vs. selling time** | 14 (4-account validated) | 12 | Strongest mid-market message. At enterprise it is a champion-building message, not a buying message. |
| **Corporate cannot market to its own customers** | 14 | **14** | Holds. At 100K reps the invisible customer base is a very large marketing asset. |

**The enterprise headline problem, stated in buyer language:**

> "Your consultants sell to end customers. Your merchant account processes those payments. Your corporate entity carries the chargebacks. Under the FTC's working test, that is three out of three indicators that your independent contractors are functioning as employees. At your headcount, that exposure is not a compliance line item."

## Element 3: Solution

**Confidence: HIGH** (the product genuinely does this)

**Motion A (Wayroo overlay), one sentence:** Wayroo gives every consultant their own ProPay merchant account, their own POS, their own inventory, and their own branded storefront, so the consultant becomes the merchant of record on every retail transaction, sitting on top of the back office you already run.

**Motion B (full platform), one sentence:** Freedom plus Wayroo replaces the back office and the rep layer as one natively integrated system, with a compensation engine that has processed $7B in payouts across 185 countries and a rep app no back-office vendor can match.

**The three mechanisms that carry the enterprise argument:**

1. **Dropship + individual ProPay merchant accounts.** Rep is merchant of record, owns the transaction, owns the chargeback, owns the customer relationship. All three FTC contractor tests satisfied simultaneously. No competitor equivalent, and per `my-gtm-context.md` §6 this is not a feature gap closable in a quarter (it requires merchant infrastructure, payment routing, and compliance logic).
2. **Spendback.** Rep wholesale order payments route through the rep's ProPay balance, dropping DSO wholesale processing from 2.55%+ to 1%. Three-account validated (Paparazzi quantified, Nancy "big benefit," Edmond asked). **At enterprise volume this is the CFO opener.**
3. **Customer auto-creation into the back office.** Rep creates a customer in Wayroo, record auto-creates in Freedom. Cross-account validated, called a "huge benefit" unprompted by both Nancy (Jordan Essentials) and Edmond (Color Street).

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** (mechanisms validated at mid-market, dollar framing untested at enterprise)

**For the CFO (Economic Buyer):**
> Wayroo pays for itself out of your processing line before it touches your growth line. Spendback drops wholesale processing from 2.55% to 1%. Dropship moves retail processing and chargeback liability off your balance sheet and onto individual consultant merchant accounts. At your volume, the savings fund the deployment.

**For the General Counsel (Risk Buyer):**
> Every consultant becomes their own merchant of record, with their own account, their own chargeback responsibility, and their own transaction ownership. That is three of three on the FTC's contractor test, satisfied structurally rather than by policy. Every retail sale produces an auditable customer record. You get the documentation mandate and the classification mandate solved by the same infrastructure.

**For the CIO (Technical Gatekeeper):**
> You keep your back office. Wayroo integrates through a documented API path that a peer CTO has already reviewed and endorsed. We are additive to your stack, not a migration.

**For the VP Field Ops (Champion):**
> One app replaces the six your consultants are paying for separately. Four thousand Paparazzi reps did $31M in 2025, up more than 60% year over year, on this.

**Positioning statement (enterprise variant, adapting `06-positioning-master.md` Step 5):**

> **For** enterprise direct selling organizations (10,000+ consultants, $100M+ revenue) **who** carry FTC contractor-classification and retail-documentation exposure on every consultant transaction processed through a corporate merchant account, and who absorb processing fees across billions in rep sales volume,
> **Wayroo** is the rep commerce infrastructure layer that makes every consultant their own merchant of record.
> **Unlike** Rallyware (sales enablement, does not transact, cannot create a merchant record), Exigo (back office with a CRM-tier rep app, no per-rep merchant accounts), or Shopify (single corporate merchant account, 3% on every transaction, full chargeback liability retained by corporate),
> **Wayroo** shifts the transaction, the fee, and the liability to the individual consultant, on top of the back office you already run.

Note: category frame stays "native end-to-end commerce platform for direct selling" per `06-positioning-master.md`. Do not introduce a new category for enterprise. Introduce a new **buyer** and a new **argument** within the same category.

## Element 5: Pricing

**Confidence: LOW. This is a live problem, flagged as a finding.**

### Finding: the published Enterprise tier does not survive contact with E1

`my-gtm-context.md` §5 defines Enterprise as **$10,000+/mo with ByDesign and Wayroo users included.**

| Account size | Enterprise tier at $10K/mo | Effective per-rep cost | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 reps (E2) | $120K/yr | $1.00/rep/mo | Reasonable |
| 50,000 reps (E2 top) | $120K/yr | $0.20/rep/mo | Underpriced |
| 300,000 consultants (E1) | $120K/yr | **$0.03/rep/mo** | **Structurally broken** |

Meanwhile Rallyware sells $150K–$1M+ into these same accounts for a product with **zero commerce primitives** (`02-competitor-analysis-rallyware.md` §Pricing). We would be entering the most demanding accounts in the category at a fraction of the price of an adjacent-category vendor with less product surface. That is not a competitive advantage, it reads as a credibility problem in an enterprise procurement review.

### Recommended enterprise pricing construct (requires Morgan alignment per `strategic-inputs.md` §9)

Do not extend the existing tier table. Build a separate enterprise construct on three lines:

1. **Platform fee**, floor $250K/yr, scaling with rep count band. Sets the credibility anchor and covers deployment.
2. **Per-active-rep**, billed on the **real-seller cohort, not nominal consultants.** This is critical and already documented: Color Street disclosed an 80/20 hobbyist/seller split, and `strategic-inputs.md` §5 warns nominal-rep math overestimates DSO cost by 5x. At E1 the ratio may be worse. Always qualify the denominator before quoting.
3. **Transaction rev-share**, which becomes the dominant revenue line at enterprise volume and is the mechanism that makes the North Star (rep sales volume) and the contract align.

Keep the two philosophy anchors from `strategic-inputs.md` §5 intact, they work even better at enterprise:
- **eSuite pass-through.** Enterprise DSOs already charge consultants a monthly technology fee. Wayroo's per-rep cost bundles into it. Corporate is net-neutral per rep.
- **"The more your reps sell, the cheaper Wayroo gets."** Three-way revenue alignment.

**The economic close:** at E1 volume, Spendback savings alone (2.55% → 1% on wholesale order processing) can exceed the entire contract value. Build the account-specific model before the first CFO meeting. That single spreadsheet is the highest-leverage enterprise sales asset we can produce.
