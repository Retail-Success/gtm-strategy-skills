# The Dunford Canvas — Story A (Enterprise DSO)

## Step 1 — Competitive Alternatives

| Alternative | What it does well | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| **Exigo + its native rep app** | The incumbent at most enterprise DSOs. It has a real mobile rep app. | ⚠️ **Never claim Exigo lacks a rep app.** The difference is scope: ERP-tier rep tooling vs. a likely lighter app. The feature-scope teardown is still outstanding. |
| **A back office plus a stitched rep stack** | Each tool is good at its job: Square for payments, Hustle or Boards for rep tools, Dropbox for content, Shopify for ecommerce | **Nothing connects.** The company can't see rep sales, customers or inventory. Reps pay $60–100/month out of pocket across 6–10 apps. |
| **Shopify as the commerce layer** | Mature storefront | One DSO merchant account absorbs every fee and every chargeback. No commissions, no genealogy, no field app. |
| **Stay put** | No migration risk | The field stays invisible, and rep tool spend keeps climbing |

## Step 2 — Unique Attributes

| Attribute | Could a competitor claim it? |
|---|---|
| **Back office and field app from one vendor, natively integrated.** Commissions, genealogy, inventory and rep commerce in one system | ❌ Stitched stacks can't. ⚠️ Exigo can claim a first-party rep app, so **the claim is depth of integration and scope, not existence** |
| **Per-rep merchant accounts through ProPay.** Each rep is her own merchant | ❌ Shopify and Square-at-the-DSO can't |
| **Inventory that loads itself and updates as reps sell** | ❌ Square does inventory, but it can't fill it from the wholesale order |
| **Rep sales and customers flow back to corporate automatically** | ❌ No bolt-on can |
| **Corporate reaches the field inside the app they sell from.** Push alerts, media library, rank reporting | ⚠️ Partial. Hustle and Aice cover content and alerts; neither is the rep's selling app |
| **25 years in direct selling, $7B+ in commissions processed** | ⚠️ Exigo has comparable tenure. **Proof, not a differentiator** |

## Step 3 — Value

The ad's closing line is the value structure:

> ### **Control to the back office. Simplicity to the field.**

| For the back office (control) | For the field (simplicity) |
|---|---|
| **Know your customers.** Every rep sale becomes a customer record you own, not a Venmo payment you never see | **One app to sell from:** in person, online, by payment link |
| **Field visibility you've never had.** Rep sales, inventory and rank activity | **Inventory already loaded, and it updates itself as she sells** |
| **Stay protected** (see Pillar 3) | **Rank and downline reporting on her phone**, with no back-office login |
| **Never lose touch.** Push alerts and unified content reach reps where they work | **Real-time alerts** from her company, not buried in email |
| **One vendor, one system of record** | **Fewer apps to pay for.** Replaces Square and nearly every app she uses to sell and create content |

## Step 4 — Target Customers

**Enterprise DSOs evaluating their back office**, especially those running a stitched rep stack or on Shopify. Best-fit signals: a large field that sells in person, reps carrying their own inventory, a home office that can't say what its field sells, and a pending back-office decision.

## Step 5 — Market Category

> ### **The all-in-one platform for direct selling: back office and field, from one vendor.**

"All-in-one" is rejected in the Essentials docs because there it describes a rep app, and every rep app says it. **Here it describes a scope nobody else in the category offers:** back office plus rep app plus rep payments, natively connected. **The phrase is earned in Story A, and only in Story A.**

Short form, from the ad: **"It extends your back office to the field without exposing more complexity."**

## Step 6 — Relevant Trends

1. **Reps became real merchants.** They carry stock, take cards and run their business from a phone, but the software still treats them as a channel. *(The Phase 11 strategic narrative, [`11-strategic-narrative.md`](11-strategic-narrative.md).)*
2. **A phone is a card reader now.** In-person selling no longer needs hardware.
3. **FTC and state contractor rules** assume the rep owns her transactions. A real DSO concern and a legitimate pillar, **but the third reason, not the first.** DSO leaders feel field activation daily; they feel FTC risk occasionally.

# Positioning Statement — Story A

> **For direct selling companies** whose reps sell across Square, Venmo, spreadsheets and a pile of apps the home office can't see,
>
> **ByDesign and Wayroo are the all-in-one platform for direct selling**: the back office that runs the business and the app that runs the field, from one vendor.
>
> **Unlike** a back office with a stitched rep stack, or Shopify in front of a commission engine, **every rep sale, customer and inventory movement lands in one system**, with each rep on her own merchant account.
>
> **Which means** control for the back office, simplicity for the field, and a company that can finally see and protect its own field.
>
> **This matters now because** reps have become real merchants, and the software around them never caught up.

# Messaging Pillars — Story A

## Pillar 1 — Control to the back office

> **Know your customers. See your field. Never lose touch.**

- Rep sales flow back as records, and the customer belongs to the company
- Rank reporting and rep activity, live
- Push alerts and the media library reach reps inside the app they sell from

## Pillar 2 — Simplicity to the field

> **One app to sell from, with the inventory already in it.**

- POS for in-person sales, storefront and payment links for online
- Inventory loads from the wholesale order and **updates itself with every sale**
- Rank and downline numbers on the phone

### The replacement claim — say exactly this much

| Claim | When |
|---|---|
| ✅ **"Replaces Square and several other apps."** | **Now, anywhere.** Name them when asked: payments (Square, Venmo), inventory spreadsheets, content folders, separate reporting logins |
| ✅ **"Nearly every app a rep needs to sell and create content."** | **Now, anywhere** (confirmed 2026-09-23; does not depend on Aice) |
| 🚫 **"Replaces every app."** | Never. *Nearly* is the claim; *every* invites a counterexample |

## Pillar 3 — Stay protected

> **Protected from FTC risk, and protected on brand.**

| Protection | What makes it true |
|---|---|
| **FTC and contractor-rule exposure** | Rep sales are documented as records. Each rep is her own merchant through ProPay, which supports the independent-business structure the FTC and state rules assume. |
| **Chargeback and fraud liability** | It sits with the rep's merchant account, not the DSO's |
| **Brand consistency** | The **media library** lets corporate push unified content, so reps post approved assets instead of their own photos |

⚠️ **Pillar 3 is third on purpose.** Per the standing hierarchy (rep transaction infrastructure, then native integration, then FTC), it closes the argument; it doesn't open it. The ad gets this right: *"stay protected"* is one of three corporate benefits, not the headline.

# Custom Branding — an Upsell, Not a Risk

The standard Wayroo app is unbranded. **Custom branding is sold separately.** Content in the media library carries the DSO's brand either way, so *"consistent branded content"* is true for every customer.

- **Enterprise DSO (Story A):** offer branding as an upsell line in the proposal
- **Pilot:** the first three partners get free branding, a stated $25,000 value, bounded at three
- **Rep-facing Essentials surfaces:** still no "Powered by Wayroo" copy. That rule is about rep-facing copy, not company-level ads

# Words — Story A Only

| ✅ Say | 🚫 Don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **All-in-one: back office and field** | "All-in-one app" on its own | The scope is what makes it true. Without "back office and field," it's a rep-app cliché |
| **Replaces Square and nearly every app a rep needs to sell and create content** | "Replaces all their apps" | True now, and it survives a follow-up question |
| **Extends your back office to the field** | "Syncs to" · "integrates with" | Vendor-speak. *Extends* says it's the same system |
| **Stay protected** (then name FTC and brand) | Leading with FTC or compliance | Third pillar, not the opener |

# The Booth Ad, Scored Against This Doc

| Ad line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *"The back office behind some of direct selling's most successful companies… 25 years"* | ✅ Proof, correctly used |
| *"Wayroo, an all-in-one app…"* | ✅ **Right for Story A.** Booth traffic is enterprise DSOs |
| *"Point-of-sale tools to manage inventory and sell in person or online"* | 🟡 True, but it **undersells the structural claim.** *"Inventory that fills and updates itself"* is what Square can't say |
| *"Consistent branded content, real-time alerts and rank reporting"* | ✅ Media library, push alerts, rank reporting, all live |
| *"Everything reps need, wherever they are"* | ✅ Brand language backed by the replacement claim. If a DSO asks, the answer is *"Square and nearly every app a rep uses to sell and create content"* |
| *"Know your customers, stay protected, never lose touch"* | ✅ Pillars 1 and 3. Booth staff need the FTC and brand answer to *"protected from what?"* |
| *"Extends your back office to the field without exposing more complexity"* | ✅ **The best line in the ad.** Now the Story A short form, and the CTO opener |
| *"Control to the back office and simplicity to the field"* | ✅ **Adopted as the company-level tagline** |

**v2 change (optional, low priority):** change *"tools to manage inventory"* to *"inventory that updates itself as they sell."*
