# The Three Strongest Intersections

> ### 1. 🔴 R1 × P1 — the in-person seller losing the sale, at a ProPay-approved account
>
> **Severity 5, fit 🟢, and it is the strongest observed reaction in the entire evidence base.** Tap-to-Pay drew the loudest response of the JE launch.
>
> **This is the beachhead cell.** It exists in volume at exactly one place today: **~16,000 un-adopted Paparazzi reps**, where ProPay is already cleared.

> ### 2. R1 × P3 — manual SKU entry, at any account
>
> **Severity 5, fit 🟢, and it needs nothing from the DSO.** Pre-loaded inventory works on download.
>
> **This is the cell that carries the un-onboarded accounts.** Where P1 is blocked, P3 still delivers — which is why the master positioning leads on *"your inventory's already in it"* rather than on Tap-to-Pay.

> ### 3. R2 × P4 — the team leader who cannot see her downline
>
> **Severity 5, fit 🟢, zero DSO dependency.** Observed at JE, and it is the one cell that works identically at every account regardless of ProPay status.
>
> **The highest-yield cell at a locked account**, and the reason team leaders are a beachhead rather than a tactic.

---

# The Cell That Should Worry You

> ### 🔴 R1 × P1 at a ProPay-pending account: severity 5, fit 🔴
>
> **The most painful problem, for the most valuable rep, with no product answer** — because the selling tools are dark until the DSO acts.
>
> This single cell is the reason for the entire locked-state design, the demand counter, and Beat 2. **It is also the argument against running Tap-to-Pay creative at un-onboarded accounts:** advertising into this cell creates demand we cannot fulfil.
>
> **The mitigation is real but partial.** P3 and P4 still deliver, which is why a rep at an un-onboarded account is not empty-handed. **But her worst problem is untouched, and no copy fixes that.**

---

# The Row That Changes the Denominator

**R4, the hobbyist, scores 1 across every problem.** She has no problems Wayroo solves because she is not running a business — she signed up for a discount on personal purchases.

**Color Street's CEO put her at 80% of his nominal field.** Under a payments-only revenue model she can download, hold Revolution Pro, count as an adopter, and generate **$0 forever.**

> **Every adoption-rate denominator in the plan should exclude her** — and the correction is a wholesale-order query, not a survey.

---

# The Column That Is Underserved

**P2 — the $60–100/month tool spend — is the only problem rated 4+ across every real segment, and fit is 🟡 everywhere.**

Wayroo replaces the selling and record-keeping half of the stack. **Canva and SMS survive**, and SMS was requested repeatedly and unprompted at JE.

> **Do not claim a clean sweep.** The honest claim is a large dent, and it survives contact with a rep who still has a Canva subscription. **The SMS gap is the clearest product opportunity the map exposes.**

---

# How This Compares to the Beachhead Ranking

**The map confirms it and sharpens one thing.** Paparazzi (A), team leaders (C), and the JE 510 (B) all sit on strong cells.

**What the map adds:** the beachhead is not really a *segment*, it is a **segment × ProPay-status pair.** The same rep is a 🟢 or a 🔴 on the most severe problem depending on whether her company has done paperwork.

> **Operating consequence:** every targeting decision, every creative selection, and every forecast should be segmented on account ProPay status — **not just on who the rep is.** That is a data field ([`04-metrics-and-analytics.md`](04-metrics-and-analytics.md) requirement #4) more than it is a strategy.
