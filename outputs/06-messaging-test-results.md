# Test 1 — Locked-State Comprehension 🔴 Run first

**Tests:** assumption A12 — the lock reads as *pending*, not as a paywall. **The entire demand mechanism depends on it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Method** | Show the screen. One question. |
| **Sample** | 5–10 JE reps |
| **Cost** | **Free** |
| **The question** | *"What would you do next?"* |

| Response | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *"I'd tell my home office"* / *"I'd ask corporate"* | ✅ **Pass** |
| *"How much is it?"* | 🔴 **Fail — reads as a paywall** |
| *"So I can't use it"* | 🔴 **Fail — reads as withheld** |
| *"I'd wait"* | ⚠️ Partial — no churn, no signal either |

**Pass bar:** 7 of 10 give a pass response.

# Test 2 — "Included" vs. "Free"

**Tests:** whether *free* reads as trial or as cheap. The whole library uses *included* on this hypothesis, untested.

**Method:** two versions of the same first-run line to matched groups of 10.

> **A:** *"Included with Revolution Pro. You're already paying for it."*
> **B:** *"Free with Revolution Pro."*

**Ask:** *"What does that mean to you?"* · *"Is there a catch?"* · *"How long does it last?"*

**Watch for:** does B produce *"how long is it free for?"* — that is the trial-perception risk, made visible.

# Test 3 — Comprehension in Their Own Words

**Tests:** whether the positioning survives one retelling. **The hardest and most revealing test here.**

**Method:** after a rep has used the app for a week — *"If you were telling someone in your downline what this is, what would you say?"*

| Listen for | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *"It's already got my stuff in it"* | ✅ **USP 1 landed** |
| *"It's for taking card payments"* | ⚠️ Hook landed, position did not |
| *"It's the company's app"* | ✅ Category landed |
| *"It's like Square but…"* | ⚠️ Framed against the wrong competitor |
| *"I'm not really sure"* | 🔴 **Fail at the first hop** |

🔴 **Capture verbatim.** Their words become the copy — this is the single highest-value output of the whole test plan.

# Test 7 — The DSO Opener ✅ **RESULT IN** 🆕 Added 2026-09-29

**Tests:** which frame earns a DSO home office's attention in the first thirty seconds.
**Method:** Method 5 — sales-call feedback loop. **Not designed as a test**; reconstructed from a sales leader's report of changing her pitch mid-campaign.
**Sample:** **15+ Wayroo demos to DSO home offices**, run by **Autumn Fowers**, reported 2026-09-28.
**Source:** [`inputs/2026-09-28-rs-management-meeting.md`](../inputs/2026-09-28-rs-management-meeting.md) §1–2.

### Result

| Variant | Opener | Observed response | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Compliance + selling tools** | *"They don't even respond. Like that's not really our business... and then they're **done before the call even ends**."* | 🔴 **Fail — disqualifying, not merely weak** |
| **B** | **"An extension of Revolution — Revolution, or the parts that matter, in the palm of your consultant's hands"** | *"Then there's interest. And now they're like, well, **can it do this?** Oh, **it would be great if it could do wholesale ordering**."* | ✅ **Pass — produced feature pull** |

**Pass signal observed:** the prospect starts asking what else the product does. That is the strongest available proxy for *"the category landed"* in a live call, and it is the same reaction pattern Color Street's H1 showed — this says it generalises across 15+ accounts.

**Failure mode, stated precisely:** variant A does not produce scepticism or objections, which would be recoverable. It produces **category rejection** — the DSO concludes the product addresses a problem they don't own. The conversation ends rather than slowing down.

### Verbatim to keep

> *"An extension of Revolution."* · *"In the palm of their consultant's hands."* · *"This is a field tool and this helps give data back."*

**Their words, now ours.** All three are in [`06-messaging-house.md`](06-messaging-house.md) §9.

### What this licenses — and what it does not

| ✅ Licensed | 🚫 Not licensed |
|---|---|
| Raising the **"compliance is never the opener"** rule from one-account inference to tested | Any **per-persona** claim. We don't know whether H2 or H3 were in those rooms, so H2–H4 confidence is unchanged |
| The **extension frame as the H-block opener** at a ByDesign client | The **D1 noun swap** (*"extends your back office"*) at a non-ByDesign DSO — assumed, untested. **That's Test 8** |
| The **perceived-cash-and-carry routing rule** as working guidance | The **economic line** (*"costs you nothing and pays you"*) — that is still Test 5, pending |

### Limitations, honestly

Reported in summary rather than transcribed. No named accounts, no per-call dates, sample not enumerated beyond *"15 plus."* **It is stronger than everything else in the library anyway**, because it is a comparison rather than an observation, n is 15+ against 1–2 elsewhere, and the failure is self-reported by the person who ran the calls — a direction bias does not usually run.

# Test 8 — The Extension Frame at a Non-ByDesign DSO 🆕 **Run at DSU**

**Tests:** whether Test 7's winner survives the substitution *Revolution → your back office*.
**Why it matters:** the tested sentence is literally true only at a ByDesign client. **D1 — the entire enterprise new-logo motion — rests on the swapped version, which nobody has said out loud to a prospect.**
**Method:** Method 5 at the booth, alongside Test 6. **Free.**

**Say:** *"It extends the back office you already run out to the field."*

| Listen for | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *"How does that work with [their back office]?"* | ✅ **Pass — feature pull, same signal as Test 7** |
| *"We already have a back office"* → and the conversation stops | 🔴 **Fail — the swap doesn't carry.** Fall back to the replacement claim |
| *"So it's a rep app"* | ⚠️ Partial — landed as a category we're crowded in. Overlaps Test 6's fail condition |
