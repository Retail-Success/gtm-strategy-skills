## Why This Matters for Wayroo (Adapted from Methodology Default)

The GTM Strategist methodology targets churned customers + lost deals + downgraded customers as standard exit interview categories. **Wayroo's situation requires segment adjustment:**

| Methodology Default | Wayroo Reality |
|---|---|
| Churned customers (recent cancellations) | **Limited supply** — primary churn driver historically is DSO bankruptcy, not competitive displacement (per `my-gtm-context.md` Section 7). Strong retention signal but small interview pool. |
| Lost deals (sales process → didn't buy) | **High supply, high value** — Daniel has 48 open opps in 2026; some will close-lost. 2025 yielded $1.84M in 9 deals — implying ~25–35 deals where Wayroo didn't close. Rich source. |
| Downgraded customers | **Not applicable** — Wayroo's pricing model is per-active-rep with eSuite pass-through; downgrading rarely meaningful. |

**Wayroo-specific high-value exit interview categories:**

1. **Closed-lost competitive deals** — DSO chose Aice, Rallyware, Shopify alone, or stayed status quo
2. **Closed-lost no-decision** — DSO entered sales process, didn't move forward, didn't pick competitor either
3. **Dormant opportunities (12+ months no movement)** — went cold after initial engagement
4. **Existing Freedom clients who declined Wayroo when offered** — the inverse: bought Freedom, said no to Wayroo upsell
5. **Wayroo customers with low rep adoption / low MAU** — functional churn even if contract active
6. **Churned customers (rare but high-value when available)** — DSO cancelled outright (excluding bankruptcy churn)

**The Aice partnership wrinkle:** If `project_aice_partnership_pending.md` confirms, some previously "lost to Aice" deals become reframable as "lost to a future partner" — those prospects may now be re-targetable through the Aice channel. Worth keeping in the exit interview lens once partnership signs.

## Interview Framework (20–30 Minutes Each)

The methodology default questions adapted for Wayroo specifically. The framing throughout should signal "we're trying to get better," not "we want to win you back."

### Section 1 — Context (3–5 min)

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| *"Walk me back to when you first started looking at rep-tool / DSO commerce platforms. What problem were you trying to solve?"* | Surfaces the buying trigger and the original pain — may not be the pain Wayroo positioned against |
| *"At the time, what was your rep stack? What was on the table for change vs. staying as-is?"* | Reveals the alternatives that were truly being considered (often broader than "Wayroo vs. competitor") |
| *"Who was driving the evaluation internally?"* | DMU composition validation — was the buying committee what we modeled? |

### Section 2 — Decision (5–10 min)

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| *"What was the specific moment or event that tipped the decision?"* | Trigger event — often more revealing than the "rational" reason |
| *"What alternatives did you seriously consider? What did you ultimately choose, and why?"* | Real competitive landscape (not what we assume); winning vendor's positioning |
| *"What did the winning vendor (or the 'do nothing' option) do better than us?"* | The most valuable single question in the entire interview |
| *"Was price the issue, or something else? If price — at what number would the answer have been different?"* | Pricing intelligence — surfaces real budget vs. positioning failure |
| *"Looking back, what would have changed your decision in our favor?"* | Often surfaces gaps (case study, integration proof, specific feature) that are addressable |

### Section 3 — Product / Experience (5–10 min)

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| *"What did we do well in the sales process?"* | Confidence-building question (gets to truth on what stays); calibrates praise vs. critique |
| *"What did we do poorly?"* | The harder question — the second one because rapport from question 1 makes this easier |
| *"If you could change one thing about the product or how we presented it, what would it be?"* | Surfaces the single highest-priority improvement |
| *"Did the [Paparazzi case study / Spendback economics / ERP framing / Path B integration] land or not? Why?"* | Direct messaging-house test — validates or invalidates Phase 6 positioning hypotheses |
| *"Was there anything we did that felt off — sales technique, communication style, urgency framing?"* | Sales-motion-specific feedback (catches things like "you felt pushy" or "you didn't follow up enough") |

### Section 4 — Forward-Looking (3–5 min)

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| *"Is there anything that would bring you back into the conversation in 6 or 12 months?"* | Future re-engagement window |
| *"What would we need to change for you to reconsider?"* | Surfaces product or positioning gaps — explicit, actionable |
| *"What advice would you give us as a vendor talking to DSOs your size?"* | Open-ended; often produces the most quotable insight |
| *"Is there anything I didn't ask that you think I should know?"* | Reveals the unsaid — the thing the prospect was hoping you'd ask |

## Outreach Approach

Getting people to do exit interviews is hard. The prospect didn't choose you. They have no reason to spend 20 minutes helping you.

### Framing

**The frame matters more than the script:** "We're trying to get better; your honest feedback would help us serve DSOs like yours better. No save attempt — I just want to learn."

If they sense you're trying to win them back, they'll decline. If they sense you genuinely want to improve, ~30–40% will say yes.

### Outreach Email Template

**Subject:** *"15-minute call to help us get better — no save attempt"*

**Body:**
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Quick note. When we last talked, [Company] ultimately went a different direction [or: this never moved forward / never closed]. I'm not writing to revisit anything — I'm writing because we're trying to get sharper, and your honest feedback on what we did poorly would help us a lot.
>
> 15–20 minutes, on the phone, off the record. I'll ask you about what alternatives you considered, what tipped the decision, and what we should have done differently. I'll send you the questions in advance so you can think about it.
>
> As a small thank-you, I'll send you a $50 Amazon gift card after the call. (Or if your company has a no-gift policy, I'll make a $50 donation to a charity of your choice instead.)
>
> Are you open to 15 minutes? Two times that work for you, and I'll send an invite.
>
> Thanks for considering it,
> [Sam Atieh]
> Product Marketing, ByDesign

**Why this works:**
- Frame is clear: learning, not selling
- Specific time commitment (15–20 min) → low friction
- Specific incentive ($50 gift card) → acknowledges their time has value
- Charity alternative → respects corporate gift policies
- Off the record → signals authentic openness
- Sam Atieh (not Daniel) as sender → distance from the deal; not the rep whose deal was lost

### Cadence

- Send email outreach to all 5–7 target accounts in week 1
- Follow up once at week 2 if no response
- Don't send a third — respect the silence as the answer
- Expect 30–40% conversion to actual call

## Conducting the Interview

### Pre-Call

- Re-read the prospect's file in Salesforce — what stage did they reach, what was discussed, what was the close-loss disposition
- Pre-send the question list 24–48 hours before the call (so they can think about answers)
- Keep your own preconceptions in check — the interview is to learn, not validate your hypothesis

### During the Call

- **Talk 20% of the time, listen 80%.** This is the inversion of a sales call.
- **Don't defend.** When they tell you what you did poorly, the answer is "thank you for that — can you say more about it?" Not "well actually we do have [feature]." Defending kills the interview.
- **Probe with "why?" and "tell me more."** The first answer is usually the surface answer. The third or fourth answer is the truth.
- **Capture verbatim language.** Their exact words become future ad copy, sales talk tracks, and positioning input. *"It just felt like we'd be one more dashboard nobody logged into"* is more valuable than "they had adoption concerns."

### Post-Call

- Send the gift card / donation receipt within 24 hours (trust signal — promise-keeping)
- Send a 2-sentence thank-you email
- Log findings in shared exit-interview tracker (see Synthesis Framework below)
- DO NOT re-pitch them. Re-engagement window is "6+ months" not "next week."
