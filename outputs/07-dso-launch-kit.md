**Phase:** 7 — Preparing Launch Assets (skill Task 4, adapted for a permission ask)
**Date:** 2026-08-13 · **Owner:** Sam Atieh · **Due: 2026-08-22 — critical path**
**Audience:** Founder / CEO / VP Sales / Field Ops at an existing ByDesign client
**Derives from:** [`06-positioning-master.md`](06-positioning-master.md) · [`06-messaging-house.md`](06-messaging-house.md)

> ### 🔴 Beat 1 rules — violate these and the ask stops being frictionless
>
> **Never mention ProPay onboarding. Never lead with Tap-to-Pay.** Both convert a free, no-effort approval into a compliance project. ProPay is beat 3, and it arrives later, pulled by the DSO's own reps. See the three-beat structure in [`06-messaging-house.md`](06-messaging-house.md).

## The frame that makes all three permissions askable

> ### Every one of the three permissions **removes work from the DSO.** None of them adds any.
>
> This is not a request for budget, headcount, or a project. It is an offer to do something for their field that they would otherwise have to do themselves. **Lead every permission with what we do, not what they grant.**

| Permission | Sounds like an ask | Is actually |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Distribute to your field** | "Send our thing to your people" | **We wrote the emails, the FAQ, and the training. You approve and we run it.** |
| **2. Let us advertise Wayroo and Aice inside Freedom and Revolution** | "Put your banners in our product" | **Reps and your team discover it where they already log in — no campaign required from you.** |
| **3. Let us market to reps directly** | "Give us your list" | **Your launch doesn't depend on your marketing bandwidth. Run your own campaign too, or don't.** |

> ### Permission 2 covers two products and two audiences — say so plainly
>
> | Surface | Who sees it | What runs there |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Freedom** | Home-office staff | Wayroo + Aice, **beat-1 messaging only** — never Tap-to-Pay |
> | **Revolution** | Reps | Wayroo + Aice, **Tap-to-Pay leads** |
>
> **Do not bundle Aice in silently.** A DSO discovering later that we advertised a second vendor to their field under a permission they thought covered one is a trust problem that costs more than the placement is worth. **Name both products in the ask.**
>
> **The Aice line:** *"Aice is the training and team-chat app — free as well, and the two work together. Same placement, same approval."*

**Permission 3 is the hard one and needs the most care.** Do not phrase it as list access. Phrase it as *"so this doesn't sit in your marketing queue."* Most DSO marketing teams are one or two people with a convention to run — **relieving them is the sell.**

# The Deck — Slide by Slide

**Format:** 12 slides, ~15 minutes, leaves 15 for discussion. Google Slides. Brand per Retail Success standards.

### Slide 1 — Title

> **Wayroo Essentials**
> The selling app that comes with your back office.
> *Included with Revolution Pro. Nothing to buy.*

Put *"nothing to buy"* on the title slide. It reframes the entire meeting before the first question.

### Slide 2 — Your reps are already buying this

> **Your reps spend $60–100 a month on tools you can't see.**
> Six to ten apps. Square for payments. A spreadsheet for inventory. Canva. A separate invoicing tool. None of them connected to you.

Visual: the app-logo pile. **Source the figure** (Jordan Essentials, July 2026) — a real number from a real client is what makes this land instead of sounding like a stat.

### Slide 3 — What that costs you

> **Under-equipped reps sell less and quit sooner. Rep churn is the most expensive line in your business, and it is a tooling problem before it is a motivation problem.**

Three bullets, no more:
- Reps lose sales at the table when a customer has no cash
- Nothing a rep sells in person reaches you as a record
- The rep who quits took a customer list you never had

### Slide 4 — What we built

> **Wayroo Essentials.** The rep opens it and **their wholesale order and inventory are already there.** No setup, no import, no spreadsheet.
> Sell at an event. Send an invoice. And every sale flows back to your back office on its own.

Visual: **a real first-run screenshot with populated inventory.** Not a feature grid. The populated screen *is* the argument.

### Slide 5 — What it costs you

> ## Nothing.
>
> It's included with Revolution Pro. **For every rep who has Pro, it's already paid for. For every rep who doesn't — it's a reason to get it.**

One word on the slide. Let it sit.

> ⚠️ **Corrected 2026-08-14.** This slide previously read *"which your reps already have and already pay for."* **A founder knows their own Pro penetration, and it is rarely the whole field** — at Jordan Essentials it is **254 of 2,614 active reps.** Overstating it costs the room in the first five minutes. **The corrected line is also the stronger one, because it sets up slide 6.**
>
> **Look up the account's Pro penetration before the meeting** and say the real number. It is a fact they already have, so quoting it back builds credibility rather than spending it — and the lower it is, the better slide 6 lands.

### Slide 6 — What you make *(the economic line)*

> ### **Wayroo is the reason your reps buy Revolution Pro — and you make margin on every one of them.**
>
> Every rep on Pro is paying for it, you or them. **You set the rep price. You keep the spread.** Wayroo is what makes that subscription worth having.
>
> ### At Jordan Essentials, **764 reps downloaded Wayroo. Only 254 had Pro.**
> ### **510 reps asked for a subscription they didn't have — without anyone selling it to them.**

> 🔴 **This is now the strongest slide in the deck, and it is evidence rather than argument.** Wayroo demand ran at **3x** JE's Revolution Pro population. Every DSO in the room has a Pro penetration number, and it is almost certainly low — **this slide tells them Wayroo will go and sell it for them.**
>
> **Ask them their Pro penetration in the room.** Whatever they answer, the JE ratio does the rest of the work. It also opens the follow-up naturally: *"how many more would you have if every rep wanted the thing Pro comes with?"*
>
> ✅ **All figures on this slide are now production-confirmed (2026-08-14).** JE charges reps **$5/month and keeps $2**; ByDesign takes $3 — a **67% markup** on cost, or 40% of the rep-facing price. The 764/254/510 numbers are confirmed. **Nothing on this slide needs hedging any more.**
>
> **Say it as the worked example, not as their number:** *"Jordan Essentials charges $5 and keeps $2. You set your own price and keep your own spread."* The markup a DSO chooses is theirs — quoting JE's as if it were a rate card invites a negotiation nobody asked for.

### Slide 7 — Why this holds your field together *(retention)*

Three arguments, in the DSO's order of concern:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rep retention** | A rep whose inventory, customers, and payments live in one place has a running business. A rep juggling six apps has a hobby that keeps breaking. **The equipped rep stays.** |
| **Revenue retention** | Every rep who runs their business in Wayroo has a reason to keep paying for Pro — **your margin line gets more durable, not just bigger.** |
| **Recruiting** | "We give you the tools" is a recruiting line your competitors are still buying separately. |

**Do not say** that Wayroo raises the DSO's switching cost with ByDesign. True, and it is our benefit, not theirs. Saying it out loud turns a gift into a trap.

### Slide 8 — The three permissions *(the ask)*

> **We're not asking for budget. We're asking for three yeses, and we do the work behind all three.**

| | The permission | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Distribute it to your field** | We wrote the announcement, the how-to, the FAQ and the training. You approve; we run it. |
| **2** | **Let us advertise Wayroo and Aice inside Freedom and Revolution** | Your reps find it in Revolution where they already log in; your team sees it in Freedom. No campaign needed from you. *(Aice is the free training and team-chat app — same placement, same approval.)* |
| **3** | **Let us reach your reps directly** | Email and in-product, so your launch isn't waiting on your marketing team. **Run your own campaign too if you want — this just means you don't have to.** |

### Slide 9 — Who does what

| We do | You do |
|---|---|
| All rep-facing copy, FAQ, training | Approve the messaging |
| In-app placement and onboarding | Say yes to placement |
| Email sequence, send and support | Give us the go-ahead |
| Support tickets from reps | Nothing |
| Adoption reporting back to you | Read it |

**This slide closes more meetings than the economics slide.** Approvers are not weighing cost — they are weighing *how much of my quarter does this eat.* The answer is visibly almost none.

### Slide 10 — Timeline

> Pilot mid-September · General availability early October.
> **Your reps can be selling on it inside a month.**

### Slide 11 — What we'll report back

> Adoption. Sales volume through the app. Which of your field leaders are driving it.
>
> ### **And how many of your reps are actually selling their own inventory.**
>
> **You will see more about your field's selling activity in the first month than you have ever had.**

Field visibility is the quiet second sell, and for VP Sales it is often the first one. Keep it on its own slide so it lands.

> **Lead the slide with the C&C-share line.** No DSO knows this number — and every one we have been able to check was wrong about it, in both directions (Jordan Essentials, Color Street; `my-gtm-context.md` §3). **Offering to measure something a founder cannot get anywhere else is a stronger hook than adoption reporting**, and it costs us nothing because the data is a by-product of the rollout.
>
> It also sets up the follow-on conversation honestly: if their real C&C share is higher than they think — and we expect it usually is — **the case for finishing ProPay onboarding writes itself at the next meeting.**

### Slide 12 — The ask

> **Three yeses and a date.**
> Who on your side approves rep communications?

Leave with a named person and a date. Not "we'll follow up."

# Objection Handling

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| **"Why are you giving this away?"** | **We're not — it's included with Pro, which your reps pay for.** We make money when reps sell through it. Our incentive and yours are the same number: how many of your reps are actually selling. |
| **"Can we brand it?"** | Not in this version — it ships unbranded so we can get it to every client at once. **White-labeling is available as a paid add-on if you want it.** *(Say this plainly. Discovered later it reads as bait-and-switch; said upfront it is just a product tier.)* |
| **"Will this compete with tools we already give our reps?"** | It replaces the ones they're buying themselves, not the ones you provide. If there's overlap, tell us where and we'll position around it. |
| **"Why are you advertising another company's app to our field?"** 🔴 | Aice is our partner and it's free for your reps — training and team chat, which sits next to Wayroo rather than on top of it. **Together they cover what your reps are currently paying for.** If you'd rather we ran Wayroo only, that's fine and the rest of the ask is unchanged. *(Concede this immediately if pushed. Aice placement is worth far less than permission 2 itself.)* |
| **"What do you do with our rep data?"** | It's your data in your back office. Wayroo writes to it; it doesn't take it anywhere. **Rep customer records land in your system — most of them for the first time.** |
| **"What if reps don't like it and blame us?"** | Fair. It's free, it's optional, and reps who ignore it lose nothing. **We take the support tickets, not you.** And the pilot exists so you see real reactions before your whole field does. |
| **"We don't let vendors email our field."** | Understood — then take permission 1 and 2 and skip 3. **You'll get a slower start and you'll carry the comms yourself.** We'd rather have two yeses than lose the launch. *(Never fight permission 3. It is the one most likely to sink the whole meeting.)* |
| **"What about reps who aren't on Revolution Pro?"** | They'll see it and be prompted to subscribe — **which is margin for you.** We'll give you the numbers so you can decide how hard to push. |
| **"How long until our reps can take card payments?"** | ⚠️ **Beat-3 trigger. Do not open this door — but answer honestly if they do.** *"Card payments, invoicing and event checkout ship together — they're live from [ship date] and included at no cost. There's a merchant-account setup step on your side with our processor whenever you want to switch them on. It isn't needed to get started."* **Then move on.** Never make it a condition of the three permissions. |
| **"So the app doesn't do much yet?"** | Honest and easy: *"The selling tools release as a set on [date]. Until then your reps get their inventory, their dashboards and your media library — which is what Jordan Essentials' field has been using."* **Do not oversell the pre-ship state**; a DSO who expects selling tools on day one and gets three features has the same experience JE's reps had for a month. |

# Corporate FAQ

**Purpose: the DSO's own support team answers their field without routing to us.** Hand it over with the deck.

> ### 🔴 Question 4 is the one that matters
>
> If a DSO support rep improvises an answer to *"why can't I take card payments?"*, the most likely improvisation is *"I think you have to pay for that."* **That single sentence turns the lock into a paywall across an entire field.** Get this answer into their hands before the first rep asks.

### For your reps

**1. Is this free?** 🔴 *Two answers — check whether the rep has Jordan Central Pro before answering.*

> **Rep has Pro:** Yes. Wayroo Essentials is included with your Jordan Central Pro subscription. There's nothing extra to buy and no upgrade to purchase.
>
> **Rep does not have Pro:** Wayroo comes with Jordan Central Pro, which is $5/month — and you can turn it on yourself in your back office. That covers Wayroo and the selling tools with nothing else to buy on top.

⚠️ **Your support team fields questions from your whole field, not just the reps we campaign to.** We reach Pro subscribers, for whom Wayroo genuinely is included — but **at Jordan Essentials that is 254 of 2,614 active reps**, so most reps asking your team will need the second answer. **Giving the first answer to a rep without Pro means telling them it's free and then taking access away** — the fastest way to turn a free product into a grievance.

*Swap in your own numbers before circulating this internally.*

**2. Do I have to use it?**
No. It's there if you want it.

**3. Do I have to set anything up?**
No. Your inventory and your orders are already in it when you open it.

**4. Why can't I take card payments yet?** 🔴
Card payments are included in your app — we're finishing the merchant-account setup with the payment processor. It's a banking requirement, not something you buy. **It'll switch on for everyone once it's done.**
> ✅ Say: *"we're finishing setup."* 🚫 Never say: *"that's a paid feature," "upgrade," "not included in your plan."*

**5. Should I stop using Square / my spreadsheet?**
Up to you. Most reps keep both at first and drift over once they see their inventory updating itself.

**6. What happens to my customer information?**
Customers you enter in Wayroo appear in our back office, so we can support you better. Wayroo doesn't sell or share your data.

**7. I'm not on Jordan Central Pro. Can I use it?**
Wayroo comes with Pro. You can turn Pro on yourself in your back office.

**8. Who do I ask for help?**
Wayroo support handles it directly — [channel]. You don't need to go through us.

### For your team

**Do we pay for this?** No.
**Do we have to run a campaign?** No — we can run it, or you can, or both.
**Does this replace anything we're paying for?** Not on our side. It may replace tools reps buy themselves.
**What if a rep complains?** Send them to Wayroo support. Escalations come to [name].
**What do we get out of it?** Better-equipped reps, visibility into field selling we've never had, and margin on every rep who subscribes to Pro.
