# The Planning Problem

Two facts define the window, and they pull against each other.

**First: 2026 targets were missed, and the misses were structural.** Not an execution failure to correct with a harder Q4 — a set of constraints that produce the same result next year if nothing changes.

**Second: the AiCE acquisition is undetermined, and if it proceeds the AiCE brand likely survives** — meaning the Wayroo and ByDesign brands would retire.

The instinct is to wait. That is the wrong call, because it treats the acquisition as though it changes everything. **It doesn't.** It changes the brand and the entry point. It does not change what the product is, who buys it, how it makes money, or why 2026 failed.

> ## This document plans the part that is true either way, and names precisely what is contingent.
>
> One motion. Branding decision deferred. No parallel plans.

# 1. What Is True Either Way — The Invariants

Six things hold whether the acquisition closes, collapses, or drags. **Everything in the plan is built only on these.**

### Invariant 1 — Lead with the rep-facing app, not the back office

Selling Freedom means asking a DSO to replace its back office: expensive, high-switching-cost, 4–6 months. Selling the rep app means asking them to add something their field already wants.

| Evidence | Source |
|---|---|
| Both live prospect records are rep-app-first. Color Street is Wayroo-only, no Freedom pitch. | `prospects/color-street.md` |
| Jordan Essentials closed in **~11 weeks** as a Wayroo attach | `prospects/jordan-essentials.md` |
| **28% of a 2,500-rep field adopted in 48 hours** | JE launch, July 17–18 |
| Founder **voluntarily shortened her own free period** | JE launch |

**Under an AiCE brand this is the same motion.** AiCE is already rep- and creator-facing. The acquisition would change the logo on the app, not the direction of entry.

### Invariant 2 — Revenue scales with *transacting* adoption, not signature

There are two revenue lines, and the smaller one is the subscription.

| Line | Rate |
|---|---|
| **SaaS** — DSO pays ByDesign | **$3.00 / user / month** |
| **Payments** — ByDesign take on transactions through Wayroo selling tools (tap-to-pay, invoicing, POS) | **~1.5% of every transaction** |
| *DSO charges its reps* | *$5.00 / rep / month, on top of the existing $10 — a **$2/rep 67% margin** for the DSO* |

**Paparazzi is the only account with real GMV data, and it shows the shape:**

| | Paparazzi, 2025 |
|---|---|
| Reps | 4,000 |
| Rep sales through Wayroo | **$31M** |
| GMV per rep | **~$7,750 / year** |
| SaaS revenue (4,000 × $36) | **$144,000** |
| **Payments revenue ($31M × 1.5%)** | **$465,000** |
| **Total** | **$609,000** |

> ### **Payments is ~76% of the revenue. The subscription is the minority line.**

### What a rep is actually worth

| Rep state | Annual value to ByDesign |
|---|---|
| Signed but not adopted | **$0** |
| Adopted but dormant | **$36** ($3 × 12) |
| **Transacting** ($1,200/mo GMV × 1.5% + SaaS) | **~$252** ($36 SaaS + $216 payments) |
| *Blended per MAU, at a ~54% transact rate* | *~$152* |

**A transacting rep is worth ~7x a dormant one, and infinitely more than a signed-but-unadopted one.**

> **Note on units:** $252 is per *transacting* rep; $152 is per *MAU* (blended across transactors and dormant adopters). Both are correct for their denominator — use $252 when reasoning about converting a dormant rep, since that is the marginal gain.

This is the most consequential fact in the document. It means the metric that matters is not logos, not seats, and not even downloads — it is **transacting reps and GMV per rep.** `my-gtm-context.md` §5 already names the right North Star (*"rep sales volume through Wayroo and replicated sites"*). **The GTM function has simply never been organized around it.**

⚠️ **One caveat on the benchmark:** the $31M is total rep sales on Wayroo. The share running through Wayroo *payment rails* — where the 1.5% applies — may be lower than 100%. Confirm the realized take before using $465K as a hard figure. The directional conclusion holds regardless.

### Invariant 3 — The DSO resells at a profit

Existing positioning calls eSuite pass-through "cost-neutral." The truth is stronger: **the DSO makes money on Wayroo.** $2/rep/month, at 67% margin, growing with every rep who adopts.

Two effects: cost stops being objection #1 (`my-gtm-context.md` §6 currently lists it as such), and **the DSO acquires its own financial incentive to drive adoption** — which is exactly the lever Invariant 2 identifies as the revenue engine.

### Invariant 4 — Back-office-agnostic integration expands the market

Wayroo integrates with Exigo and any other back office (confirmed Aug 8). This changes the addressable market from *"DSOs willing to replace a back office"* to *"any DSO"* — including the installed base of the category's largest player.

Unaffected by the acquisition. Arguably more valuable under it, since AiCE already connects to multiple back offices.

### Invariant 5 — Proof is the binding constraint on every channel

Zero published case studies. Zero testimonials. The Paparazzi result — **4,000 reps, $31M, 60%+ YoY** — has been unpublished since May.

This throttles partner enablement, inbound conversion, and sales credibility simultaneously. And it is **not an evidence problem — it is a permission-and-packaging problem.** The material exists; nobody has asked.

Proof assets survive a rebrand. A published customer result is reusable under any logo.

### Invariant 6 — Capacity gates new-logo growth

One rep, a 4–6 month cycle, 0.4% of revenue in marketing against a 5–20% benchmark, and unit economics of 15–67:1 on every retained channel.

Those returns don't indicate great marketing. They indicate **structural under-investment in a funnel that cannot absorb more volume**, because everything downstream queues behind one closer.

# 2. Why 2026 Was Missed — The Structural Diagnosis

Stated plainly, because the go-forward plan is only credible if the diagnosis is.

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| One sales rep, 4–6 month cycle | ~2 closes/year is the natural throughput. A 5-logo target was never reachable. |
| Zero published proof | Every channel converts below its potential; partners can't be equipped at all |
| Best channel starved — 71.4% conversion, **7.5% of opportunity supply** | The highest-yield input receives almost none of the investment |
| $17K into Google Ads → **$0 closed** | Budget consumed by a channel with no measured return |
| Revenue booked at signature; **value realized at adoption** | Post-sale adoption — where the money is — has no owner and no motion |
| Wayroo sold as an attachment to a harder product | Long cycles inherited from a sale we didn't need to make |

**None of these are fixed by trying harder in Q4.** Five of the six are fixed by changes inside GTM's control. The sixth (capacity) is a hiring decision.

# 3. The Operating Model

Three motions. Ordered by yield per unit of effort, which — given Invariant 2 — is not the conventional order.

## Motion A — The Transacting-Adoption Engine *(new; highest yield by a wide margin; no equivalent exists today)*

**The thesis:** with ~76% of revenue coming from a 1.5% transaction take, the money is made **after** the signature, by reps who actually sell. Growing transacting adoption inside accounts already signed produces more revenue, faster and cheaper, than signing new ones.

### The Jordan Essentials arithmetic

Applying the Paparazzi GMV benchmark (~$7,750/rep/year) to Jordan Essentials' ~2,500 reps:

| Scenario | Transacting reps | SaaS | Payments | **Total/year** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Today** (28% adopted) | ~700 | $25,200 | $81,375 | **$106,575** |
| **Target** (85% adopted) | ~2,125 | $76,500 | $247,031 | **$323,531** |
| | | | **Delta** | **+$216,956/year** |

> **~$217K of annual revenue at a single existing account, available through adoption work rather than a sales cycle.** No procurement, no competitive threat, no 4–6 month wait. The customer has already said yes.

For comparison, `my-gtm-context.md` §7 puts the average new-logo deal at ~$100K. **Deepening one existing account is worth more than two new logos, and it is available now.**

### Why nobody is doing it

Because at $3/user it looked like a $25K→$90K retention exercise. **With payments included it is a $107K→$324K revenue engine**, and it is the highest-yield activity available to the company.

### The lever inside the lever

There are **two** ways to grow that number, and only one has ever been considered:

| Lever | Mechanism | JE upside | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| More adopters | 700 → 2,125 downloads | +$218K | Slow |
| **More of the adopters transact** | 54% → 80% of the *existing* 700 | **+$39K** | **Fast — they're already in the app** |

**~$39K/year at Jordan Essentials with zero new downloads.** Every metric currently reported stops before the point where this lever operates.

**Full playbook:** [`10-transacting-adoption-playbook.md`](10-transacting-adoption-playbook.md)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Jordan Essentials first, then Paparazzi and every live deployment |
| **Owner** | Cassie Lewis + Autumn Fowers; Sam builds the playbook |
| **Mechanism** | Launch playbook, rep enablement, field-leader activation, GMV reporting to the DSO |
| **Why the DSO helps** | They earn $2/rep/month on adoption **and** their reps sell more. Make both visible. |
| **Brand exposure** | **None.** Works identically under any brand. |

### The metric change this forces

**Stop measuring downloads. Measure transacting reps and GMV per rep.**

| Retire | Adopt |
|---|---|
| Downloads | **% of rep base transacting monthly** |
| Adoption rate | **GMV per transacting rep** |
| MAU | **Time from download to first transaction** |

A 700-download launch is a good headline and an incomplete result. **The 700th download is worth $36/year until that rep makes a sale.**

> ### 🔴 Tap-to-Pay is not a feature request. It is a revenue line.
>
> Tap-to-Pay was the single strongest rep draw at the Jordan Essentials launch **and** it is a payments-revenue instrument — every transaction through it earns 1.5%. It has not shipped, and ~2,500 reps hold a convention-dated expectation.
>
> **Each week it slips costs adoption momentum and transaction revenue simultaneously.** This reclassifies it from "product dependency" to the highest-leverage revenue item on the roadmap. It belongs in the revenue conversation, not just the product one.

**Hypothesis:** a DSO on Exigo takes a meeting about a rep app that sits *on top of* their back office at a materially higher rate than one about replacing it.

**Scope: 5–10 accounts. Hard cap.** Measured on **meeting acceptance rate vs. the Freedom-led baseline** — explicitly not on closed revenue, which a 4–6 month cycle forbids. Uses only shipped capability.

| | |

| **Owner** | Daniel Lang |

# 5. The Branching Layer — What the AiCE Decision Actually Changes

Kept deliberately short. Most of the plan doesn't branch.

## What does not change

Motions A, B, and C. The proof assets. The pricing logic. The adoption playbook. The back-office-agnostic architecture. The capacity constraint. **Roughly 85% of the work above is unaffected by the outcome.**

## What changes

| Dimension | Acquisition proceeds | Acquisition does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand** | AiCE survives; Wayroo + ByDesign retire | Wayroo/ByDesign continue |
| **Rep-paid PLG (Track 2)** | **Acquired.** AiCE runs it today at $9.99–$49.99/mo with ~100K users. | **Must be built** — gated on rep personal inventory, deferred to 2027 |
| **Creator segment** | Acquired — ~50K individual sellers | Not addressable |
| **Top-of-funnel** | AiCE's PLG motion feeds the enterprise motion | Partner + inbound only |
| **Back Office Lite** | Replaces AiCE's Pillars referral; captures that revenue internally | Standalone product decision on its own merits |
| **Collateral** | Full reskin required | No change |

## The one decision that should not be made before the acquisition resolves

> **Track 2 / rep-paid PLG is a buy-versus-build, and the acquisition is the buy.**

AiCE already operates a rep-paid PLG motion with ~100K users. Building the equivalent internally requires rep personal inventory (deferred to 2027), a self-serve funnel, rep-facing marketing, and a support model — none of which exist.

**If the acquisition proceeds, that capability arrives fully built and staffed. Building it in parallel risks paying twice for the same motion.**

Conversely, **if the acquisition fails, Track 2 becomes the most important product decision of 2027** — because it is the only path to a motion that doesn't queue behind one sales rep. Leaving it unexamined concedes the rep-paid segment to AiCE as a competitor.

**Recommendation: hold Track 2 as a formal decision gated on the acquisition outcome. Do not drift into building it, and do not forget it if the deal dies.**

## What Phase 1–2 work feeds the acquisition decision itself

Worth naming, because it reframes this plan as an input rather than a bet placed alongside one:

| Evidence produced | What it tells the acquisition decision |
|---|---|
| **Exigo test result** | Whether a rep-app-led, back-office-agnostic motion wins meetings — the core thesis under either brand |
| **Adoption playbook results** | Whether transacting adoption is a repeatable engine or a Jordan Essentials one-off |
| **GMV per transacting rep** | **The valuation input** — see below |
| **Published proof** | Diligence-grade evidence Wayroo works at scale |

### The payments take is the acquisition's core valuation logic

AiCE's ~100K users (≈50K DSO reps + ≈50K creators) are worth very different amounts depending on which revenue line you value them on:

| Basis | 50K DSO reps |
|---|---|
| **SaaS only** ($3/user/mo) | **$1.8M/year** |
| **+ payments** — 50K × ~54% transacting × $14,400 GMV × 1.5% | **~$5.8M/year in payments alone** |

**Valued as subscription seats, AiCE's user base is a moderate asset. Valued as transaction volume routed onto Wayroo rails, it is the whole thesis.**

### And the model itself quantifies the acquisition premium

The Wayroo/AiCE 12-month model was built on **partnership** economics. Re-framed for acquisition:

| | Partnership | **Acquisition** |
|---|---|---|
| **ByDesign's share of the model** | **$3,355,500** | **$6,186,000** |
| | | **+$2,830,500 (+84%)** |

Composed of **$670,500** in commission no longer paid out — a line that compounds, since it is 20% of the fastest-growing revenue item — and **$2,160,000** of consolidated AiCE subscription revenue.

⚠️ **Heavy caveats on the user valuation, and they matter for diligence rather than direction:** not all 50K would migrate to Wayroo payment rails; creator GMV almost certainly differs from DSO-rep GMV and is unmeasured; the transact rate is derived from one account; and the Paparazzi benchmark is one account. **This is a framing for how to value the deal, not a number to put in a model.**

**Which is exactly why Motion A's measurement work is diligence work.** Establishing real GMV-per-transacting-rep and a measured transact rate across Paparazzi and Jordan Essentials gives a defensible multiplier to apply to any acquired user base. Without it, the acquisition gets valued on seats — the minority revenue line.

# 8. The Short Version

**2026 was missed for structural reasons, and the structure is fixable.**

**The central fact: ~76% of revenue is a 1.5% take on rep transactions, not the $3/user subscription.** At Paparazzi that is $465K in payments against $144K in SaaS. It means a transacting rep is worth ~4.2x a dormant one, and a signed-but-unadopted DSO is worth nothing at all.

**The company sells like a SaaS business and earns like a payments business.** Closing that gap is the plan.

Three things change the trajectory, none of which depend on the AiCE outcome:

1. **Build the transacting-adoption engine.** ~$217K/year is available at Jordan Essentials alone — more than two average new logos, with no sales cycle. No motion exists here today.
2. **Instrument it.** Transacting reps and GMV per rep are currently unmeasured. The majority of revenue is invisible at the rep level.
3. **Publish the proof and feed the partner channel.** Zero case studies against a $31M result; 71.4% conversion on 7.5% of supply. Both are cheap and both are overdue.

**One thing to hold rather than decide:** rep-paid PLG is a buy-versus-build, and the acquisition is the buy. Gate it, don't drift into it.

**And two reframes for the acquisition itself.** Valued as subscription seats, AiCE's ~100K users are a moderate asset; valued as transaction volume routed onto Wayroo rails, they are the entire thesis — and **the measurement work in Motion A is what makes that valuation defensible.** Separately, re-framing the existing 12-month model from partnership to acquisition takes ByDesign's share from **$3.36M to $6.19M (+84%)** on identical operating assumptions.

**One caution to carry into the deck:** the model's Year-0 baseline of 15,000 transacting users is **~6x the current ~2,500**, and only ~41% covered even with Color Street closed and Jordan Essentials fully penetrated. Better disclosed with a path attached than found in diligence.

**The through-line:** whether the company ends up called Wayroo or AiCE, it is becoming a business that leads with the rep-facing app, rides on any back office, and **earns when reps sell.** Everything above is an investment in that company.
