## Overview: Two Outbound Flows

Wayroo + Freedom has two distinct outbound motions. They share positioning anchors but use different target lists, owners, channels, and messaging emphasis.

| Dimension | **Flow 1 — New-Logo (Freedom + Wayroo)** | **Flow 2 — Existing-Client Wayroo Upsell (Hybrid DSO)** |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Daniel Lang (ByDesign Sales Executive) | Cassie Lewis (Account Exec) + Autumn Fowers (Product/Demo) — sourced from Daniel's Freedom client view |
| Target list source | New-logo prospecting (LinkedIn Sales Nav, Apollo, DSA events, Capterra) | Existing Freedom client base (51 active clients; 7 already identified as Hybrid candidates per memory) |
| Primary ICP | Mid-market DSOs (1K–10K reps) on Exigo / custom / party plan platform / Shopify-as-back-office | Existing Freedom clients with 10–30% C&C cohort + measurable rep performance decline + upcoming convention/event |
| Deal motion | New back-office + rep-app sale | Wayroo overlay on existing back office (lower-friction, faster-cycle) |
| Lead opener | Rep tool fragmentation + Shopify TCO (master positioning Pillars 1 + 2) | Decline reversal + convention announcement (Hybrid variant Pillar 1) |
| Avg deal cycle | 4–6 months (per `my-gtm-context.md` Section 8) | 8–14 weeks (existing relationship + simpler integration) |
| Channel mix | Email (primary) + LinkedIn (secondary) + phone (late-sequence) + DSA events | Direct relationship outreach (Cassie warm intro) + email + scheduled QBR conversion |
| Sequence length | 8–12 touchpoints over 3–4 weeks | 4–6 touchpoints over 2 weeks (warmer audience) |
| Personalization tier | Mostly Tier 2 (semi-personalized); top 25 are Tier 1 (fully custom) | Always Tier 1 (existing relationship; full personalization) |

Below: target list criteria + personalization framework + email sequences for both flows.

## Target List Criteria

Build a list of **100–200 net-new accounts** matching the ICP. Refresh quarterly. Tools: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, Clay, ZoomInfo, Capterra.

**Filters (apply in order):**

1. **Industry / vertical**
   - Jewelry, accessories, beauty, nail, cosmetics, skincare, wellness, party plan, personal care
   - Direct selling / MLM company type confirmed (vs. retail or wholesale)
2. **Size**
   - 200–10,000 active reps (sweet spot: 1,000–5,000)
   - Exclude <200 (below ICP economic threshold) and >10,000 (enterprise tier — Rallyware territory)
3. **Geography**
   - US-primary (per `my-gtm-context.md`); Canada secondary; no international 2026
4. **Tech stack signal**
   - On Exigo (active migration target — Color Street precedent)
   - On Party Platform Solutions (price hikes; active displacement window)
   - On Thatcher (bankruptcy; clients in active migration)
   - On Shopify-as-back-office (TCO at scale becoming visible)
   - On custom-built back office (Color Street pattern — likely Wayroo-only)
   - On legacy CRM / generic tools (rep fragmentation pain at maximum)
5. **Pain signals (any of)**
   - Recent stylist complaints on Trustpilot, BBB, social
   - Public rep churn announcements
   - Recent leadership change (new VP Sales / CRO / CTO)
   - Hiring for "rep enablement," "field operations," "distributor relations" roles
   - Recent FTC inquiry or compliance-related news
   - DSA Partnership Award winner / DSN Premier Supplier — industry-engaged
6. **Reachability**
   - Champion contact identifiable (LinkedIn presence, email accessible)
   - Has attended DSA / ANMP / industry conferences in last 18 months
   - Has warm-connection path (partner referral, mutual LinkedIn, prior touchpoint)

**Tier classification:**
- **Tier A (top 25)** — Fully custom outreach. Examples to research: Trades of Hope, Chloe + Isabel, Origami Owl, Scout & Cellar (per `my-gtm-context.md` C&C beachhead candidates).
- **Tier B (next 50)** — Semi-personalized with industry/role hooks.
- **Tier C (remaining 100+)** — Template-based with one personalized line per message.

## Personalization Framework

| Tier | Treatment | Time investment per account | Reply rate target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier A** | Fully custom email with research from prospect's website, leadership LinkedIn, recent press, public stylist signals. Reference specific products, recent news, or hiring activity. Custom subject line. | 30–45 min per account | 10–15% |
| **Tier B** | Semi-personalized — industry / company-size / pain-pattern specific. Custom opening line + customized middle (1 personalized fact). Standard subject line. | 8–10 min per account | 5–10% |
| **Tier C** | Template-based — one personalized line (typically the opening hook referencing their company name + a pattern observation). Standard subject line. | 2–3 min per account | 2–5% |

**Across all tiers — never:**
- Use generic openers like "I hope this email finds you well"
- Open with "I came across your website" (everyone says this; reveals zero research)
- Include more than one CTA per email
- Send sequences from a no-reply or marketing@ address
- Pitch in the first email — the first email surfaces a pain or insight; the second adds value; the third asks for the meeting

## FLOW 1: New-Logo Outbound Sequence

**Owner:** Daniel Lang
**Sequence length:** 11 touchpoints over 21 days
**Channels:** Email + LinkedIn + phone
**Target reply rate:** 8–12% (across all tiers blended)
**Target meeting-booked rate:** 3–5% of contacts

### Touchpoint Map

| Day | Channel | Action |
|-----|---------|--------|
| 0 | LinkedIn | Connection request (no pitch) |
| 1 | Email | Email 1 — Problem-focused opener |
| 3 | LinkedIn | Engage on prospect's recent post (like + thoughtful comment) |
| 5 | Email | Email 2 — Insight + Paparazzi proof |
| 7 | LinkedIn | Direct message (referencing email 1) |
| 10 | Email | Email 3 — Specific value + meeting ask |
| 12 | Phone | Phone call 1 (referencing email 3) |
| 14 | Email | Email 4 — Different angle (segment-specific pain) |
| 17 | Phone | Phone call 2 |
| 19 | Email | Email 5 — Break-up email ("should I close the loop?") |
| 21 | LinkedIn | Final LinkedIn message |

### Email Sequence Copy

#### Email 1 — Problem-Focused Opener (Day 1)

**Subject line options (test):**
- A: *"Quick question about [Company]'s rep tools"*
- B: *"[Company] reps + Square + Excel + Venmo — sustainable?"*
- C: *"Watching [Company] grow — one observation"*

**Body (under 100 words):**

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Saw [specific recent thing — product launch, leadership change, rep milestone, conference appearance]. Quick observation: most [vertical] DSOs at [company size] reach a point where their reps are running their business across 4–6 disconnected tools (Square + Excel + Venmo + Shopify + Facebook), and that fragmentation becomes the bottleneck for rep activation and retention — not the cause everyone names ("rep adoption problem").
>
> Curious if that pattern resonates at [Company], or if you've solved this differently?
>
> [Daniel's first name]

**Personalization slots:**
- `[Company]` — Prospect company name
- `[vertical]` — Specific vertical (jewelry, nail, beauty, etc.)
- `[company size]` — Sized to the prospect (e.g., "2,000 reps")
- `[specific recent thing]` — Research-based hook (Tier A = specific; Tier B = industry/size pattern; Tier C = company name only)

**Why this email:**
- Opens with research signal (something specific to them)
- Reframes their problem (fragmentation as cause, not "rep adoption" as named problem)
- Ends with a question, not a pitch
- Under 100 words → respect for their inbox

---

#### Email 2 — Insight + Paparazzi Proof (Day 5)

**Subject line:** *"Re: [Email 1 subject]"* (threading keeps it visible)

**Body (under 130 words):**

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Following up on the rep-tool fragmentation thought from earlier this week.
>
> One data point you might find useful: 4,000 active selling stylists at one direct selling org (Paparazzi) generated **$31M in 2025 — 60%+ year-over-year growth** — running on one consolidated platform instead of the typical 5+ tools. The headline isn't the revenue; it's that consolidation directly reduced rep churn and accelerated activation.
>
> Most DSO leaders we talk to have framed this as "we need better rep training." It's almost always a tooling problem disguised as a training problem.
>
> Worth a 20-minute conversation to compare notes? I have [Thursday afternoon / Friday morning] open — would either work?
>
> [Daniel]

**Why this email:**
- Adds value (Paparazzi proof point) before asking for time
- Reframes again ("training problem" → "tooling problem disguised")
- Specific calendar offer (two options) → easier to say yes than "let me know when works"

---

#### Email 3 — Specific Value + Meeting Ask (Day 10)

**Subject line:** *"[Company] + rep economics — quick observation"*

**Body (under 130 words):**

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Quick follow-up. I'll be brief.
>
> Two specific things I'd want to walk you through in a 20-minute call:
>
> **1.** How DSOs at your scale are dropping their wholesale processing cost from ~3% to 1% by routing rep wholesale orders through ProPay balances (we call it Spendback — three DSOs have confirmed the savings)
>
> **2.** How "we don't own those customers" — a direct quote from a CEO at a 20,000-stylist DSO last week — gets structurally solved when rep customer records auto-create in the back office at point of sale
>
> Either of these worth 20 minutes?
>
> [Daniel]
>
> [Calendar link]

**Why this email:**
- Specific outcomes, not features
- References a real customer quote (anonymized) for credibility
- One clear CTA with calendar link

---

#### Email 4 — Segment-Specific Pain (Day 14)

**Subject line:** *"[Company] + your next stylist event"*

**Body (under 130 words):**

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Different angle. The strongest signal I've seen for whether a DSO is ready for a Wayroo conversation isn't the size of their compliance gap or their processing cost — it's whether they have an upcoming stylist convention, summit, or launch event.
>
> Conventions compress sales cycles dramatically. We're working with [reference DSO type — anonymized] right now where the convention announcement is the closing forcing function. Their leadership has positioned Wayroo as the announcement that turns a sales engagement story around.
>
> When's [Company]'s next stylist event? If it's in the next 6 months, this conversation is probably worth having now rather than after.
>
> [Daniel]

**Why this email:**
- Different angle from previous emails (avoids fatigue)
- Convention forcing function reframe (cross-account validated pattern — Color Street Paparazzi conference + Jordan Essentials ~July 22)
- Open-ended question creates space for reply without pressuring meeting ask

---

#### Email 5 — Break-Up (Day 19)

**Subject line:** *"Should I close the loop on this?"*

**Body (under 70 words):**

> Hi [First Name],
>
> I've reached out a few times and haven't heard back, so I want to respect your inbox.
>
> A couple options:
>
> **1.** This isn't a priority right now → I'll close the loop and check back in 90 days
>
> **2.** You're interested but timing is off → 20-minute call when you're ready ([calendar link])
>
> **3.** You'd like me to talk to someone else on your team → who should I reach?
>
> Just hit reply with the number.
>
> [Daniel]

**Why this email:**
- Break-up emails consistently get the highest reply rate in the sequence (sometimes 15–25%)
- Three concrete options remove ambiguity
- "Just hit reply with the number" is the lowest-friction CTA possible

### LinkedIn Touchpoints

#### LinkedIn 1 — Connection Request (Day 0)

> Hi [First Name], working with mid-market direct selling orgs on rep-tool consolidation — would love to connect and follow your work. No pitch incoming.

**Note:** Send connection request *before* email 1. Most prospects accept within 24-48 hours; gives LinkedIn signal context for later email/DM touchpoints.

#### LinkedIn 2 — Engagement (Day 3)

Like + thoughtful comment on prospect's most recent post. Comment should add value or perspective — NOT a thinly-veiled pitch.

Examples:
- If they post about rep success: *"Love seeing this — what's the activation curve looking like for first-time stylists? Curious if cycle time is changing."*
- If they post about industry trends: *"Agreed on [X]. The under-discussed angle is [Y]. Curious if you're seeing that play out at [Company]."*

#### LinkedIn 3 — Direct Message (Day 7)

> Hi [First Name], I sent you a couple emails this week (subject: [Email 1 subject]). LinkedIn is sometimes easier — happy to send context here if useful. The short version: 4K reps at one Wayroo client did $31M in 2025 by consolidating rep tools. Worth comparing notes?

#### LinkedIn 4 — Final Message (Day 21)

> Hi [First Name], closing the loop on outreach. If timing's wrong now, I'll check back in Q3. If timing's right, here's a 20-min slot: [calendar link]. Either way — appreciate you watching the space.

### Phone Touchpoints

**Phone 1 (Day 12) — After Email 3**

Opening (10 seconds):
> Hi [First Name], this is [Daniel] from ByDesign Wayroo. I sent you an email yesterday about Spendback and rep customer records — did you see it?

If yes → discovery questions (use script in `outputs/12-sales-call-script.md` when produced)
If no → "Totally fine. Quick context: we work with direct selling orgs on rep-tool consolidation. The two specific things worth your time are [Spendback economics + customer record flow-back]. Worth 20 minutes this week or next?"
If voicemail → "Hi [First Name], [Daniel] from ByDesign Wayroo. Following up on the email about [topic]. I'll try again next week, but easiest is [calendar link]. Thanks."

**Phone 2 (Day 17) — Follow-up call**

Opening: "Hi [First Name], it's [Daniel] following up one more time. I'll be brief — want to keep it focused on whether this is worth 20 minutes, not pitch you."

## FLOW 2: Existing-Client Wayroo Upsell Outbound

**Owner:** Cassie Lewis (account-warm relationships) + Autumn Fowers (product/demo)
**Sequence length:** 4–6 touchpoints over 14 days
**Channels:** Direct email (warm relationship) + scheduled QBR conversion + occasional Daniel-sourced introduction
**Target reply rate:** 25–40% (warm audience)
**Target meeting-booked rate:** 15–25% of contacts

### Approach Difference vs. Flow 1

Existing-client outbound is materially different:
- Existing relationship → no cold-outreach friction
- Cassie is the relationship anchor; she carries the trust
- The reframe is from "discovery" to "expansion conversation"
- Hybrid DSO positioning lead is **decline reversal + convention announcement** (NOT rep tool fragmentation — that's a new-logo pain frame)

### Touchpoint Map

| Day | Channel | Action |
|-----|---------|--------|
| 0 | Email | Email 1 — Pattern observation + open invitation |
| 4 | Email or call | Follow-up if no response (Cassie or Daniel-sourced warm intro) |
| 7 | Scheduled QBR or strategic call | Cassie inserts Wayroo discussion into existing cadence |
| 10 | Email | Email 2 — Hybrid DSO positioning (Recovery Story + Spendback math) |
| 14 | Calendar | Schedule formal Wayroo introduction call with Founder + VP Sales |

### Email Sequence Copy

#### Email 1 — Pattern Observation (Day 0)

**From:** Cassie Lewis
**To:** Founder + VP Sales (separate emails or single email cc'd; Cassie's judgment based on existing relationship)
**Subject:** *"Something we should talk about — [Company] + Wayroo"*

> Hi [Founder First Name],
>
> Quick note. We've been seeing a specific pattern across the existing Freedom client base — DSOs with a minority cash-and-carry rep cohort (typically 10–30%) that's growing slower than the rest of the rep base, and a leadership team that's been quietly watching engagement decline over the last 6–12 months.
>
> If that pattern resonates at [Company], we should talk. We've just worked through a Wayroo upsell scenario with [reference type — anonymized — "another personal care DSO" or similar] where the convention announcement became the lever to turn the curve around. The math on customer data ownership + Spendback wholesale-processing savings made the deal viable.
>
> Would a 30-minute strategic conversation in the next 2 weeks be useful?
>
> [Cassie]

**Why this email:**
- Opens with cross-account pattern (not generic value pitch)
- References Jordan Essentials anonymized (becomes case study language when JE closes)
- Specifies "strategic conversation" — signals this isn't a sales pitch, it's an expansion discussion
- Time-bounded ("next 2 weeks") creates lightweight urgency

#### Email 2 — Hybrid DSO Positioning (Day 10)

**Subject:** *"Re: [Company] + Wayroo — quick economic snapshot"*

> Hi [Founder First Name],
>
> Following up. Three concrete data points I want to put in front of you before we talk:
>
> **1.** Customer auto-creation — every customer your reps sell to becomes a record in your existing Freedom back office. Automatically. No sync. **Nancy at Jordan Essentials called this a "huge benefit to the DSO"** when she saw it last week.
>
> **2.** Spendback economics — your wholesale processing fee drops from ~2.55%+ to 1% on every order paid through rep ProPay balances. At [Company]'s wholesale volume, that's [estimated savings] annually at modest adoption.
>
> **3.** Convention forcing function — if [Company] has a stylist event in the next 3–6 months, Wayroo is the announcement story. We've seen this work as the deal-closing accelerant in multiple existing-client expansions.
>
> Want 30 minutes? [Calendar link]
>
> [Cassie + Autumn]

**Why this email:**
- Three specific value claims, each with proof
- References Nancy (named customer when Jordan Essentials publishes; anonymized until then)
- Specific savings estimate (requires per-prospect modeling before send)
- Co-signed by Cassie + Autumn (account + product)

## Metrics Dashboard

Track per campaign, per tier, per sequence. Review weekly.

| Metric | Flow 1 Target | Flow 2 Target | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Connection request acceptance** | 30–40% | N/A (warm) | Quality of profile/positioning at the LinkedIn layer |
| **Email open rate** | 40–60% | 60–80% | Subject line + sender reputation |
| **Email reply rate (all)** | 8–12% | 25–40% | Email content + targeting fit |
| **Positive reply rate** | 3–5% | 15–25% | Whether the message actually resonates |
| **Meetings booked per 100 contacts** | 3–5 | 15–25 | The headline outbound funnel metric |
| **Meeting → opportunity conversion** | 30–50% | 50–70% | Whether discovery → real opportunity (depends on Phase 6 messaging fit) |
| **Opportunity → close** | 20–30% | 40–60% | Late-stage sales execution |
| **Pipeline generated per campaign** | Track quarterly | Track quarterly | The business outcome metric |

**Track in CRM (Salesforce per `my-gtm-context.md`):**
- All touchpoints logged per account
- Reply quality coded (positive / neutral / negative / not interested / wrong contact)
- Disposition reason on every "not interested" reply (timing / not a fit / using competitor / no budget / other)

**Disposition codes feed Phase 6 Task 5 messaging testing** — patterns in "wrong contact" or "not a fit" replies indicate targeting drift; patterns in "using competitor" replies feed competitive battlecard updates.

## What's Not in This Campaign (Out of Scope)

- **Inbound lead nurture sequences.** This deliverable is outbound-only. Inbound nurture (Capterra leads, website demo requests, etc.) is a separate workstream.
- **Partner referral outreach.** Daniel's 71.4% partner conversion (per gtm-context) suggests partners are a higher-leverage channel than cold outbound. Partner outreach is covered in Task 5 (Partnership Program).
- **Direct-to-rep (Track 2) outbound.** This is DSO corporate outreach only. Rep-facing acquisition is a future Track 2 motion not yet developed.
- **ABM-tier orchestration.** Tier A in this campaign is the *closest* to ABM but isn't a full ABM program. Full ABM with multi-channel orchestration, account-level ad campaigns, and direct mail is covered in Task 8.
