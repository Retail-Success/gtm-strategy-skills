## The One-Sentence Frame

> **"Aice becomes the brand the whole thing runs under. We put a payments-and-commerce engine beneath it that would take you years and millions to build — and you keep equity in something far bigger than standalone Aice."**

Internally this is a merger with ByDesign as majority owner. **Brian never hears "acquisition."** He hears *a partnership of equals where his brand leads.*

---

## Meeting Objectives

**Primary objective:** Get Brian emotionally bought into the JV *structure* — that keeping the Aice brand on top and taking equity in a combined entity beats both (a) standalone Aice and (b) holding out for a clean acquisition. Walk out with his verbal "I want to keep talking about this shape."

**Secondary objectives (in priority order):**
1. **Rapport & credibility.** Scott and Shannon establish that ByDesign is a serious, 25-year, $7B-payouts operator Brian would be proud to build with — not a suitor trying to swallow his company.
2. **Make the white-label payments/POS engine real.** Brian leaves picturing Aice with instant POS + payment rails under the hood.
3. **Surface his decision criteria.** What does Brian actually want — exit, control, legacy, a bigger swing? We can't structure the deal until we know.
4. **Agree a next step + owner** (joint working session on structure/economics with Morgan).

**What success looks like:** Brian leans in on the brand-leads framing, asks "how would the equity actually work," and agrees to a follow-up. **Not** success: a signed anything, or a valuation debate.

**What to avoid:** Negotiating the equity split live, saying "acquisition," diminishing Aice's brand or his role, or drowning him in the nine-motion revenue mechanics. Today is *shape and trust*, not *terms*.

## Why a JV Is the Harder Sell (read this before the room)

Founders default to wanting a **clean exit** — cash, done, move on. A JV asks Brian to *stay in, share control, and bet on a combined entity.* That is a harder yes than "name your number." So every talking point below exists to make the JV upside so compelling he *prefers* it to holding out for an acquisition. The four pillars in §"Core Pitch" are the levers that do that work.

## The Core Pitch — Four Upside Pillars

These are the heart of the meeting. Each is a reason the JV beats a clean exit.

### Pillar 1 — Your brand survives and becomes the parent face
- In an acquisition, Aice gets absorbed and disappears. Here, **Aice is the brand everything sits under** — Wayroo, ByDesign, the whole stack runs as the engine *beneath the Aice name.*
- "You've built 'side gig to main gig' into 100k users and 20,000 new reps in four months. We're not buying that to bury it — we want it to be the front door for a much bigger company."
- Founder-led brand equity is preserved and *amplified*, not erased.

### Pillar 2 — Instant POS + payments (a stack that would take you years)
- Aice today is the rep's **top of funnel** — attract, communicate, nurture. The one thing it doesn't have is the **transaction backbone**: POS, payments, payouts, inventory, invoicing, merchant accounts, compliance-grade transaction records.
- We hand you a **mature, in-market commerce engine, white-labeled under Aice** — native mobile POS (barcode + tap-to-pay coming), real-time inventory, next-day payouts, individual rep merchant accounts (Dropship — no competitor has it), and FTC/tax-grade transaction records.
- "Building this from scratch is years of engineering and payments/compliance infrastructure. We've already built it and it's live — 4,000 reps drove **$31M in sales last year** on it, up 60%+ YoY. You'd be shipping it under the Aice brand in months, not years."
- This closes Aice's structural Shopify dependency (3% rate, single merchant account, liability) with a native stack.

### Pillar 3 — Payments rev-share you can't access alone
- The combined entity earns **payments revenue on every transaction** — not just license fees. That's the highest-LTV, recurring line in the whole business.
- ByDesign already holds processor relationships with **Nuvei, ProPay, Authorize.Net, and Adyen** — economics a company at Aice's stage simply can't get on its own.
- "Your ~100k users and your DSO-startup pipeline become a payments-volume engine. That revenue line doesn't exist for Aice standalone — it only opens up inside this combination."

### Pillar 4 — Equity + shared revenue in a bigger entity
- "A minority stake in a combined company that shares **all** revenue lines — license, subscription, *and* payments — is worth more than 100% of standalone Aice."
- You're not trading your company for a check and walking away from the upside. You're swapping into a **larger, faster-compounding entity** and keeping skin in the game as it scales.
- This is the answer to "why not just acquire me": an acquisition caps your upside at the purchase price; the JV keeps you exposed to the combined growth.

## What "White-Labeling Wayroo's POS + Payment Rails" Means (be concrete)

When Brian asks how this actually works, keep it simple:
- **The Aice app stays the Aice app.** Same brand, same UX surface his users love.
- **Underneath, the commerce actions route through Wayroo's engine:** a rep takes a card payment, runs a vendor-event sale, sends an invoice with a payment link, gets paid next-day, manages inventory — all powered by Wayroo + our processor rails, but presented as Aice.
- **Campfire / Medusa commerce gets a real payments-and-payouts backbone** instead of a Shopify dependency.
- **DSO + rep transaction volume** flows through ProPay/Nuvei/Adyen → payments rev-share to the combined entity.
- Net: Aice goes from "great front end, no checkout" to "the rep's whole business in one branded app" — without Aice writing the hard part.

Frame it as a **merge, not a bolt-on integration**: the engine becomes part of Aice, not a third-party widget.

## Anticipated Objections & Responses

**"Why a JV instead of just buying me out / why wouldn't I want a clean exit?"**
> "We could have that conversation — but we think you'd be leaving the best part on the table. A clean exit caps you at a number today. This keeps your brand leading and keeps you owning a piece of something much bigger as it compounds. We'd rather build it *with* you."

**"If ByDesign is the majority owner, am I just an employee with a logo?"**
> "No — the structure is built so Aice leads the market and you lead the brand. We'll define governance and your role explicitly with Morgan; majority ownership is about capital and control of the combined entity, not about sidelining you." *(Don't over-promise specific control terms — commit to defining them, not to a number.)*

**"What happens to my team / my users?"**
> "Your users are the reason this works — they become the demand engine for the payments and commerce layer. Your team builds the front end they're best at; we run the rails. Nobody's redundant — the two halves are complementary, not overlapping."

**"How is the equity split / valuation decided?"**
> "That's exactly the work we'd do next, with Morgan, off a real financial model — not something to pin down in this room. We wanted you to meet the people and the shape first."

**"Won't 'under the hood' bury my brand the way an acquisition would?"**
> "Opposite. In an acquisition your brand is the thing that disappears. Here it's the thing on top — Wayroo and ByDesign are the names *nobody sees.* Aice is the name in the market."

**"My ecommerce already works via Shopify — why do I need your rails?"**
> "Shopify gives you a checkout but it costs you — 3% and a single merchant account you carry the liability on. Our rails give every rep their own merchant account, next-day payouts, POS, and a payments rev-share line that becomes *your* revenue, not Shopify's cost."

## Landmines — What NOT to Say

- ❌ The word **"acquisition"** (internal structural reference only).
- ❌ Anything that frames Aice as the smaller/absorbed party.
- ❌ A specific equity split or valuation number.
- ❌ The full nine-motion revenue mechanics (overwhelming; Morgan holds these in reserve).
- ❌ "Wayroo will be visible to your users" — the engine is *under the hood*; Aice is the face.
- ❌ Pushing for a signature or a deadline today.
