## The one sentence

> **"Wayroo extends your back office to the field. It's one app your reps run their whole business on, and every sale they make comes back to you as data."**

This is the opener that was message-tested across **15+ Wayroo demos**. *"Extends your back office"* produced feature pull ("well, can it do *this*?"). Opening with compliance got *"that's not really our business"* and the call was effectively over. With a non-client, say **"your back office"**, never "Revolution."

## The pitch: ~3 minutes, spoken

Deliver it after 5–10 minutes of discovery, not cold. The bracketed lines are where you plug in what they just told you.

> **[1. Their world, 30 sec]**
> "Here's what we see at almost every company your size. Your reps run their business across six to ten apps: Square at the table, Venmo or PayPal for everything else, a spreadsheet for inventory, Canva for graphics. At Jordan Essentials, reps told us they spend at least $60 a month of their own money on that stack. Industry-wide it's $60 to $100. *[You mentioned ___.]*
>
> None of those apps know what the rep ordered from you. And none of them tell you what she sold."
>
> **[2. The problem, 30 sec]**
> "So you get three things. Reps lose sales at the moment of purchase: wrong payment type, no reader, the app didn't load. Reps spend their time on admin instead of selling: shipping labels, spreadsheets, reconciling Venmo. One CEO put it to us as *'none of these activities generate revenue.'* And you never see the customer. Every cash or Venmo sale is a buyer your marketing team can't reach. One CTO told us flatly, *'We don't own those customers.'*"
>
> **[3. What Wayroo is, 30 sec]**
> "Wayroo extends your back office to the field. It's one app where your rep takes a card on her phone, sends a branded invoice with a payment link, runs her own storefront, and sells at a vendor event. Her inventory is already loaded from her wholesale order and updates itself as she sells. She doesn't touch a spreadsheet.
>
> It sits on top of the back office you already run. *[If Exigo: 'We integrate with Exigo.']* You don't replace anything."
>
> **[4. What you get back, 30 sec — tailor to the persona, see routing below]**
> "Every sale your reps make in Wayroo becomes a record you can see: who sold what, to whom, how fast stock is moving. Customers your reps create show up on your side, so for the first time you can market to the people your field has been selling to for years. And every rep runs her own merchant account, so chargebacks land with the rep, not on your merchant account."
>
> **[5. Proof, 30 sec]**
> "Jordan Essentials launched Wayroo at their convention in July. 764 reps, nearly 30% of their active field, downloaded it within two or three days. The deal took about 11 weeks, not the usual four to six months, and the founder asked to start the paid contract early. *[Verbal only, until published:]* At Paparazzi, about 4,000 reps on Wayroo generated $31 million in sales in 2025."
>
> **[6. The ask, 15 sec]**
> "So here's what I'd suggest. A 45-minute technical call with whoever owns your back office, so we can map exactly how Wayroo connects to your system. Then we size it against your real sellers, not your total rep count, and line the launch up with your next convention or event. When's your next big field event?"

**Pacing:** stop after beats 1 and 2 and ask *"does that sound like your field?"* If they say yes, keep going. If they push back, you've learned their real problem, so go back to discovery.

## Persona routing: who's in the room changes beat 4

| Persona | Their priority | Lead beat 4 with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder / CEO** | Field productivity and retention | The extension frame, then **reps sell more and spend less time on admin**. Then customer records come back to you. | Merchant-account mechanics, implementation detail, anything that sounds like a project. **Never open with compliance.** |
| **VP Sales / Field Ops** | Visibility and coaching | **"Every rep sale becomes a record you can see."** Inventory levels and sell-through per rep, so you coach the rep with stuck stock before she quits. | Payments mechanics |
| **CTO / back-office owner** | One source of truth, integration risk | **Wayroo is downstream of your system of record, not a second one.** At Color Street the CTO endorsed integrating with their commission engine rather than adding a third sync surface. | Overpromising timelines. Defer architecture to the technical call. |
| **CFO / Finance** | Cost and liability | **Rep-owned merchant accounts:** chargebacks and processing on rep sales sit with the rep. **You set the rep price and keep the spread** on the per-rep fee. | Rep excitement, download numbers |

**Two routing rules from the messaging house:**
- **Match their word for reps:** stylists, consultants, ambassadors, whatever they use. Don't say "users" or "distributors."
- **If the DSO believes it has no cash and carry**, don't say "point of sale." Say **"a field tool that gives you data back,"** and lead with invoicing, payment links, and the storefront.

## Discovery hooks: ask these before you pitch

Keep it to five or six. Each answer feeds a specific beat.

| # | Question | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What back office are you on, and who owns it day to day?" | Beat 3 + the technical-call ask |
| 2 | "When a rep sells at a party or a vendor event, how does she take payment today?" | Beat 1 (the app stack) |
| 3 | "How much time do your reps spend on admin each week, and how much of that makes them money?" | Beat 2 (validated across 4 accounts) |
| 4 | "When a rep sells to someone outside your site, does that customer ever show up on your side?" | Beat 4 (customer records) |
| 5 | "Of your active reps, roughly how many are real sellers versus buying for themselves?" | Sizing. Color Street said 80/20. Price against real sellers. |
| 6 | "When's your next convention or big field event?" | The close. JE closed in about 11 weeks against a convention date. |
| 7 | "Are you paying for a rep app today, like Hustle or Boards?" | Objection prep plus a budget signal (Hustle charges $5 per monthly active rep) |

## Objection handling (acknowledge → explore → respond)

| Objection | Explore | Respond |
|---|---|---|
| **"Exigo already has a rep app."** | "What do your reps use it for today?" | "Right, and Wayroo works alongside Exigo. The question is whether your reps take a card, invoice, and run their inventory in it, or whether they're still on Square and Venmo. If it's the latter, that's the gap we fill." *(Never claim Exigo has no rep app.)* |
| **"Square is free and does this."** | "Is that what most of your reps use?" | "Square is good at POS and basic inventory. What it doesn't do is know your rep's wholesale order, load her inventory automatically, or send the customer and the sale back to you. **The connection to your back office is the product.**" |
| **"We already pay for Hustle / Boards."** | "What do you get out of it, and what's missing?" | "Wayroo has a media library too, but that isn't why people buy it. Hustle and Boards don't transact. Wayroo is where the rep actually sells and gets paid. Jordan Essentials is moving off Boards now." *(Don't claim feature parity with Hustle.)* |
| **"Our reps won't adopt another app."** | "What happened last time you rolled one out?" | "764 Jordan Essentials reps downloaded it within days of the convention, without a mandate. Reps adopt it because it replaces apps they're already paying for themselves." |
| **"What does it cost?"** | "How do you charge your reps for tech today?" | "There's a per-rep fee and a small share of payments. Most companies fold the per-rep fee into the tech fee they already charge reps. **You set the rep price and keep the spread**, so it becomes a margin line, not a cost line. We'll model it on your real-seller count in the proposal." |
| **"Integration sounds like a big project."** | "What made the last integration painful?" | "That's exactly what the technical call is for. Wayroo reads from your system of record. It doesn't replace it or compete with it. We'll come back with a scoped plan, not a guess." |
| **"Is this a compliance pitch?"** | — | "No. It's a selling tool. The fact that every sale leaves an auditable record is a nice side effect." *(Only go deeper if they bring up FTC or 1099s themselves.)* |

## Claim guardrails: say this, not that

| ✅ Safe to say | 🚫 Don't say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "764 reps downloaded within 2–3 days of the convention" | "...and still growing" | Nothing after the launch window has been measured (`my-gtm-context.md` §7) |
| "~4,000 reps on Wayroo generated $31M in 2025" *(verbally only)* | Paparazzi on a slide, or "Paparazzi's whole field" | The case study is unpublished and approval hasn't been requested. The field is ~20,000, so $31M came from ~20% of it. |
| "Extends your back office to the field" | "A compliance platform" or opening with FTC | Message-tested: the compliance opener ends calls |
| "The connection to your back office is the differentiator" | "Better inventory management" | Free Square does basic inventory well |
| "Works alongside Exigo" | "Exigo has no rep app" or "we're the only ones doing custom dev" | Both are false (verified 2026-09-24) |
| Tap-to-Pay, invoicing with payment links, storefront, vendor-event POS, customer records back to the DSO | **Dropship** or **Back Office Lite** as available today | Both are unshipped. Mention Dropship only as roadmap, if asked. |
| "Rep-owned merchant accounts shift chargebacks off you" | Specific ProPay rates | Rates are quoted per portfolio in the proposal (Color Street is still owed one) |
| Nothing about Aice | Anything for or against Aice | Partnership/acquisition is unresolved. Stay neutral and don't build on it. |
| — | "Free," "revolutionary," "empowering" | It's a paid product for new logos, and the voice rules ban hype words |
