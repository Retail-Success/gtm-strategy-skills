# The Rep Layer — where the fight actually is

| Alternative | What it is | Price to rep | Genuine strength | Where it stops | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Doing nothing** | Cash, Venmo, memory, a spreadsheet | $0 | **Zero friction, zero learning.** No decision required. | Nothing connects; no record; sales lost at the table | **Highest — this is the majority behaviour** |
| 🔴 **Square** | Free POS + basic inventory | **$0** | **Genuinely good.** Mature, trusted, spreadsheet upload, categories, tax codes. **📊 Confirmed as the actual substitute: 5 of 7 Paparazzi non-adopters use it.** | **Knows nothing about the company the rep sells for.** No wholesale order, no inventory from corporate, no record reaching the home office. | **High** |

## The honest composite

**A rep runs Square or Venmo for money, her head or a spreadsheet for stock, and logs into the back office when she has to. Nothing connects.**

> ### The competitor is a habit, not a product.
> Which means the fight is **switching cost and first-run experience**, not feature comparison. A rep does not choose Wayroo over Square — she either changes what she does on Saturday, or she doesn't.

> ### 📊 Evidenced 2026-08-30 — and the loss mechanism is not what a competitive analysis would predict
>
> Paparazzi Premiere survey ([`02-survey-results.md`](02-survey-results.md)), 7 non-adopters:
>
> | Why they didn't adopt | Mentions |
> |---|---|
> | 🔴 **"Getting started seemed too complicated"** | **6** |
> | Already using another payment solution | 2 |
> | What they use instead: **Square (5)** · Cash App (1) · Zelle (1) | |
>
> **Nobody said the product was worse. Nobody compared features. They lost to setup friction and then settled on Square.**
>
> **Strategic consequence: we do not lose to Square on capability — we lose to Square by not getting started, and Square is what fills the gap afterwards.** That makes onboarding a competitive weapon, not a UX chore. A "switch from Square" path aimed at reps who already stalled is a more valuable asset than any feature battlecard.
>
> ⚠️ **Sampling caveat:** these 7 attended a Premiere training workshop. They are the *warmest* possible non-adopters. **The cold majority is likely worse, not better.**

# Two Claims That Do Not Survive Contact

> ### 🚫 Do not compete with Square on **subscription** price.
> Square's free tier is **$0**. Wayroo Essentials is **$0 incremental**. Leading on the monthly price invites the obvious reply and loses the room. **Against Square, integration is the strongest argument.**
>
> ✅ Subscription price *does* differentiate against **Hustle ($5/monthly-active-rep) and Boards.** Use it there.

> ### 🔴 Correction 2026-09-02 — the transaction fee is a real argument we ruled out too broadly
>
> "Never compete on price with Square" was written about the **subscription**. It got applied to **everything**, and that cost us a legitimate point.
>
> | | Square | Wayroo |
> |---|---|---|
> | Monthly subscription | **$0** *(some tiers charge)* | $0 |
> | 🔴 **Per transaction** | **~2.6% + 10¢ on every sale** | ⚠️ **Rep rate not documented anywhere in this library** |
>
> **A rep running $1,500/month through Square pays roughly $40 in fees she has probably never totalled** — and pays it whether or not the month was good.
>
> ### ✅ Resolved 2026-09-02 — the rate is roughly level, and that is the better answer
>
> **Our rep-facing rate is only slightly below Square's** — not enough to move anyone.
>
> **So price is not the argument, and saying so out loud is the argument.**
>
> > ## **"Roughly the same rate — and it does what Square can't."**
>
> ### Why this beats winning on price
>
> A price advantage is temporary, checkable, and puts you in a spreadsheet next to a company with more scale. **A structural capability gap is none of those things.**
>
> **Square will never have the rep's back-office data or her inventory** — not as a roadmap gap, but because it has no relationship with the company she sells for. **No amount of Square product investment closes it.**
>
> | ✅ Say | 🚫 Don't |
> |---|---|
> | *"About the same rate."* | *"We're cheaper."* — technically true, materially not, invites arithmetic |
> | *"The difference is it's connected to your company."* | *"Square is expensive."* |
> | *"Square doesn't know what you ordered, and nothing you sell on it reaches your back office."* | Any framing that makes this a price fight |
>
> **Conceding the price comparison is what makes the capability argument credible.** A rep who has heard "we're cheaper" from four apps this year discounts it automatically.

> ### 🚫 Do not claim inventory management as a differentiator.
> **Square does basic inventory well** — spreadsheet upload, categories, tax codes. A rep who has used Square will say so.
>
> ✅ **The sync is the differentiator, not the feature.** Never demo inventory as a capability. **Demo it as already populated.**

# The One Durable Claim

> ## Wayroo is the only selling app already connected to the company the rep sells for.

| Alternative | Can claim it? |
|---|---|
| Square · Hustle · Boards · PayPal · Excel · doing nothing | ❌ **None** |
| The DSO's own back office | Has the data — **not in her pocket, not at the table, not a card reader** |

**No competitor reaches this without building an integration to ByDesign.** Everything else in the positioning is downstream of it, which is why USP 1 is the whole position.

**The white space is the top of the chart, and it is empty.** Wayroo is alone there — not because the product is broader, but because connection is structural and nobody else has the integration.

⚠️ **Being alone in a quadrant is only valuable if reps care about the axis.** *"Connected to my company"* is not something a rep asks for by name. **She asks for her inventory to be right and her card payments to work** — which is what connection delivers. **Sell the outcome, not the axis.**

# Strategic Implications

1. **The real target is inertia.** Budget is not the barrier; changing Saturday behaviour is. **First-run is the whole battle.**
2. **Square is the honest benchmark.** Any claim that would not survive a rep saying *"Square does that free"* should be cut.
3. **Hustle and Boards are feature competitors we have partially answered and never measured.** Media Library is a shipped partial answer; **the teardown is still outstanding.**
4. **Paparazzi is both the biggest opportunity and the clearest competitive loss** — ~16,000 un-adopted reps at an account paying a competitor $5/rep.
5. **SMS is the clearest product gap.**
