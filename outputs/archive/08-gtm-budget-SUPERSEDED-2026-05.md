## Headline Finding

> **ByDesign spends $36,400/year on marketing against $8–10M ARR — roughly 0.4% of revenue — and the measured return on the largest line is 11:1.**
>
> **The binding constraint is not budget. It is sales capacity. Increasing spend without increasing capacity converts a cash constraint into a queue.**

Growth-stage B2B SaaS typically runs marketing at 10–20% of revenue; even a conservative benchmark is 5%. ByDesign runs at 0.4% — and 2025 Capterra returned $213,275 in closed revenue on $19,345 of spend.

That combination usually means "spend more immediately." Here it doesn't, and the reason is the single rep. Daniel carries a 4–6 month cycle alone. Doubling lead volume into a funnel with one closer produces aging leads, not revenue.

**The correct 2027 ask is therefore a person, not a media budget.** This is the strongest quantified argument in the plan and it should be made explicitly to leadership.

## Per-Channel ROI

**Important caveat on how to read this table.** Cycle math means most 2026 spend produces 2027 revenue. CAC and LTV:CAC below are calculated on a **full-cycle basis** — the revenue each channel eventually produces from spend committed in this window — not on 2026 bookings. Reading them as in-year returns will overstate everything.

LTV assumes $100K first-year contract value and a 4-year average client life (`my-gtm-context.md` §7 notes churn is driven by client bankruptcy rather than competitive loss — an unusually strong retention signal). **LTV ≈ $400K gross.**

| Channel | Cash | Time cost | Expected opps | Expected closes | CAC | LTV:CAC | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Installed base** | $0 | ~$12,000 | 4 | 2 | **$6,000** | **67:1** | <1 mo (upsell, no cycle) |
| **Partner network** | $2,500 + 15% comm. | ~$14,000 | 5 | 1 | **$16,500** + comm. | **~15:1** | 4–6 mo |
| **Capterra** | $12,060 | ~$6,000 | 10 | 2.9 | **$6,227** | **64:1** | 4–6 mo |
| **Exigo test** | $0 | ~$8,000 | 1–3 | 0–1 | n/a — buys evidence | n/a | n/a |
| Google Ads *(2025 actual)* | $17,000 | ~$3,000 | 5 | **0** | **∞** | **0:1** | never |

**Every retained channel clears the 3:1 target by more than an order of magnitude.** That is not a sign of exceptional marketing execution — it is a sign of **structural under-investment.** When every channel returns 15–67:1, the portfolio is too small, not well-optimized.

**And the reason it stays too small is capacity.** Capterra at 64:1 would justify 5–10x the spend on pure unit economics. It cannot absorb that, because ten additional opportunities per quarter would sit unworked in a one-rep pipeline.

## The 2027 Ask, Previewed

Stating this now because the Aug–Dec numbers make the case, and the exec doc should carry it.

**The argument is not "increase the marketing budget."** At 64:1 on Capterra, marketing efficiency is not the problem.

**The argument is that capacity gates everything.** The evidence:

| Evidence | Source |
|---|---|
| Every channel returns 15–67:1 | This document |
| The best-converting channel (71.4%) supplies 7.5% of opportunities | `my-gtm-context.md` §7 |
| A 4–6 month cycle run by one rep | §8 |
| 40 open opportunities carrying the entire 2026 revenue number | `08-funnel-projection.md` |
| Marketing at 0.4% of revenue vs. a 5–20% benchmark | This document |

**Recommended 2027 shape:**

1. **One additional sales hire — SDR or AE.** The highest-return investment available. Every channel is supply-constrained downstream of Daniel.
2. **A content owner who is not Sam** — unlocks inbound, the highest-ceiling channel (18/25 on fit, blocked entirely on resourcing).
3. **Only then, more media spend.** Increasing spend before capacity converts a cash constraint into a queue.
