# Patterns

| Pattern | Read |
|---|---|
| **The rep-facing layer is free or near-free** | Square, Canva, PayPal all have real free tiers. **Charging a rep directly for selling software is not the norm.** |
| **DSO-paid per-rep subscription is the paid model that works** | Hustle ($5/MAR across ~20,000 Paparazzi reps) and Boards both. **Direct evidence DSOs will pay per-rep.** |
| 🔴 **Monthly-active-rep is the smartest metric in the market, and it is Hustle's** | Aligns cost to usage, protects the DSO from paying for dormant reps, and scales with success. **We should have taken this metric.** |
| **Transaction fees are invisible** | Reps pay Square 2.6% without registering it as a bill. **Percentage-of-transaction is the least-resented pricing in this market.** |
| **Nobody prices on outcome** | No competitor charges on rep earnings or GMV. Whitespace — **and Wayroo's ~1.5% is the closest thing to it.** |

# Price Floor and Ceiling

| | |
|---|---|
| **Floor** | **$0.** Square sets it, and it is genuinely good. |
| **Ceiling, rep-paid** | **~$15/mo.** JE's rep tier is $15 all-in. Reps spend $60–100 total but across many small subscriptions — **no single rep tool commands a large share.** |
| **Ceiling, DSO-paid** | **$5/monthly-active-rep** — Hustle's number, paid at scale today |

# Where Wayroo Sits

> ### We are priced at the floor on the rep side and taking our margin somewhere reps do not feel it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rep-facing price** | **$0 incremental** — at the floor, tied with Square |
| **Actual revenue** | **~1.5% of transactions** — the invisible, least-resented model |
| **DSO-facing price** | **$0** — below Hustle's $5/MAR |

## Three consequences

**1. 🚫 Price is not a differentiator against Square.** Both are $0. Leading on price invites the obvious reply. **Against Square, integration is the entire argument** — and this mood board is why.

**2. ✅ Price *is* a differentiator against Hustle and Boards.** *"Wayroo Essentials is included; Hustle is $5 per active rep"* is true, sharp, and lands with a DSO already writing that cheque.

**3. 🔴 The pricing freedom is almost entirely gone, and that was deliberate.** Free-to-rep and free-to-DSO leaves exactly two levers: **the ~1.5% transaction rate**, and **the unpriced rung-2 upgrade.** Everything else has been spent.

# The Whitespace We Are Not Using

| Gap | Note |
|---|---|
| **Monthly-active-rep pricing** | Hustle proved DSOs pay it. **We gave the model away** — defensible as an adoption strategy, but it is a real forgone line. |
| **Outcome-based pricing** | Nobody charges on rep earnings. Wayroo's ~1.5% is adjacent; **an explicit "we win when your reps sell" frame is unclaimed.** |
| **The $15–30/mo rep tier** | Project Broadcast lives here. **If Wayroo Pro ever prices, this is the band** — and SMS is the most-requested missing feature. **That is not a coincidence.** |

---

# The Uncomfortable Observation

> ### White-label was the only paid Wayroo surface, and the pilot gives it away three times at a stated $25,000 each.
>
> **After that, the rep-facing business has no price at all** until rung 2 exists. Revenue is entirely transaction volume.
>
> **That is a coherent strategy** — payments scale with adoption, and adoption is what a free product buys. **But it means every pricing lever now depends on volume**, and volume depends on a channel whose conversion rate is unknown.
>
> **Pricing risk and channel risk are now the same risk.**
