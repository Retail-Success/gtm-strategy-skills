# Problem Space Deep Dive — Aice JV

**GTM author:** Sam Atieh
**Date:** June 22, 2026
**Entity:** Aice JV (ByDesign/Wayroo ~70–75% + Aice ~25–30%; Brian Juhl CEO) — **provisional, ~Q4 2026 close; hedge until close**
**Builds on:** Phase 1 OPE/Power Hour/SWOT (JV), the May 23 deck, installed-base data, and the existing Wayroo-era problem prioritization in `my-gtm-context.md` (§4).

> **What's new vs. the Wayroo-era problem map.** The merger adds a **third customer — the creator/individual seller** (Aice's native audience) — and surfaces a class of problems that *only an integrated entity* solves: the DSO's four-vendor stitching problem and the rep's "five tabs" problem. This map is organized by the three customers the JV serves, across four problem layers, with severity / frequency / current workaround.

Severity scale: **Hair-on-Fire (HoF)** / Important / Nice-to-Solve. Frequency: Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Occasional.

## Prioritized problems — the top 5 the JV is uniquely positioned to solve

Ranked by **severity × frequency × how badly the current workaround fails × how uniquely the *merged* entity solves it.**

1. **The DSO four-vendor stitching problem (and the rep's mirror: "five tabs").** No competitor spans funnel → commerce → payments → back office. *The single problem the merger exists to solve* — "one contract instead of four," "one surface, not five tabs." HoF, Daily, workaround fails badly, uniquely ours.
2. **Rep activation + churn as one growth problem.** Reps never activate, churn before first sale (installed-base data: 3–19% active). The all-in-one collapses time-to-first-sale; the back-office visibility makes coaching proactive. HoF, Daily.
3. **Payment friction + tool fragmentation at the rep level.** Lost sales at the point of purchase + admin time crowding out selling time. HoF, Daily/Weekly — and the payments engine that solves it is *also* the JV's primary revenue driver.
4. **The creator can't transact on the coaching surface (NEW).** Aice's 100K creators can attract but not sell/get-paid without a bolted-on Shopify stack. The JV puts commerce + payouts directly on the Aice surface — "sell on day one, on rails." HoF for the PLG motion; this is what makes the rep-paid PLG worth paying for.
5. **DSO cost + liability + compliance (Shopify 3%, chargebacks, FTC/tax, invisible customers).** Per-rep ProPay merchant accounts shift fees/liability off corporate, create the audit trail, and surface customer data. Severity varies by segment (HoF for C&C, Important elsewhere).

> **Note on the bridge:** problems #2–#4 are exactly where the **M7 PLG→enterprise** flywheel lives — a creator solves #4, becomes an activated rep (#2/#3), and rep density inside a DSO surfaces #1/#5 to the corporate buyer. The problem map *is* the flywheel.

## Validation Questions (for interviews — test these problems exist)

**For DSO leaders:**
1. "Walk me through every vendor and tool your reps and corporate team pay for today. Which ones talk to each other?"
2. "Of the reps who sign up, what % make a first sale within 30 days — and what happens to the ones who don't?"
3. "When a rep sells at a vendor event or off Venmo, what record do you have of that customer and that transaction?"
4. "What does Shopify (or your cart) actually cost you all-in — fees, liability, the second vendor — and have you ever priced the alternative?"

**For reps / creators:**
5. "Show me the last sale you made. How many apps did it touch from 'customer interested' to 'I got paid'?"
6. "When was the last time you lost a sale because of how you take payment — and what happened?"
7. "How much of your week is admin vs. actually selling — and which of those hours pay you?"
8. *(Creator-specific)* "You've built your audience in Aice — what stops you from selling to them right now, today?"
