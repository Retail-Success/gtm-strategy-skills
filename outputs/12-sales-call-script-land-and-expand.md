# Sales & CSM Call Script — Land-and-Expand

**Phase:** 12 — Executing Sales, Task 9 *(Land-and-Expand variant)*
**Date:** 2026-09-02 · **Owner:** Sam Atieh
**For:** Sales team (Dan, Autumn) and **CSM team (Cassie)** — existing ByDesign clients only
**Companion:** [`07-dso-launch-kit.md`](07-dso-launch-kit.md) *(the deck)* · [`06-persona-positioning.md`](06-persona-positioning.md) *(who's in the room)*
**Not this:** [`12-sales-call-script.md`](12-sales-call-script.md) is the **new-logo** script. Different motion, still valid, do not use it here.

> ### This is not a sales call, and running it like one loses it
>
> **Nobody is buying anything.** There is no budget ask, no contract, no procurement, no cycle. The rep adopts on her own; the DSO's only job is ProPay approval.
>
> **Which means the classic frame is wrong.** No BANT — there is no budget and no purchase authority to qualify. No discovery-then-demo arc — you are not uncovering a need they'll pay to solve. **The call is closer to an account check-in that ends with a form.**
>
> 🔴 **The single rule: never open cold.** Open with something true about *their* field.

---

# Who runs which version

| | **CSM (Cassie)** | **Sales (Dan, Autumn)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Relationship** | Existing, warm, ongoing | Existing account, less frequent contact |
| **Natural opener** | A regular check-in | A reason to reconnect |
| **Advantage** | Trust, and they take your call | Fresh attention, a "new thing" frame |
| **Risk** | Sounds like an upsell and damages the relationship | Sounds like a pitch and gets deferred |

**Both run the same middle and the same ask.** Only the opening differs.

# Before the call — four things to look up

| # | Look up | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 🔴 **Their ProPay status** | Determines whether this call is the ask, or a check-in |
| **2** | **Their Revolution Pro penetration** | *"254 of 2,614"* is a real number they have and rarely think about. **Never guess it out loud.** |
| **3** | **How many of their reps have already adopted** | The opener |
| **4** | 🔴 **Demand-counter taps, if the counter is live** | The strongest possible opener |

⚠️ **If you cannot answer #1 and #3, reschedule.** A call that opens *"I wanted to tell you about Wayroo"* is a pitch. A call that opens *"forty of your reps are already using it"* is a conversation.

# 1 · Opening (2–3 min)

## CSM version

> *"Before we get to the usual — something's happening in your field I thought you'd want to see. **[N] of your reps have started using Wayroo.** They found it themselves; we didn't run a campaign at them."*

## Sales version

> *"I wanted to grab you because something changed on our side that affects your field, and it doesn't cost you anything. **[N] of your reps are already on Wayroo.**"*

**Then, both:**

> *"It's included with the Revolution Pro they already pay for, so there was nothing for them to buy. I wanted to walk you through what they're getting — and there's one thing that needs you, which I'll come to."*

> ### Why flag the ask early
>
> **Naming that there's one ask, without naming it yet, buys the rest of the call.** An approver who doesn't know what's coming spends the meeting waiting for the catch. **"There's one thing, I'll come to it"** removes that.
>
> 🚫 **Do not say ProPay in the opening.** It converts a good-news call into a compliance project before they know why it matters.

---

# 2 · What their reps are getting (5–8 min)

**Lead with the persona that matches their field.** Ask if you don't know:

> *"Roughly what share of your reps carry their own stock versus selling from the catalog?"*

⚠️ **Whatever they answer, treat it as a hypothesis.** Every DSO we have been able to check was wrong about this — in both directions. It is also the number you can offer to measure for them later.

## If they carry stock (C&C-heavy)

> *"Her wholesale order shows up in the app already counted. No spreadsheet, no typing in SKUs. She sells at an event, inventory comes down on its own, and the sale lands back in your back office as a record."*
>
> *"Today she's doing that across Square, Venmo and a spreadsheet — and paying for most of it herself. **Reps tell us $60 to $100 a month across six to ten apps.**"*

## If they sell from the catalog (non-C&C)

> *"Even without inventory, she's getting her back office in her pocket — **sales reports, downline summaries, KPIs, your push notifications, and your media library assets — without logging into the back office.**"*
>
> *"That last part is the one reps react to. Nobody enjoys logging into a back office on a laptop to find out how their team did."*

## For both — the three DSO benefits

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| **"You'll see rep sales activity you've never had visibility into."** | "Corporate oversight" |
| **"Your notifications reach them where they're already working."** | "Engagement tooling" |
| **"Your media library means they post your photos, not theirs."** | "Brand governance" |

> 🔴 **Media library is a brand-control pitch to corporate and an "assets without a second login" pitch to reps.** Same feature, two arguments. Do not mix them up in the room.

# 3 · The economics (3–5 min)

> *"It costs you nothing. It's included with Revolution Pro, which your reps already pay for — you or them."*

**Pause. Let that land before the next part.**

> *"And there's an option, if you want it. **Wayroo Essentials is included for every Revolution Pro rep — that part you can't charge for and wouldn't want to.** But the selling tools, the ones your ProPay approval unlocks, are a different thing. **You could charge your reps $3 to $5 a month for those and keep it.** Jordan Essentials does something similar — they charge $5 and keep $2."*
>
> 🔴 **Why the distinction matters in the room:** charging for a tier *their own work unlocks* is defensible to their field. Charging for something we gave away free is not, and a rep will notice.

> ### 🔴 Then give them the recommendation, unprompted
>
> > *"What I'd actually suggest is launch it included first, see what adoption looks like, and price it later against a real number. If you add a fee before your field wants the app, you suppress the adoption that makes the fee worth having."*
>
> **Giving away the immediate upsell buys enormous credibility**, and it is also correct: a markup that suppresses adoption costs us both more than it earns them. **Say it out loud. It is the most trustworthy thing in this call.**

⚠️ **JE's numbers are a worked example, not a rate card.** They set their own price; so do these people.

---

# 4 · The ask (3–5 min)

> *"Here's the one thing. Everything I've described works today, with nothing from you. **What doesn't work yet is payments** — taking a card, invoicing, and **getting your reps their money the next day.**"*
>
> *"That needs a merchant account, so it needs ProPay approval on your side. **It's underwriting paperwork, not a purchase and not an IT project.**"*

## Lead with the payout, not the acceptance

> ### *"Your reps can't get paid the next day until this clears"* beats *"your reps can't take cards."*
>
> Taking a card solves her customer's problem. **Getting paid solves hers** — and a DSO leader hears the second one differently.

## The peer proof

> *"Jordan Essentials went through it. Paparazzi went through it. Same process — and we'll walk whoever owns it on your side through the whole thing."*

**A first-of-its-kind ask is a risk. A thing two peers already did is a process.**

## The close

> *"Who on your side owns something like that — Finance, or Ops?"*

🔴 **The paperwork owner is almost never the person in the room.** The close is an **introduction**, not a decision. Leave with a name.

# 4b · The founding-partner variant *(pilot conversations only)*

**Use this only in the three pilot conversations.** It replaces nothing above — it comes *after* the ProPay ask has landed, as the reason to do it now rather than eventually.

> ### 🔴 Say it exactly this way
>
> *"One more thing, and it's the reason I wanted you specifically. **We're rolling Wayroo out to every Revolution client — your reps will see it either way.** What we're doing separately is picking a small group of founding partners to launch it properly with, and for those we're building the app in your brand at no cost. Your name, your colors, in the app store. **That's a $25,000 build. There are three slots.**"*

## Why this frame and not a softer one

| ✅ This | 🚫 Not this |
|---|---|
| *"This is happening anyway — we're choosing who gets the branded version"* | *"We'd love you to try this"* |
| **An invitation** | **A request** |
| Removes their best objection *(why should we go first?)* before they raise it | Invites it |

🔴 **And be clear what the branding is for.** It is **not** payment for adopting Wayroo — adoption needs nothing from them and is already underway. **It is payment for going through ProPay onboarding and going first.** Say that out loud; it is the honest version and it is also the persuasive one.

## What we ask in return — name all three

1. **ProPay approval** — the paperwork
2. **A launch moment** — a convention session, or one email to their field
3. **Permission to name them as a reference** — 🔴 **agreed in writing at kickoff, not later**

## ⚠️ Expect this from the accounts that don't get a slot

> **"Why did they get free branding and we didn't?"**
>
> **Answer honestly and bound it:** *"Three slots, first movers, in exchange for being a named reference. That's closed now — branding is a paid build from here."*
>
> **Bounded scarcity is defensible. A vague "maybe later" is not** — it teaches every remaining account to wait.

# 5 · Objection handling

Structure: **acknowledge → explore → answer.** Never argue the first version of an objection; find out what's underneath it.

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| **"Why are you giving this away?"** | *"We're not — it's included with a subscription your reps already pay for. We make money when they sell through it. Our incentive and yours are the same number."* |
| **"What's the catch?"** | *"There isn't one, and I'd say so if there were. The catch, if you want to call it that, is that we only make money if your field actually uses it — so we're motivated to make that happen."* |
| 🔴 **"Why are you contacting our reps without asking us?"** | **The one that can go badly. Do not get defensive.** *"Fair. They're Revolution subscribers and this is included in what they pay for, so we made it available — but you should hear it from us and not from a rep, which is why I'm here. If you'd rather we changed how we reach them, tell me and we will."* |
| **"How much work is the ProPay piece?"** | Answer plainly and don't minimise. *"It's a form, financial details, and an underwriting review. Days, not weeks. Two of your peers have done it."* |
| **"Our reps already use Square."** | *"Most do, and Square's good. Our processing rate is about the same as theirs — I'm not going to pretend we win on price."* **Then the real answer:** *"What Square will never have is your back office. It doesn't know what a rep ordered from you, it can't fill her inventory, and nothing she sells on it reaches your system. That's not a feature they haven't built — they have no relationship with you to build it on."* 🚫 **Never claim we're cheaper.** Conceding price is what makes the rest land. |
| **"We tried a rep app before and adoption was terrible."** | *"What happened?"* **Then listen — this is the most useful thing they'll tell you.** Usually setup. *"That's the failure we designed against — hers is already loaded when she opens it. And it's free, so nobody has to be sold on it first."* |
| **"Can we brand it?"** | *"Not in the standard version — it ships unbranded so we can get it to everyone at once. Branding is available, and we're doing it at no cost for three founding partners — that's a $25,000 build."* **Say the number, and bound it to three.** |
| **"What about reps who aren't on Revolution Pro?"** | *"They'll see it and be prompted to subscribe — which is margin for you. At Jordan Essentials, 764 reps downloaded it and only 254 had Pro. **510 asked for a subscription nobody sold them.**"* |
| **"Send me something and I'll look at it."** | The deferral. *"Happy to — can I also get fifteen minutes with whoever owns the payments paperwork? That's the only piece that needs anyone, and it moves faster with them in the room."* **Trade the document for the introduction.** |
| **"Our CTO will have questions."** | 🔴 **Good — get his name.** *"He should. The short version is we consume from your system of record, we don't become a second one. I can get you a page on that, or fifteen minutes with someone technical."* See the CTO appendix in the deck. |

# 6 · Post-call — within the hour

- [ ] **Follow-up email** — what they get, the one ask, and the named next step
- [ ] **Log the ProPay owner's name.** If you didn't get one, the call didn't close.
- [ ] **Log their stated C&C share** — and flag it as self-reported
- [ ] **Log the objection you actually got.** Patterns across accounts are how this script gets better.
- [ ] **Note whether a CTO exists.** It changes the next conversation.
- [ ] Update the account row: ProPay stage, adopters, demand density

# What to never say

| 🚫 | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Free"** | Invites *"then why am I paying for anything?"*. It is **included**. |
| **ProPay in the opening** | Turns good news into a compliance project |
| **Tap-to-Pay as the lead** | Implies the ask before you've earned it |
| **"We're cheaper than Square"** | **Rate confirmed 2026-09-02: only slightly below.** Concede it — *"about the same rate, and it does what Square can't"* is the stronger line. |
| **"This makes you stickier with ByDesign"** | True, ours, and it turns a gift into a trap |
| **A date for anything** | The launch calendar is being reset |
| **A guess at their Pro penetration** | They know the real number. Being wrong costs the room. |
