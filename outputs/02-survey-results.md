> ### 🔴 Read the sampling caveat before quoting any number in this document

>
> | | |
> |---|---|

> | Recruitment | **Attendees of a Premiere training session** |
> | Tenure | **70% have used it more than a year** |

> **The 86% adoption figure is a property of the sample, not of Paparazzi.** Field penetration is **~4,000 of ~20,000 — roughly 20%.** These are not in conflict; they have different denominators. **Anyone who quotes 86% as adoption will be wrong by a factor of four.**

> **What this survey is genuinely good for:** how *habituated users* feel, which features they actually use, and — most valuably — **why a warm non-adopter still said no.**

# The Three Findings That Matter

## 🔴 1. Onboarding friction is the adoption barrier — now confirmed, not assumed

**Six of seven non-adopters said "getting started seemed too complicated." Not one said the product was bad.**

This is direct evidence for the single most load-bearing design claim in the positioning library:

> *"If first-run shows an empty state or a setup flow, the unbranded app is just another tool to configure, and USP 1 is destroyed at the only moment it can be proved."* — [`06-positioning-statement.md`](06-positioning-statement.md)

**These reps sat through a training session and still did not start.** If onboarding defeats a rep who attended a workshop about it, **it will defeat a cold rep reached through a Revolution placement without question.**

> ### This raises data-first first-run from a design preference to the highest-confidence product requirement in the plan.
> Assumption **A8** moves Certainty **3 → 4**. Assumption **A11** — that first-run actually shows populated data — becomes the most urgent thing to instrument, because **we now know what happens when it doesn't.**

## 🔴 2. Reply to Buy: 3 use it, 24 struggle with it

**The only feature where friction exceeds usage by 8x.** Every other feature shows normal friction on heavy use; this one is inverted.

**What it is not:** dissatisfaction. Overall rating is 4.5/5.
**What it is:** either nobody knows how it works, or it does not work the way reps expect.

**Cross-account context that sharpens it:** at Color Street, Brian B said of Reply to Buy *"we could train"* — his stylists do not live-sell. It was filed as a **capability expansion, not a pain reliever**. This survey shows that even at an account where it *is* available, **reps have not adopted it and 24 of them hit a wall.**

> **Reframe: Reply to Buy is not an under-marketed feature. It is an unlanded one.** Do not put it in acquisition creative. Fix or explain it first — and **check whether the 24 are describing a usability problem or a "what is this?" problem**, because the fixes are completely different.

## 🔴 3. Adoption rises with rank — independent evidence for the team-leader persona

**Director 100% → Star Consultant 80% → Consultant 50%.**

Higher rank in a direct selling organisation means a larger downline and higher personal volume. **This is independent corroboration of R2, the team leader, as the highest-adopting persona** — arrived at from a completely different account and method than the JE convention observation.

**Two readings, and they are not exclusive:**
- **Selection:** higher-volume sellers have more reason to push through the learning curve
- **Value:** the product delivers more to someone with a team and real volume

Either way the operational conclusion is the same, and it is the one the playbook already acts on: **lead with field leaders.** This survey moves R2 from *one account, observed* to **two accounts, two methods.**

> ⚠️ **The inverse is the warning.** Adoption is lowest exactly where onboarding friction bites hardest. **A cold Revolution placement reaches the whole rank distribution, not just Directors** — so the population most likely to see the ad is the population least likely to survive setup.

# What This Survey Cannot Tell Us

| Question | Why not |
|---|---|
| **What share of Paparazzi's field uses Premiere** | Sample is convention attendees. **~20% is the real figure.** |
| **Why the ~16,000 never adopted** | 🔴 **They are not in this sample.** The 7 non-adopters here attended a training session — the warmest possible non-adopters. |
| **Whether reps would pay** | Not asked. Premiere is DSO-provided. |
| **Rep tool spend** | Not asked — the $60–100 figure is still one account |
| **Whether branding matters** | Premiere **is** branded, and there is no unbranded comparison |
| **Transact rate or GMV** | Not asked, and not instrumented |

> ### The most valuable follow-up is the one this survey makes obvious and cannot answer
>
> **Ask the ~16,000 un-adopted Paparazzi reps why they have not started.** If a warm, workshop-attending non-adopter says *"too complicated,"* the cold majority almost certainly says something stronger — or does not know the product exists.
>
> **That is a re-engagement survey to a population we can reach in-product, at an account that is already ProPay-approved.** It is the highest-value research available and it costs a send.
