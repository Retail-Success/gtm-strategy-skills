> **What this is.** The questions worth asking a DSO leader in a hallway, on a first call, or at a booth — and what each answer actually tells you. Two of these came from Sam; the rest are the validated set from the prospect library plus the ones that pair with his two.
>
> **Two rules that govern all of it:**
> 1. **Never open with compliance.** Under Land-and-Expand there is no budget ask, so FTC framing reads as a threat instead of an offer (`my-gtm-context.md` §4). It is the *answer* to a question they ask, never the question you lead with.
> 2. **Never say "free." Say "included."** *Free* invites "then what's the catch."

## Part 1 — The Two You Brought

### Q1. *"What's the next big product you guys are gearing up for?"*

**Why it works.** It is the only rapport question on this list that a DSO executive genuinely enjoys answering — it is their favorite subject — and it hands you a date, a forcing function, and three separate pain surfaces without you having asked about a single one. It also passes the DSN test: nothing about it sounds like a vendor question.

**Ask it early — minute one or two.** It is the opener, not a discovery question.

**Then branch on the answer:**

| They say | What it tells you | Follow up with |
|---|---|---|
| A named launch with a date | **Forcing function.** Same structural urgency the Jordan Essentials convention created — an 11-week cycle instead of 4–6 months | *"How do reps get product in hand for launch day — do they pre-order, or do they wait like everyone else?"* |
| A whole new category or line | Catalog rotation + rep inventory load are both about to spike | *"What happens to last season's stock that's still sitting in rep garages?"* |
| *"We don't pre-announce"* | Fine — don't push. Get the cadence instead | *"Fair. How many launches a year is a normal year for you?"* |
| Nothing on deck | Different play entirely. This is a decline/recovery account, not a launch account | *"What's field engagement looked like the last couple of quarters?"* |

**The follow-up that does the actual work — ask it every time:**

> **"When the launch goes live, who can you actually email about it?"**

Let them answer. Most DSO leaders will describe their house list. Then:

> *"Right — but what about the customers your reps have been selling to for years on Venmo and cash? Do those people exist anywhere you can reach?"*

They don't. That is the [invisible customer base](../my-gtm-context.md) — the #4-ranked DSO problem (scored 14/15), and the one where two separate accounts (Nancy at Jordan Essentials, Edmond at Color Street) called the fix a "huge benefit" **unprompted**. A product launch is the single most vivid way to make the cost of that gap concrete, because it is the moment they most want a list they don't have.

**The three bridges a launch answer opens — pick one, not all three:**

| Launch pain | Wayroo answer | Say it as |
|---|---|---|
| Can't market the launch to rep-sold customers | Customer record auto-creates in the back office when a rep creates a customer in Wayroo | *"Every rep sale creates a customer record you can see. Next launch, that's a list."* |
| Launch assets go out and reps post whatever they want | Media library — approved assets pushed to the rep's phone | *"They post your photos instead of their screenshots of your photos."* |
| Old stock stranded in the field | Vintage/generic SKUs + rep-to-rep transfers | *"The stuff that didn't sell doesn't have to be dead — reps can move it between each other and you can see it happen."* |

> ⚠️ **Media library is a DSO-high / rep-low feature** (Jordan Essentials: Hope rated it high, ~700 reps didn't react). Use it with corporate. Never use it as a rep-adoption argument.

### Q2. *"Have you launched a TikTok Shop yet?"*

**Why it works.** It is a 2026 question, not a 2019 one, and it instantly sorts the room. It also puts you on the right side of the thing everyone at a social selling event is privately worried about: the sale happens somewhere corporate can't see, and now it's happening on a platform corporate doesn't own either.

**This is the hook, restated for social:** *"The social selling problem nobody's solved is that the sale happens in a DM and the company never sees it."* TikTok Shop doesn't fix that — it moves it. It puts the customer record on ByteDance's side of the wall instead of Venmo's.

**Branch on the answer — all four branches are useful:**

**(a) "Yes — corporate runs one."**
> *"How does a rep get credit when a customer buys there? Or do they?"*

This is the channel-conflict question and it is frequently a sore subject. If corporate sells direct on TikTok and the rep gets nothing, they have a field-morale problem they may not have named out loud yet. If the rep *does* get credit, ask how attribution works — it is usually manual, usually contested, and always a month behind.

**(b) "Yes — our reps sell on it / we have an affiliate program."**
> *"So when a rep sells a unit on TikTok out of her own stock — does that come off her inventory count anywhere you can see?"*

It doesn't. She is now running two sets of stock counts and two sets of income records, and corporate sees one of them. Follow with: *"How long until TikTok pays her out?"* — the payout delay is the rep-side wedge, and next-day payout is the answer.

**(c) "No, but we're looking at it / it's on the roadmap."** ← **highest-value branch**
> *"What's holding it up?"*

Then be quiet. The answer is almost always one of: rep channel conflict, income-claim and compliance exposure on creator content, or nobody owns it internally. Each of those is a real conversation and none of them requires you to pitch. If they name compliance, *that* is your invitation to talk about transaction records — asked for, not led with.

**(d) "No, and we're not going to."**
> *"Where does your field actually sell socially, then — Facebook lives, DMs, group chats?"*

You are back on home ground. Same problem, older platform. Go to the hook.

**The honest bridge — do not overclaim.** Wayroo does not integrate with TikTok Shop. There is no connector, and there is nothing about one in the roadmap. What you can say truthfully:

> *"We don't plug into TikTok Shop, and I wouldn't tell you we do. What we do is give the rep something to point the content at — her storefront or a payment link — so the transaction lands in a place where you get the customer, the record, and the inventory decrement. Right now that link goes to Venmo and you get none of the three."*

> 🔴 **Product/PM flag.** TikTok Shop appears nowhere in `my-gtm-context.md`, the competitive landscape, or any Phase 6/8/11 output. If Sam's question produces signal across the next 2–3 conversations, it is a genuine gap in the GTM library — a DSO with a live TikTok Shop is structurally a *fourth* rep sales channel we don't model. Capture verbatim answers via `analyzing-call-transcripts` and promote to a pattern in `prospects/_index.md` at two accounts.

## Part 2 — The Rest of the Bank

### Situation — how selling actually works there

1. **"Who's your back office?"** — *Ask this before anything else on a new logo.* It routes the entire conversation (Branch A: ByDesign client → it's already included. Branch B: Exigo or other → Wayroo runs on top, nothing gets ripped out).
2. **"Walk me through how one of your reps takes an order today — from the customer asking, to money moving."** — The single most productive open question on the list. Let it run.
3. **"Roughly what share of your reps carry their own stock versus selling from the catalog?"** — Establishes C&C vs. Hybrid vs. Non-C&C.
4. 🔴 **"What percent of your active reps are actually selling to customers?"** — Brian B (Color Street CEO) volunteered *"80% of our people are just the hobbyists that buy for themselves."* If that 80/20 split holds across accounts it changes deal economics fundamentally. **Probationary pattern — probe it every call until confirmed.**
5. **"How are your reps getting paid today — and what do they say about it?"**

> ⚠️ **On self-reported C&C share:** Jordan Essentials said ~20% and it was confirmed understated. Where ByDesign holds the back office, measure it rather than asking. Treat every self-reported number here as directional only.

### Problem — where the friction actually is

6. 🔴 **"How much time do your reps spend on admin per week — and how much of that generates revenue?"** — **Cross-account validated across four accounts.** Use it in every call. It is the cornerstone pain and it is the one they always have a story about.
7. **"What's your rep paying for out of pocket every month — Square, Canva, a texting tool, a spreadsheet?"** — Jordan Essentials quantified it at $60+/mo across Canva, ChatGPT, Square, Boards, Project Broadcast. You are looking for them to say a number.
8. **"Walk me through what happens in the first 30 days after a new rep signs up — do you feel that flow is working?"** — Brian B disclosed three operational gaps in one call when asked something close to this. Executives will admit debt if the question isn't accusatory.
9. **"When a rep goes quiet, how do you find out?"** — Usually: they don't, until the rep is gone.
10. **"We tried a rep app before and adoption was terrible" → "What happened?"** — If they raise this, it is the most useful thing they will tell you all call. Listen to the whole answer before responding. It is almost always setup friction.

### Money — the arguments that fund themselves

11. **"Are you absorbing processing fees on rep wholesale orders today?"** — Spendback. **Three-account validated** (Paparazzi quantified, Nancy "big benefit," Brian did the math live). 2.55%+ → 1%.
12. **"Who owns the payments paperwork on your side — Finance, or Ops?"** — Not really a discovery question. It is the ProPay path, asked early enough that it sounds procedural.
13. **"What's your rep technology fee today?"** — If they charge an eSuite fee, the pass-through model is live and proven, not theoretical: ~2,500 reps × $5 incremental ≈ $12.5K/mo to the DSO.

### Timing — the forcing function

14. **"When's your next convention?"** — Added to the standard set after Jordan Essentials. Event calendars compress cycles; no event means you have to manufacture urgency, which is much harder.
15. **"Is there anything driving a timeline — a renewal, a board review, a roadmap decision?"**
16. **"What would need to be true for this to happen this quarter rather than next?"**

### Trap questions — surface the gap without naming the competitor

17. **"Can your reps take a card payment on their phone without buying hardware?"**
18. **"Does your rep's checkout run through a third-party storefront, or is it part of your back office?"**
19. **"When a rep's inventory count is wrong, who fixes it?"**
20. **"Does a rep see her downline numbers on her phone, or does she have to log into a desktop back office?"** — Dashboard widgets and phone access to back-office data were two of the four top-tier rep reactions at Jordan Essentials, and **both work with zero DSO action.**

### The qualifying question, under the current motion

21. **"Has anyone on your side been through payment underwriting before?"**

ProPay approval is the **only** qualifying criterion under Land-and-Expand. Not rep count, not relationship warmth, not vertical. Reps adopt and get real value without it — nothing transacts until it's done. Answer plainly when asked how hard it is: *"A form, financial details, an underwriting review. Days, not weeks. Two of your peers have done it."*

## Part 3 — What Not to Ask, and What Not to Say

| Don't ask | Because |
|---|---|
| *"What keeps you up at night?"* | Every vendor at the event asks it. You get a rehearsed answer. |
| *"Are you happy with your current platform?"* | Invites a yes and ends the thread. Ask #2 instead and let the friction surface itself. |
| Anything about FTC in the first five minutes | See rule 1. Compliance is an answer, never an opener. |
| *"Would you be interested in a demo?"* | Trade for a person instead: *"Can I get fifteen minutes with whoever owns the payments paperwork?"* |

**Words that cost you the room:** "free" (say *included*), "empower," "seamless," "holistic," "game-changing," "best-in-class."

**Two standing cautions:** don't name the $31M client in writing or to press — *"one client's field"* is fine verbally, one-to-one. And confirm Tap-to-Pay ship status before claiming it; if it hasn't shipped, it's *"in release,"* not *"live."*
