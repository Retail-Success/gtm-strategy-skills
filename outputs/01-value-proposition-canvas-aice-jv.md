## PRIMARY CANVAS — Enterprise DSO

### Customer Profile (right side)

**Customer Jobs**
- Run a scalable field operation — recruit, activate, retain, and pay reps accurately at scale.
- **Build and own a differentiated rep + customer experience** — large enterprise DSOs build their own back offices; they want control and customization, not a boxed SaaS.
- Stay compliant (FTC retail-sales documentation, contractor classification, tax/1099, sales tax).
- Control cost and liability across millions of transactions.
- Get real-time visibility and data across the field.

**Pains**
- **Rigid SaaS forces them to build their own back office** — huge cost, long timelines, $50K+ custom-dev tickets, and they still don't get integrated commerce/payments.
- Four-vendor stitching (back office + rep app + payments + cart) — no integration, four bills, four support lines.
- Rep activation/churn leaky bucket (installed-base: 3–19% active).
- Shopify's 3% on a single merchant account + full chargeback liability at scale.
- FTC/tax exposure; rep customer data invisible to corporate.
- No real-time field visibility; coaching is reactive.

**Gains**
- One vendor, one contract, one integrated stack.
- A platform they can **customize infinitely without rebuilding the rails**.
- Modern selling tools for reps built in (POS, tap-to-pay) — a recruitment/retention differentiator.
- Lower cost + liability; customer data as a marketing asset; faster activation, higher retention.

### Value Map (left side)

**Products & Services**
- **Headless back office** — Freedom/Revolution rails (comp engine, genealogy, reporting) exposed via API + the Aice developer platform.
- **All-in-one stack** — commissions + commerce + payments + POS + rep enablement, one relationship.
- **Wayroo POS + tap-to-pay** — modern in-person and remote selling tools.
- **Per-rep ProPay merchant accounts** + Nuvei/ProPay payment rails.
- **Aice rep + creator/community surface** (funnel, content, communications).

**Pain Relievers** (→ which pain)
- Headless customization platform → *rigid-SaaS / build-your-own-back-office* pain. Build on our rails; don't rebuild the plumbing.
- All-in-one, one contract → *four-vendor stitching*.
- POS + tap-to-pay → *rep payment friction / outdated selling tools*.
- Per-rep ProPay merchant accounts → *Shopify 3% + single-merchant liability + FTC contractor classification* (all three satisfied at once).
- Real-time back-office ↔ rep-app sync → *no field visibility* (proactive coaching).
- Customer auto-creation in back office → *invisible customer data*.

**Gain Creators**
- "Build your own experience on our rails" — enterprise control *with* all-in-one completeness (no one else offers both).
- Payments-funded pricing → most complete *and* lowest cost (undercut weapon).
- One stack → materially lower TCO + support overhead.
- Modern POS/tap-to-pay + rep sub-business infrastructure → a recruitment/retention edge in a high-churn industry.

### Fit Score (Enterprise DSO)

| Pain / Gain | Fit |
|---|---|
| Rigid SaaS → wants to build own back office | **Strong** — headless platform is the direct answer |
| Four-vendor stitching | **Strong** — all-in-one, one contract |
| Rep payment friction / outdated tools | **Strong** — POS + tap-to-pay native |
| Cost + liability + FTC at scale | **Strong** — per-rep merchant accounts + payments-funded pricing |
| Field visibility / proactive coaching | **Partial** — depends on rep-app adoption |
| Customer data as marketing asset | **Partial** — depends on rep transaction adoption |
| AI sales enablement depth (vs. Rallyware) | **Gap** — not the JV's lead strength |
| Enterprise customization actually shipping | **Gap/risk** — headless depth is an engineering dependency (Cart V3, dev platform, GA Q4 2026) |

**Strongest fit → positioning anchor:**
> **The all-in-one operating system enterprise DSOs can build on** — headless back office for unlimited customization, with selling tools (POS, tap-to-pay), payments, and commissions built in. One stack you control, not four vendors you stitch.

This is the initial positioning hypothesis to carry into the Phase 6 rebuild.

## SECONDARY CANVAS — Rep + Creator (PLG motion / M7 bridge feeder)

### Rep (independent seller)
- **Jobs:** sell anywhere, get paid fast, run the business in one place. **Pains:** 4–6 disconnected tools, payment friction, admin > selling, ~30-day commission wait. **Gains:** one app, instant pay, more selling time.
- **Pain relievers / gain creators:** one app replaces the stack; POS + tap-to-pay + invoicing; next-day payouts; Dropship (immediate pay, own merchant account).
- **Value prop:** *"One app to sell, get paid, and run your business — so you spend your time selling, not managing software."*

### Creator / Individual Seller (Aice-native — NEW)
- **Jobs:** attract an audience and convert it to income on one surface. **Pains:** can't sell/get-paid in Aice without bolting on Shopify + a processor; waiting on a DSO sponsor; reconciliation across tools. **Gains:** sell on day one, on rails; one surface from attract → transact.
- **Pain relievers / gain creators:** commerce + payouts built onto the Aice creator surface; **invoicing/payment-links monetize any transaction** (no C&C required); no Shopify dependency.
- **Value prop:** *"Sell on day one — on rails. Your audience and your store, finally in one place."*

**Fit Score (secondary):** Strong on tool-consolidation, payment friction, and "sell on day one" (the creator's HoF problem). This canvas is *secondary in priority* but *primary to the flywheel* — it's how PLG users enter and get pulled up-chain (M7) into enterprise DSO deals.

---

## How the two canvases connect (the bridge)

The creator/rep value props solve individual-level pains cheaply (PLG); as rep density builds inside a DSO, the enterprise canvas's pains (#1 stitching, #5 cost/compliance) surface to the corporate buyer — and the *primary* enterprise motion closes them. **One problem map, two value maps, one flywheel.**
