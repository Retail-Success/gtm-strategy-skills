## 1. The Endgame

**Build the category-defining operating system for direct selling — reps and DSOs on one platform — and scale the combined entity to a strategic acquisition / PE exit within 3–5 years (target exit window 2029–2031).**

The thesis: three companies that each owned one slice of the rep's day now own the whole thing under one brand.

- **Aice** — top of funnel (attract, communicate, nurture, community, creator tools, light CRM) + a proven **rep-paid PLG motion** ("20,000 reps in 4 months").
- **Wayroo** — the transaction backbone (POS, payments, next-day payouts, inventory, invoicing, shipping, per-rep ProPay merchant accounts).
- **Freedom / Revolution** — the corporate back office (compensation engine, genealogy, eCommerce, $7B+ in payouts processed, 51 active DSO clients).

No competitor (Exigo, Rallyware, Fluid, Shopify) spans all three layers. That end-to-end completeness — *plus* two working go-to-market motions that feed each other — is the equity story an acquirer buys.

**The metric that defines winning:** a defensible, growing **combined ARR with strong net revenue retention** and a *proven, repeatable bridge between the two motions* (reps acquired via PLG converting into — or pulling in — DSO contracts, and DSO contracts seeding rep PLG density). An acquirer pays a premium for a two-motion flywheel, not for two separate businesses stapled together.

## 2. Why This Matters

**For the entity (the collective endgame):** Each of the three companies hits a ceiling alone. ByDesign/Wayroo has enterprise credibility and transaction depth but a slow 4-month B2B cycle and a thin top-of-funnel. Aice has PLG velocity, brand heat, and 100K users but no commerce, no payouts, no compliance spine, and a Shopify/Pillars dependency. Merged, each one's ceiling becomes the other's floor — and the combined asset is acquirable in a way none of the three is on its own. The deck's framing says it plainly: **a 12-month head start over any PE rollup attempt — already integrated, already in market.** That head start is a depreciating asset; the endgame is to convert it into exit-grade scale before a rollup catches up.

**For the GTM function:** This is founder-level scope at the scale of a three-company merger with an explicit exit thesis. The job is to make the two motions *one flywheel* and document it well enough to survive diligence.

## Key Insight

> **The JV is now staffed to run two motions — so the scarce resource isn't capacity, it's the BRIDGE between them. The acquirer isn't buying a B2B company and a PLG company in the same building; they're buying the flywheel that connects them.**

The flywheel: reps acquired cheaply via Aice PLG raise rep density inside DSOs, which manufactures and accelerates enterprise deals (deck Motion 7 — "up-chain to DSO"); enterprise deals mandate the platform, which seeds the next wave of PLG reps. The deck's nine motions are the spokes — but the M7 up-chain bridge is the hub, and it's the one motion with no prior proof at the JV. Everything in the 90-day plan should be evaluated against one question:

*Does this strengthen the bridge between the rep motion and the DSO motion — or does it just add revenue to one side?*

Two highest-leverage first assets: (1) the **Paparazzi case study** — proof the transaction backbone drives real rep revenue at scale, anchoring both the enterprise pitch and the "why reps pay" PLG pitch; (2) **instrumentation of the M7 up-chain** so the PLG→enterprise conversion can be measured from day one — because that single metric is what an acquirer underwrites.
