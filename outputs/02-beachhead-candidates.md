# Beachhead Segment Candidates — Which Reps First

**Phase:** 2 — Collecting Intelligence, Task 1
**Date:** 2026-08-30 · **Owner:** Sam Atieh
**Builds on:** [`01-swot-analysis.md`](01-swot-analysis.md) · [`03-customer-archetype.md`](03-customer-archetype.md) · `my-gtm-context.md` §§2–3

> ### The beachhead question is different under this motion
>
> The classic question is *"which market segment do we attack first?"* We already know the market: **75,000 Revolution Pro reps across 55 clients.**
>
> **The real question is which slice of that base to point the Revolution placement at first** — because the placement is finite attention, the pilot has exactly three slots, and ProPay approval is the gate on every dollar.
>
> **Reference density is unusually high here.** Direct selling organises people into uplines and downlines with dense internal communication and a financial incentive to talk. **Word of mouth is not a hoped-for side effect — it is the structure of the market.**

# The Top Three

## 🔴 A — Un-adopted Paparazzi reps

**~16,000 reps, ProPay already cleared, inside an account that already runs Wayroo.**

| Why it wins | Detail |
|---|---|
| **No gate** | ProPay is done. **Selling tools work on day one** — the only segment where that is true at scale. |
| **Revenue-ready immediately** | Every adopter can transact from the first session |
| **Proven demand** | The account **pays Hustle $5/monthly-active-rep** across ~20,000 reps. They will pay for rep tooling; ours is free. |
| **Reference density** | One company, one field, dense internal comms |
| **The benchmark lives here** | Paparazzi's ~$7,750 GMV/rep/year is the only real GMV data the business owns |

⚠️ **Wayroo is at ~20% penetration there, not full coverage** — a fact that reads as a stronger per-rep result and a weaker coverage claim. Say the former.

> **This is the single highest-yield segment available and it appears in no current plan.** It should be the first target of the Revolution placement, ahead of the pilot programme.

> ### 📊 The re-engagement path is now specific — Premiere survey, 2026-08-30
>
> The survey ([`02-survey-results.md`](02-survey-results.md)) tells us exactly why warm Paparazzi reps have not adopted, and it is not the product:
>
> | Finding | What it means for this segment |
> |---|---|
> | 🔴 **6 of 7 non-adopters: "getting started seemed too complicated"** | **The entry point is onboarding, not features.** Lead creative on *"nothing to set up"*, not on capability. |
> | **5 of 7 settled on Square** | **Build the switch-from-Square path.** They have a working alternative and need a low-friction reason, not a comparison table. |
> | **Adoption falls with rank** — Director 100% → Consultant 50% | The un-adopted ~16,000 skew toward **lower-rank, lower-volume reps** — the population onboarding friction hurts most |
> | **Satisfaction among users is 4.5/5** | **The product is not the problem.** Anyone who gets in, stays. |
> | 🔴 **Reply to Buy: 3 use it, 24 struggle** | **Keep it out of acquisition creative entirely** |
>
> ⚠️ **These 7 attended a training workshop — the warmest non-adopters available.** The cold ~16,000 are a harder population. **Survey them directly before sizing the re-engagement opportunity** — it is a send, at an account already ProPay-approved.

## B — The Jordan Essentials 510

**Small, but it answers a question nothing else can.**

They have used the product and are about to be asked to pay $5. **Their conversion rate is the only willingness-to-pay signal available before the Wayroo Pro / Aice Pro tier is priced** — and it prices the Wayroo → Revolution Pro attach before it runs at 54 other clients.

⚠️ **Discount the result** — restoring something you had beats buying something new, so it will overstate genuine upgrade appetite.

## C — Team leaders, across all accounts

**The multiplier, and the one segment that works everywhere.**

| Why it wins | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Works at un-onboarded accounts** | Downline dashboards need **nothing** from the DSO |
| **Derivable from data we hold** | Downline size is a query, not a survey |
| **They are the referral network** | One activated leader with 20 downline reps is worth 20 individually-persuaded reps |
| **Observed** | Downline dashboards were a top-tier reaction at the JE convention |

**Already operationalised** in [`10-transacting-adoption-playbook.md`](10-transacting-adoption-playbook.md) §4 — this analysis confirms it as a beachhead, not just a tactic.

# Why the Others Rank Lower

| Segment | Why not first |
|---|---|
| **D — C&C accounts with conventions** | Strong, but convention timing is not ours to control and ProPay status is mixed. **Good pilot slot 1 candidate.** |
| **E — ProPay-approved accounts generally** | Right instinct, too broad to be a beachhead. ⚠️ **Nobody has counted how many of the 55 are approved.** Get that number — it defines the addressable-today base. |
| **F — Catalog-only reps** | 🔴 **Pain score 2 is the problem.** Selling tools do not serve them, and the Aice pairing that would is unsigned. **Pilot slot 2 exists to test this, not to lead with it.** |
| **G — Net-new DSOs** | Deprioritised, capacity-gated, 4–6 month cycle. Year-0 sits inside signed accounts. |
| **H — Exigo installed base** | Strategically the biggest prize and **currently unreachable** — no in-product access, no relationship. A Wayroo-led entry question, not a beachhead. |

---

# Recommendation

> ### Attack in this order: **Paparazzi → team leaders everywhere → the JE 510.**
>
> All three are reachable in-product today, need no sales cycle, and two of them can transact immediately.
>
> **The pilot programme is the right second move, not the first.** It buys branded reference accounts and answers the catalog-only question — both valuable, neither urgent. **Paparazzi needs no pilot, no branding, and no approval.**
