# The Shift

**The business moved to the field, and the money followed it out of your system.**

For thirty years, direct-selling software was built around the home office. The back office ran commissions and genealogy, the website took orders, and the field got a login and a monthly cheque. **The company was the centre, and the field was a channel.**

That's no longer how the business works. Your field sells at a pop-up on Saturday, in a social comment on Sunday, by invoice from a parking lot on Monday and at a convention in a hotel lobby. Your customers shop on their phones and expect a storefront as good as anything else they buy from. **Most of the moments that make you money now happen outside the systems you run.**

So the stack grew to follow them. A storefront on one platform, glued to the back office with an integration someone maintains. A card reader or payment app in every rep's pocket. Content tools, texting tools, training tools. **Each one is good at its job. None of them knows the others exist**, and the money moves between them in pieces.

---

# The Stakes

**What it costs a home office that keeps stitching:**

- **You can't see your own business.** Sales happen on Square, Venmo and cash, and never become a record. Customers your reps sell to are customers you can't reach. A field of thousands, and no full view of it.
- **You pay a tax on every seam.** A storefront licence, an integration to build and maintain, a fee on every transaction, a reconciliation at month-end. None of those bills makes your field sell more.
- **Your reps pay one too.** Six to ten apps, $60–100 a month out of their own pockets, often before they've made a sale. New reps stall and leave, and turnover is the most expensive line in the business.
- **Every change is a project.** A new promotion, a rank rule, a storefront tweak: each one crosses three vendors and waits on the slowest.
- **The next wave makes it worse.** The next generation of tools, AI included, is only as good as the data it can see. **A stack in pieces can't feed it.** The companies with one connected record will get the benefit first. The ones with ten partial records will buy another integration.

**The gap widens every year you wait**, because every new tool you add is one more seam.

---

# The Promised Land

**Every dollar takes one path, from the customer to the commission, and you own it.**

A customer meets a rep at a table and pays on the rep's phone. Next month she reorders from a storefront that looks exactly like your brand and sets up AutoShip. Later she decides to join, and her sponsor is already attached. **At every moment the sale lands with the rep who earned it, the commission is calculated and paid from the same record, and the rep sees it in her app.** Nothing to reconcile, because nothing left the system.

For the home office, that means:

- **Every field sale, online or in person, on your record.**
- **Checkout that stays on your platform**, in your brand, under your control.
- **One app your reps actually want**, because it already holds their orders, their inventory, their numbers and their team, and it takes the payment.
- **Changes in days, not projects**, because there's one system to change.
- **One partner who answers for all of it.**

**The outcome is the one every home office is measured on: reps who earn more, stay longer and recruit.**

# The Contrarian Truth

> ### Everyone in this industry is fighting over features: a better comp engine, a better rep app, a better cart. **That isn't where the war is.**
>
> Good storefronts are everywhere. Good payment apps are free. Good content tools are cheap. **The scarce thing is the connection between them**: owning the whole path a dollar travels, from the customer's payment to the rep's commission.
>
> **Tools that stop at the link can't win this, however good they are.** A rep app that doesn't take the payment can't credit the sale. A storefront that doesn't know what a distributor is can't credit the rep. A payment app that doesn't know the company can't send the record home. **Only a company that runs the back office *and* the field *and* the money path can connect them.**

## The enemy

**Not Shopify, not Square, not any back-office vendor.** The enemy is **the stitched stack**: the belief that best-of-breed tools plus integrations is good enough for direct selling. It's the reason home offices pay three bills for one sale, the reason reps carry ten apps, and the reason no one can see the whole business.

> **🆕 Refined after TLC (2026-11-05): the sharpest form of the enemy is the stitch nobody owns.** TLC chose Shopify, likes it, and credits it with a conversion lift. What they hate is the middleware they built themselves, which broke when their back-office vendor changed its API. **So with a home office that has chosen its storefront, don't attack the storefront. Offer to own the stitch:** *"keep the storefront you chose; the connection to the back office becomes ours to maintain."* The full one-stack story is where they can go next, not the price of entry.

## Why it's inevitable

1. **The phone is the point of sale.** Reps no longer need hardware to take payment. They need a system that knows the company.
2. **The integration tax is now visible.** Storefront licences, connectors, per-transaction surcharges and rep app subscriptions have stacked into numbers that CFOs and reps both notice.
3. **Selling went social and in-person at the same time.** No single point tool covers both. Only a connected stack follows the rep everywhere she sells.
4. **AI rewards whoever owns the connected data.** The tools that help a rep know who to follow up with and what to share need her orders, customers and team in one place.
5. **Consolidation is already happening.** The industry is moving from "ten vendors" to "one platform." The only question is whose.

# Where We Fit: Back Office, Field and Coaching on One Stack

**ByDesign** runs the back office: compensation and genealogy at scale, the commerce engine, and now a new storefront with a Branding Studio the home office controls itself.

**Wayroo** extends the back office to the field: the rep's orders, inventory, numbers and team on her phone, with selling tools that send every sale home.

**Aice** brings the coaching, content and community side of the field. **Together, Wayroo and Aice are building one app for the whole selling day**: it tells a rep what to share and who to reach, takes the payment, and pays her, with the back office underneath.

**What nobody else in the space owns: the whole money path.** The Product Team's 2026 competitive landscape found that none of the rep-enablement players owns payments, payouts and commissions together. They stop at the link.

# Guardrails

**External ambition vs. internal ambition.** Internally, the ambition is to make this stack the standard way direct selling runs. **Externally, don't claim dominance or leadership.** The Product Team's strategy says plainly that we are not the leader in these arenas yet. Let the prospect conclude we're the future; never tell them.

| ✅ Safe | 🚫 Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "One stack for the whole money path" · "nobody else owns the whole path" | "We're the processor" · "we own the rails" | We partner for processing (Nuvei for DSO checkout, Adyen for reps) |
| "The stitched stack" as the enemy | Naming or criticising a competitor, partner or connector | The enemy is a paradigm. Shop IQ is a partner, Exigo has a rep app, and so on |
| "We've teamed up with Aice" | Merger, acquisition, ownership, brand changes, combined-app dates | Equity, brand and architecture are undecided |
| "AI is only as good as the data it can see" | "Our AI does X" | We have no rep AI today. AI coaching comes from the Aice side, combined app planned |
| "Reps who earn more" as the outcome | A percentage uplift | No measured before/after exists yet. Paparazzi ~$7,750 GMV per transacting rep is the benchmark, internal only |
| The rep's $60–100 a month on 6–10 apps | "Cheaper than Square" | Price is roughly level with Square |
| — | FTC or compliance as the lead | Message-tested failure. A late supporting point for Finance at most |

# Content Angles (6–12 months)

1. **"Follow one dollar."** Trace a single sale through a stitched stack vs. one stack. (Blog, keynote, carousel)
2. **"The three bills for one sale."** Storefront licence, integration, transaction fee: what direct selling pays to look like Shopify. (CFO-targeted)
3. **"The war isn't features."** The contrarian truth, as an opinion piece or a podcast topic.
4. **"Your field's invisible sales."** What happens to the Venmo and cash sale, and what it costs the company. (VP Sales)
5. **"AI needs one record."** Why the next wave of field tools rewards connected data. (Board, press)
6. **"Your brand at the table, in the shop and at signup."** The marketing persona's angle.
7. **"Why we teamed up with Aice."** Coaching and commerce on one stack. (Announcement follow-ups)
8. **"One customer, three moments."** Meet, shop, join, credited to the same rep. (Demo video)
9. **"The rep who sells before she pays."** Rep economics and app fatigue, from the home office's point of view. (Retention)
10. 🆕 **"Who owns the stitch?"** The integration you built yourself is a liability the day your vendor changes its API. (CTO. From TLC, 2026-11-05)
11. 🆕 **"Your sales numbers are yours."** What your back-office vendor can see, and what it should never share. (CEO. ⚠️ Generic only: never cite TLC's allegation about Exigo, and publish only once our own contract terms are confirmed)
12. 🆕 **"Not just the engine."** *"We make sure the engine runs, but anything else on the car you have to figure out"*: why home offices now want a back-office vendor that's product-led. (From the TLC call notes, paraphrased)
