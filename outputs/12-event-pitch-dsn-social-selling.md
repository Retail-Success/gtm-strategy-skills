> ### 🔴 Read these four before you walk in
>
> 1. **DSN is a publication. Assume everything is on the record.** No Aice. No acquisition. No client names that aren't already public. No revenue numbers.
> 2. **Never say "free." Say "included."** *Free* invites "then what's the catch." *Included* explains itself.
> 3. **Never say we're cheaper than Square.** Our rate is roughly level. Concede it — that concession is what makes the integration argument land.
> 4. ⚠️ **Confirm Tap-to-Pay ship status before you claim it.** It was due ~mid-September. If it hasn't shipped, say *"in release"* — not *"live."*

# PAGE 1 — THE PITCH

## The hook (use the room's own theme)

> **"The social selling problem nobody's solved is that the sale happens in a DM and the company never sees it."**
>
> *"Your rep closes in a comment thread or a live. Money moves on Venmo or Cash App. No receipt, no customer record, no tracking number — and corporate finds out at the end of the month, if at all. Every one of those is a customer your marketing team will never reach again."*

**Then stop talking and let them agree.** Every DSO leader in that room knows this is true and most have never heard a vendor say it out loud. That's your opening.

---

## The 30-second pitch — two branches

**Ask first: "Who's your back office?"** The answer routes the whole conversation.

### Branch A — They're on ByDesign *(55 clients, ~75,000 Revolution Pro reps)*

> *"Then your reps can already have this. Wayroo is the selling app that comes with your back office — it's included with the Revolution Pro they already pay for. Her wholesale order and inventory are already in it the first time she opens it, nothing to set up. She sells at an event or through a payment link, and the sale lands back in your back office as a record you can see.*
>
> *It costs you nothing. There's exactly one thing that needs you, and it's paperwork — I'll tell you about it if you want."*

### Branch B — They're on Exigo, or anything else *(most of the room)*

> *"That's fine — Wayroo runs on top of whatever back office you have. It's not a replacement, it's the rep-facing layer your back office doesn't have. Your rep gets her inventory, her downline, her numbers and your announcements on her phone, plus card payments through her own merchant account. Every sale flows back as a record.*
>
> *You don't rip anything out. That's the whole point."*

> ⚠️ **Branch B is the Wayroo-led entry test, not settled company positioning.** Pitch it freely, take the meeting — but do **not** commit to standalone Wayroo contract terms in the room. "Let's scope it" is the right close.

---

## The three things that actually land — in this order

| # | Say it like this | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **"Her inventory is already in the app the first time she opens it."** | This is the whole position. Every other claim depends on it. It's also checkable in one screen. |
| **2** | **"You'll see rep sales activity you've never had visibility into."** | Field visibility, not "corporate oversight." Nobody objects to visibility framed as knowing your own business. |
| **3** | **"She gets paid the next day, onto a card she can spend or use to buy her next wholesale order."** | The loop back into the supplier is the part no competitor can build. Lead with the payout, not the card acceptance. |

**Two more, only if they lean in:** your push notifications reach her where she already works; your media library means she posts your photos, not hers.

---

## Proof — three numbers, use one

| Number | Say it as |
|---|---|
| **$31M** | *"One client's field did $31 million through it last year — and that came from about a fifth of their reps."* |
| **764 reps / 48 hours** | *"At a client's convention this summer, 764 reps downloaded it inside two days. Nobody sold them."* |
| **25 years / $7B+** | *"We've been doing direct selling back office for 25 years and processed over $7 billion in commissions. We also built the LuLaRoe app — a lot of their top reps already know the workflow."* |

⚠️ **Don't name the $31M client in writing or to press** — that case study is still unpublished. Verbally, in a one-to-one conversation, "a client" is fine.

# PAGE 2 — WORKING THE ROOM

## Five questions that do the qualifying for you

Ask these instead of pitching. Each one tells you something and makes them talk.

| Ask | What the answer tells you |
|---|---|
| **1. "Who's your back office?"** | Branch A or B. Ask this first, always. |
| **2. "Roughly what share of your field carries their own stock versus selling from the catalog?"** | Sizes the commerce pitch. ⚠️ **Treat the answer as a guess** — every DSO we've been able to check was wrong, in both directions. Offering to *measure* it for them is a strong follow-up hook. |
| **3. "What are your reps actually paying for, out of pocket, to run their business?"** | Most don't know. The real number is **$60–100/month across 6–10 apps.** Telling them is the single most interesting thing you'll say to them all night. |
| **4. "When a rep sells at a vendor event or in a Facebook group — how does that customer reach you?"** | It doesn't. This is the pain, in their own words, which is much better than in yours. |
| **5. "Have you tried a rep app before?"** | If yes: **"What happened?"** Then shut up and listen. It's almost always onboarding — which is the failure we designed against. |

## Objections — acknowledge, explore, answer

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"Our reps already use Square."** | *"Most do, and Square's good — our processing rate is about the same, I'm not going to pretend we win on price."* **Then:** *"What Square will never have is your back office. It doesn't know what she ordered from you, it can't fill her inventory, and nothing she sells on it reaches your system. That's not a feature they haven't built — they have no relationship with you to build it on."* |
| **"Why would you include it at no cost?"** | *"We're not giving it away — it's included with a subscription your reps already pay for. We make money when they sell through it. Our incentive and yours are the same number."* |
| **"We already have a rep app / we use Hustle."** | *"Then you've already made the case internally, which is the hard part."* **Do not claim parity — we have no teardown of theirs.** *"The question I'd ask is whether it's connected to your back office. If her inventory and her downline aren't in it on day one, she's still doing the setup work."* |
| **"We tried one and adoption was terrible."** | *"What happened?"* → *"That's the failure we designed against. Hers is already loaded when she opens it, and there's nothing to buy, so nobody has to be sold on it first."* |
| **"Can we brand it?"** | *"Standard version ships unbranded so we can get it to everyone at once. Branding is a real build — about $25,000. We're doing it at no charge for three founding partners, and that's bounded at three."* **Say the number. Bound it.** |
| **"What's the lift on our side?"** | *"Almost none. The one thing that needs you is merchant-account approval with our processor so your reps can take a card and get paid. It's underwriting paperwork — days, not weeks. Not an IT project."* |
| **"Our CTO will have questions."** | **Good — get the name.** *"He should. Short version: we consume from your system of record, we don't become a second one."* |
| **"Send me something."** | The deferral. *"Happy to — who owns payments on your side? That's the only piece that needs anyone, and it moves faster with them in the room."* **Trade the document for the introduction.** |

## 🚫 What not to say at this event

| Don't | Because |
|---|---|
| **"Free"** | Invites *"then why am I paying for anything?"* It's **included**. |
| **"We're cheaper than Square"** | Rate is roughly level. The claim invites arithmetic we don't win. |
| **Anything about Aice, acquisitions, or partnerships** | Unsigned. Press in the room. |
| **A date for anything** | The launch calendar is being reset. "Shipping" not "shipping in October." |
| **A guess at their rep count or Pro penetration** | They know the real number. Being wrong costs the room. |
| **"This makes you stickier with us"** | True, ours, and it turns a gift into a trap. |
| **"Replaces all their apps"** | Canva and SMS survive. A large dent, not a clean sweep. |
| **ProPay as your opener** | Turns good news into a compliance project before they know why it matters. |

## The close — one line, three versions

| Situation | Close |
|---|---|
| **ByDesign client** | *"Let me pull your numbers before we talk — how many of your reps are on Pro, and how many are already on Wayroo. I'd rather show you that than pitch you."* |
| **Non-client, interested** | *"Twenty minutes, and I'll show you it running on a back office that isn't ours."* |
| **Anyone, lukewarm** | *"Can I send you the number your reps are actually spending per month on tools? It surprises most people."* **This one converts the coldest contacts.** |

## Capture — do this at the table, not in the car

For every real conversation, get: **name · company · back office · their stated C&C share · the objection they actually raised · who owns payments.**

**Within 24 hours:** one email each, referencing the specific thing *they* said — not a generic follow-up. Log the back office and the C&C share; those two fields are how this list becomes a target list.

> ### The one-sentence version, if you only remember one thing
>
> **"It's the selling app that comes with your back office — her inventory's already in it, and every sale she makes comes back to you as a record."**
