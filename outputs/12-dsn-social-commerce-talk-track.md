## The 30-second open

> "Every company here can tell me what their field *posts*. Almost none can tell me what their field *sold* — because it settled in Venmo. That's the gap we work on."

Then **stop and ask a question.** Do not describe the product until they've told you which version of the problem they have. On a networking floor the person who asks second loses.

---

## Three questions, in order

| # | Ask | What the answer gives you |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"When a rep sells face-to-face or in a DM, does that customer ever show up in your system?"** | The opener. The answer is almost always *no*, and they don't enjoy saying it out loud. Let the pause sit. |
| 2 | **"What share of your reps carry their own inventory and sell it themselves?"** | Sizes the fit. **Treat the number as a hypothesis** — DSO self-reported inventory share has been wrong in both directions at every account where we could check it. More reps sell in person than their companies think. |
| 3 | **"Who's your back office on?"** | Routes everything. See the two rooms below. |

## You are standing in two different rooms

| | **They run ByDesign** (Freedom / Revolution) | **They run Exigo, or anything else** |
|---|---|---|
| **This is** | Not a sale | A platform conversation, at normal length |
| **Lead with** | *"Your reps can have this now"* — it's **included** with the Revolution Pro subscription they already hold | The customer-capture gap first, platform second |
| **Then** | Field visibility they've never had — plus a channel to reach the field in-app | Wayroo integrates with other back offices |
| **The one step** | Enabling payments, which is what switches the selling tools on | A scoping call |
| **Don't** | Quote a price. There isn't one to quote. | Promise integration timelines |

> **Best line for a non-client:** *"An existing platform customer's reps get this at no extra charge. You'd get exactly the same the day you're on the platform."*
> That turns "why is it free for them" into a reason to buy the platform, instead of a discount you have to defend.

## The three things worth saying to a DSO executive

Pick one. You will rarely get to a second in a networking conversation.

**1. The customer capture.** When a rep creates a customer in Wayroo, the record **auto-creates in your back office** — not synced on a schedule, created. A field-level customer base that has been invisible for years becomes an audience you can market to directly. *Executives at two separate DSOs called this a "huge benefit" unprompted, before we pitched it.*

**2. You finally have a way to reach your field.** Corporate push notifications land **in the app the rep opens to run her business** — not in an inbox she doesn't read or a Facebook group she's muted. Plus a media library, so she posts your assets instead of her own photos with the product names wrong. *This is the argument we have historically under-pitched, and DSO leadership rates it far higher than reps do.*

**3. Nothing to set up, so reps actually activate.** The app opens with her wholesale order and inventory already in it, and her sales, downline and KPIs on the phone. **Zero configuration on your side.** Rep activation normally dies at setup — this skips the step.

## Proof you can say out loud

> **Paparazzi** — 4,000 reps selling through Wayroo generated **$31M in 2025**, up 60%+ year over year.
>
> **Jordan Essentials** — **764 reps adopted within days** of their July convention, about 29% of their active field. Deal closed in roughly 11 weeks. Afterward the founder asked to move her paid start date *earlier.*

**The convention detail is the one that travels.** It leads naturally to the best qualifier on the floor:

> ### "When's your next convention?"
>
> Ask everyone. A dated company event is the strongest deal accelerant observed in this business — Jordan Essentials hit 29% of its field in two to three days because the launch happened on a convention stage. A date on the calendar turns a quarterly-pace pipeline into an event-pace one, and the floor is the cheapest place to collect them.

## Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"We already give our reps an app."** | "Does it know what they ordered from you? Wayroo opens with their wholesale order and inventory already in it, because it's reading your back office. That's the part a bolt-on can't do." |
| **"Square does this and it's free."** | "Square's genuinely good at point of sale. It also knows nothing about your company — no wholesale order, no inventory from corporate, and the customer never reaches your system. The difference isn't features, it's the connection." |
| **"Another app is the last thing our field needs."** | "Agreed, which is why the argument is subtraction. Your reps are paying $60–100 a month out of their own pockets across six to ten tools. This replaces a stack rather than joining it." |
| **"What does this cost us?"** | *(Client)* "Nothing incremental — it's included with the Revolution Pro subscription your reps already hold." *(Non-client)* "It's part of the platform. Let's scope it properly rather than me guessing a number at a cocktail hour." |
| **"So can our reps take card payments today?"** | **Straight answer: "Not yet — that's the next thing shipping."** Then: "What's live today is everything that doesn't touch money: inventory, dashboards, your assets, your notifications. The payment side switches on once a company completes payment setup." **Do not hedge this one.** The room is small and the truth gets back to you. |
| **"Is this a compliance product?"** | "It's a selling product that happens to produce the record. Reps adopt it because their inventory's already in it and they can see their numbers on their phone — the audit trail is a byproduct. Nobody adopts software to be compliant." |

## 🔴 Do not say

| 🚫 Never | ✅ Instead |
|---|---|
| **Any Tap-to-Pay availability or ship date** | It has not launched anywhere, JE included. "Next thing shipping" is as specific as you go. |
| **"Free"** | **"Included."** *Free* invites "what's the catch" and reads as a trial. |
| **"764 and still growing"** | **No post-launch adoption data exists.** "764 adopted within days" — full stop. |
| **"Wayroo reversed their sales decline"** | No pre-launch baseline was ever captured. Unsupported. |
| Anything implying **Paparazzi's whole field** is on Wayroo | 4,000 adopters sit inside a ~20,000-rep field. *"4,000 reps generated $31M"* is true and stronger per rep. |
| Feature comparisons vs. **Hustle** or **Boards** | Neither has a feature-level teardown. You may say Hustle charges $5 per monthly-active rep. You may not compare feature sets. |
| Anything about **Aice** | The acquisition is unsigned. Don't mention the partnership; don't run anti-Aice positioning either. |
| **A per-rep price** | $3, $5 and $1 all circulate internally and do not reconcile. Quote nothing. |
| "Empowering" · "revolutionary" · "game-changing" | House voice is outcome-first with specific numbers. |

> **On naming clients out loud.** Using Paparazzi and Jordan Essentials in a 1:1 conversation is consistent with how both already appear in the sales deck and one-pager. **What has never been approved is publication** — so keep them out of anything on a stage, a slide, a handout, or social. If you end up on a panel, switch to *"a client with 4,000 reps on the app."*

If a specific executive is worth a real asset afterward, the master [`12-one-pager.md`](12-one-pager.md) is the post-conversation leave-behind — **but its pricing line is stale and must be fixed before you send it to anyone.**
